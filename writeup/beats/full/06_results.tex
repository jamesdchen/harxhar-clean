\section{Results}
\label{sec:rh_results}
\label{sec:rh_exp_setup}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Design, benchmark and outline.} Every forecast follows the design, spans and tests of
  Sections~\ref{sec:rh_methods_setup} and~\ref{sec:rh_results_eval}: every bar of $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{oos}}$ is
  scored, and the primary benchmark is the OLS on the HAR benchmark set, the OLS row of Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}, which
  the HAR-set penalized models reproduce. The window length was fixed after out-of-sample scoring
  (Section~\ref{sec:rh_results_robust}). We report accuracy on every bar (Section~\ref{sec:rh_results_oos}), the
  predictor groups that carry the gain (Section~\ref{sec:rh_results_ablation}) and robustness (Section~\ref{sec:rh_results_robust});
  Section~\ref{sec:rh_fi} asks which series the models use.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Accuracy on every bar}
\label{sec:rh_results_oos}
\label{sec:rh_results_predictors}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Overview.} Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos} reports the MSE, the average QLIKE loss and $R^2_{\mathrm{OOS}}$ of
  the six model classes on the HAR benchmark set and on the full predictor set.
\item \textbf{Against the primary benchmark.} The full predictor set lowers the average QLIKE loss of every model
  class below that of the OLS on the HAR benchmark set.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Penalized linear models, whose HAR-set forecast is the benchmark: \rhorVsOlsPctRidgeFull\% (ridge regression),
    \rhorVsOlsPctLassoFull\% (lasso) and \rhorVsOlsPctEnetFull\% (elastic net), with $\DM={}$\rhorDmRidgeB,
    \rhorDmLassoB{} and \rhorDmEnetB.
  \item Tree ensembles: \rhorVsOlsRedTreeRng{}\%, with $\DM={}$\rhorVsOlsDmLgbmFull,
    \rhorVsOlsDmXgbFull{} and \rhorVsOlsDmRfFull{} for LightGBM, XGBoost and the random forest.
  \item The reduction is significant for \rhorVsOlsLowerSig{} of the \rhorAfPairs{} full-set forecasts.
  \item On the HAR benchmark set alone the tree ensembles stay within \rhorVsOlsPctTreeHarMaxAbs{}\% of the
    benchmark, so the trees' gain comes from the added predictors rather than from a nonlinear use of the HAR terms.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Within each class.} The paired comparison of $f_{m,\mathrm{Full},\mathcal I_{\mathrm{rh}}}$ with
  $f_{m,\mathrm{HAR},\mathcal I_{\mathrm{rh}}}$ (Section~\ref{sec:rh_results_eval}; the DM column of
  Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}) isolates what the exogenous predictors add to a given model $m$.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item The full set has the lower loss in \rhorAfLower{} of the \rhorAfPairs{} classes, \rhorAfLowerSig{} of them
    significantly.
  \item The tree ensembles gain more from the same predictors than the linear models: \rhabLgbmFullAllPct\% for
    LightGBM, \rhabXgbFullAllPct\% for XGBoost and \rhabRfFullAllPct\% for the random forest ($\DM={}$\rhorDmLgbmB,
    \rhorDmXgbB{} and \rhorDmRfB). % RECHECK (D13): "gain more ... than the linear models"
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Squared-error loss.} Under squared error the comparison reverses: on every bar the full set raises the
  MSE in all \rhorAfPairs{} classes, by \rhcaLinMsePctBarSpanMin{} to \rhcaLinMsePctBarSpanMax\% for the penalized
  linear models and by \rhcaTreeMsePctBarSpanMin{} to \rhcaTreeMsePctBarSpanMax\% for the tree ensembles, and lowers
  $R^2_{\mathrm{OOS}}$ (Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}). A handful of days in the COVID-19 crash account for the difference
  (Appendix~\ref{app:rh_results_calib}).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item On \rhcaYearsPre{} the full set has the lower every-bar MSE in all classes (\rhcaLinMsePctBarPreMin{} to
    \rhcaLinMsePctBarPreMax\% for the linear models).
  \item On \rhcaYearsOos{} the \rhcaTopDays{} days with the largest excess squared error of the full set, all between
    \rhcaTopFirstBarOos{} and \rhcaTopLastBarOos, carry \rhcaLinTopShareBarOosRng\% of that excess for the linear
    models and \rhcaTreeTopShareBarOosRng\% for the trees; without the days from \rhcaCrashStart{} to \rhcaCrashEnd,
    the full set has the lower every-bar MSE on \rhcaYearsOos{} in all classes, each significantly.
  \item The excess is not a level bias (the bias term is at most \rhcaAllShBiasBarOosMax\% of any forecast's MSE) but
    one of scale: on \rhcaYearsOos{} the full-set forecasts move too much in levels.
  \item QLIKE scores an error relative to the forecast and grows only logarithmically in over-prediction, so the crash
    bars weigh little in it, and in logs the full-set forecasts are well scaled. QLIKE, which is robust to noise in
    the realized-variance proxy \citep{Patton2011}, remains the primary loss.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Which predictors carry the gain?}
\label{sec:rh_results_ablation}
\ifdefined\rhbyLXgbReading\else\input{generated/rehaul_beyond_ladder_numbers}\fi
\ifdefined\rhbyFXgbReading\else\input{generated/rehaul_beyond_factor_numbers}\fi
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Two groups carry the gain.} The FOMC calendar and the futures' realized moments, added to the HAR
  benchmark set, deliver at least \rhabBaseShareMin\% of every class's every-bar gain from the full set. What remains is
  small, only the tree ensembles capture it, and a few series carry it. How much a model uses a series is a poor guide
  to these gains (Section~\ref{sec:rh_fi}).
\item \textbf{Design} (Table~\ref{tab:rh_ablation}; Appendix~\ref{app:rh_results_ablation}). The design of
  Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}, including the full set's frozen hyperparameters, with only the columns changed:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Panel~A removes one of the \rhfiNBuckets{} predictor groups from the full set; Panel~B adds one to the HAR
    benchmark set.
  \item Panel~C fits by OLS the semivariance HAR of \citet{PattonSheppard2015}, the HARQ model of
    \citet{BollerslevPattonQuaedvlieg2016} and the leverage HAR of \citet{CorsiReno2012}, built from the same
    one-minute E-mini returns as the target.
  \item Panel~D sets the full set against the HAR benchmark set, alone and with the realized moments, the FOMC
    calendar or both added (HAR\,+\,RM\,+\,FOMC); Panel~E scores the late-starting groups on the bars on which their
    values are live.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Not an augmented HAR.} The futures' realized moments, computed from the same one-minute returns that
  define the target, are the natural augmented HAR. Added to the HAR benchmark set they lower the loss in every class
  (Panel~B; \rhabOlsAddRmAllPct\% for the OLS), by about as much as the three classical extensions estimated together
  (\rhabOlsSharqlAllPct\%; Panel~C). Yet the full set is significantly more accurate than the HAR benchmark set plus
  the realized moments in \rhabNClassesBeatAddRm{} of the \rhabNClassesAddRm{} classes (Panel~D).
\item \textbf{The FOMC calendar and the realized moments} (Panels~A, B and~D).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Together, HAR\,+\,RM\,+\,FOMC delivers \rhabBaseShareTreeMin--\rhabBaseShareTreeMax\% of the full set's
    every-bar gain in the tree ensembles and at least \rhabBaseShareLinMin\% in the penalized linear models.
  \item Removing the FOMC calendar from the full set raises the loss by \rhabLoFomcAllPctMin--\rhabLoFomcAllPctMax\%,
    removing the realized moments by \rhabLoRmAllPctMin--\rhabLoRmAllPctMax\% ($\DM={}$\rhabLoRmAllDmMin{} or larger);
    removing any other group changes it by at most \rhabLoOtherAllMaxAbs\%.
  \item Added on its own, the FOMC calendar delivers \rhabTreeFomcGainShareMin--\rhabTreeFomcGainShareMax\% of the
    trees' gain.
  \item The constituent-market and liquidity groups lower the trees' loss when added alone (\rhabXgbAddVwAllPct\% for
    the value-weighted market and XGBoost) but little when removed from the full set: their information overlaps with
    that of the realized moments.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{What remains} (Panel~D, last row; Appendix~\ref{app:rh_results_beyond}).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Against HAR\,+\,RM\,+\,FOMC the full set changes the every-bar loss of the ridge, lasso and elastic net by
    \rhabRidgeFullVsAddRmFomcAllPct, \rhabLassoFullVsAddRmFomcAllPct{} and \rhabEnetFullVsAddRmFomcAllPct\%, none
    significantly ($p \geq{}$\rhabLinFullVsAddRmFomcAllPMin), and that of LightGBM and XGBoost by
    \rhabLgbmFullVsAddRmFomcAllPct{} and \rhabXgbFullVsAddRmFomcAllPct\% ($\DM={}$\rhabLgbmFullVsAddRmFomcAllDm{} and
    \rhabXgbFullVsAddRmFomcAllDm); the random forest's \rhabRfFullVsAddRmFomcAllPct\% is not significant ($p$-value
    \rhabRfFullVsAddRmFomcAllP).
  \item A few series carry the trees' remainder. In follow-up tests outside the pre-specified design, scored on
    ordinary days (the trading days without a scheduled FOMC statement), LightGBM and XGBoost refitted on
    HAR\,+\,RM\,+\,FOMC plus their own top five series recover shares of \rhbyLLgbmKFiveOrdR{} and
    \rhbyLXgbKFiveOrdR{} of the remaining gain; ten principal components of the six remaining groups recover
    \rhbyFLgbmPcaTenOrdR{} and \rhbyFXgbPcaTenOrdR. The gain sits in specific series, not in a factor common to them.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Alternative data on their live windows} (Panel~E). The implied-volatility, StockTwits and
  volatility-demand series start after the data and enter as zero until the rolling window holds \rhorWindowDays{}
  trading days of their history (Section~\ref{sec:rh_predictors}), so over most of the span Panels~A and~B measure
  them through their availability shares. On the bars on which their values are live the gains remain small:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Adding the VIX changes the loss of XGBoost by \rhabXgbAddIvLiveIvPct\% from \rhabLiveIvStart{}
    ($\DM={}$\rhabXgbAddIvLiveIvDm).
  \item The StockTwits series change no class's loss significantly from \rhabLiveSentStart{} (the largest change is
    the elastic net's, \rhabEnetAddSentLiveSentPct\%).
  \item The volatility-demand series raise the loss of the penalized linear models between \rhabLiveVdStart{} and
    \rhabLiveVdEnd{} (\rhabRidgeAddVdLiveVdPct\% for ridge regression, $\DM={}$\rhabRidgeAddVdLiveVdDm).
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Robustness}
\label{sec:rh_results_robust}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Estimation window.} The window length $W$ of \rhorWindowDays{} trading days was chosen after the
  \rhwsNWinWord{} candidate windows from \rhwsWinMin{} to \rhrbWinLong{} trading days had been
  scored out of sample on $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{oos}}$; it is not a pre-sample choice. Appendix~\ref{app:rh_robust_window}
  reports every window.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Every bar: the comparison does not depend on the window. Across the candidate windows the every-bar gain of the
    full-set elastic net over its HAR-set forecast lies between \rhwsEnetBarPctMin{}\% and
    \rhwsEnetBarPctMax{}\%, significant at each, and with a window of \rhrbWinLong{} days the full set
    remains significantly more accurate on every bar in \rhrbNFullLowerSigBarLong{} of the \rhrbNModelsLong{} classes
    (Table~\ref{tab:rh_robust_w3500}).
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Multiple comparisons.} Adjusting for the number of comparisons leaves the every-bar result intact: under
  QLIKE the 90\% model confidence set \citep{HansenLundeNason2011} holds \rhmcsBarQlikeTmaxNNinety{} of the
  \rhmcsNModels{} distinct pooled forecasts (\rhmcsBarQlikeTmaxSetNinety), and StepM \citep{RomanoWolf2005} rejects
  equal accuracy of the full and HAR benchmark sets in \rhmcsBarQAfNRej{} of the \rhmcsNAlt{} classes (SPA
  $p$-value \rhmcsBarQAfSpaC; \citealp{Hansen2005}), against \rhmcsBarMAfNRejWord{} under squared error
  (Tables~\ref{tab:rh_mcs} and~\ref{tab:rh_mcs_spa}).
\end{itemize}
