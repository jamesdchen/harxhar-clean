\subsection{The straddle strategy}
\label{sec:rh_econ_strategy}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Position:} SPXW options that expire the same day (0DTE). With the index at $S_t$ at the 15:30 ET forecast
  origin, the call and the put at the nearest strikes on either side,
  \[
  K_C = \min\{K \ge S_t\}, \qquad K_P = \max\{K \le S_t\},
  \]
  over strikes with live quotes. If $K_C = K_P = S_t$ the package is an at-the-money straddle; otherwise it is a
  strangle on the two strikes adjacent to $S_t$. We call both the straddle: a position that is approximately
  delta-neutral at entry, entered at 15:30 ET and held, not hedged, to expiration.

The variance forecast is ES futures realized variance over 15:30--16:00, so the strike can be on the cash index while $\RV_t$ is on the futures. ``Live quotes'' is undefined. A comment under this item drops a day when $\max(K_C-S_t,S_t-K_P)>10$ or fewer than 10 contracts are quoted; that filter is not in the live text. % Code: notebooks/atm_straddle_lib.py:1591-1661. A day is dropped if max(K_C - S, S - K_P) > 10 or fewer than 10
% contracts are live at the stamp.
\item \textbf{Entry prices:} at the mid-quote, $P_t$, and at the quoted bid and ask of the package, $P^{b}_t$ and
  $P^{a}_t$, from the legs' bid quotes $b$ and ask quotes $a$ at 15:30 ET:
  \[
  P_t = \tfrac12\bigl(b^C_t + a^C_t\bigr) + \tfrac12\bigl(b^P_t + a^P_t\bigr),
  \qquad
  P^{b}_t = b^C_t + b^P_t, \qquad P^{a}_t = a^C_t + a^P_t .
  \]
\item \textbf{Settlement} in cash at the official close $S^{\mathrm{close}}_t$, with no delta hedging; $R_t$ is the
  straddle's simple return on the premium paid at the mid-quote:
  \[
  X_t = \bigl(S^{\mathrm{close}}_t - K_C\bigr)^+ + \bigl(K_P - S^{\mathrm{close}}_t\bigr)^+,
  \qquad
  R_t = \frac{X_t}{P_t} - 1 \;\ge\; -1 .
  \]
% Code: atm_straddle_lib.py:1725-1741 (settle_package); R = exit / entry - 1 (notebooks/_write_0dte_nb.py:452).
\item \textbf{Implied variance:} $\mathrm{IV}^C_t$ and $\mathrm{IV}^P_t$ are the legs' implied volatilities at
  15:30 ET, quoted per hour; the implied volatility of the closing half hour is
  \[
  \mathrm{IV}_t = \frac{\mathrm{IV}^C_t + \mathrm{IV}^P_t}{2\sqrt2},
  \]
  and $\mathrm{IV}_t^2$ is the implied variance to expiration.
% Code: atm_straddle_lib.py:1450-1464 (mean of the two legs, / sqrt 2; NaN if either leg is a censored solver node);
% methods_close_option.tex:540-546.
\item \textbf{Forecast variance risk premium:} the variance risk premium of the closing half hour is
  $\mathrm{IV}_t^2 - \E_t[\RV_t]$ \citep{CarrWu2009, BollerslevTauchenZhou2009}, and its forecast is
  $\mathrm{IV}_t^2 - \hat\sigma^2_t$, with $\hat\sigma^2_t$ the variance forecast of
  Section~\ref{sec:rh_methods_setup}. Every strategy below
  conditions on the forecast only through the gap $\hat\sigma^2_t - \mathrm{IV}_t^2$: it buys when the forecast exceeds
  implied variance.

\item \textbf{Strategies:} position $q_t$ in straddles ($q_t > 0$ long, $q_t < 0$ short) and daily return
  $r_t = q_t R_t$. The forecast enters only through $q_t$, so the comparison across strategies measures the economic
  value of the forecast \citep{FlemingKirbyOstdiek2001}:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item the unconditional short-straddle benchmark, a static short-variance position: $q_t \equiv -1$;
  \item sign strategy: $q_t = \sign\bigl(\hat\sigma^2_t - \mathrm{IV}_t^2\bigr)$, with $q_t = -1$ on ties;
  % 2026-10-15 (consolidation): dummy day index u -> t' in the three rules below (u is an exogenous series).
  % 2026-10-15 (finish): parked -- the sizing scale m_t clashes with the model index m; renamed MAD_t (median
  % absolute gap). Was: $q_t = \clip((\hat\sigma^2_t - \mathrm{IV}_t^2)/m_t, -1, 1)$,
  % $m_t = \operatorname{median}_{t'<t}|\hat\sigma^2_{t'} - \mathrm{IV}_{t'}^2|$; full long, scaled short:
  % $q_t = \max((\hat\sigma^2_t - \mathrm{IV}_t^2)/m_t, -1)$.
  \item robust clip: $q_t = \clip\bigl((\hat\sigma^2_t - \mathrm{IV}_t^2)/\mathrm{MAD}_t, -1, 1\bigr)$, with the
    scale $\mathrm{MAD}_t = \operatorname{median}_{t'<t}\bigl|\hat\sigma^2_{t'} - \mathrm{IV}_{t'}^2\bigr|$, the median
    absolute gap over the earlier days of $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{opt}}$;
  \item full long, scaled short: $q_t = 1$ if $\hat\sigma^2_t > \mathrm{IV}_t^2$, else
    $q_t = \max\bigl((\hat\sigma^2_t - \mathrm{IV}_t^2)/\mathrm{MAD}_t, -1\bigr)$;
  \item rank of the absolute gap, over the $t - 1$ earlier days $t' < t$ of $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{opt}}$:
    \[
    q_t = \sign\bigl(\hat\sigma^2_t - \mathrm{IV}_t^2\bigr)\cdot\frac{1}{t-1}\sum_{t'<t}
      \mathbf{1}\bigl\{|\hat\sigma^2_{t'} - \mathrm{IV}_{t'}^2| \le |\hat\sigma^2_t - \mathrm{IV}_t^2|\bigr\}.
    \]
  \end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Sizing sample:} $q_t$ uses days $t' < t$ only. The first \rheSzWarmup{} days of $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{opt}}$ build the
  scale $\mathrm{MAD}_t$, so every sizing rule, the sign strategy included, is evaluated on the remaining \rheSzDays{} trading

  days, $t > \rheSzWarmup$.

If the printed sign-strategy Sharpe uses all $\cmNdays$ days, delete ``the sign strategy included'' here. If it uses the $\rheSzDays$ days, the sum in the Sharpe bullet has to run over those days.}
\item \textbf{Execution:} at the mid-quote, $r_t = q_t R_t$. At quoted bid/ask prices a purchase pays the ask and a
  sale receives the bid; the spread is paid at entry only, since settlement is in cash:
  \[
  r^{\mathrm{ba}}_t =
  \begin{cases}
  |q_t|\,\bigl(X_t/P^{a}_t - 1\bigr), & q_t > 0,\\
  |q_t|\,\bigl(1 - X_t/P^{b}_t\bigr), & q_t < 0.
  \end{cases}
  \]

\item \textbf{Transaction costs:} trading at the quoted bid or ask instead of the mid-quote costs a median
  \cmSvCostStraddle\% of the premium. No other transaction costs are deducted.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Performance measures}
\label{sec:rh_econ_measures}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Annualized Sharpe ratio,} from daily returns, at mid-quotes and at quoted bid/ask prices, over the
  days $t \in \mathcal T_{\mathrm{opt}}$ of the option-data subsample, $|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{opt}}| = T_{\mathrm{opt}}$ (the
  \rheSzDays{} days with $t > \rheSzWarmup$ for the sizing rules):
  \[
  \SR = \sqrt{252}\;\frac{\bar r}{\mathrm{sd}(r)},
  \qquad
  \mathrm{sd}(r)^2 = \frac{1}{T_{\mathrm{opt}}-1}\sum_{t \in \mathcal T_{\mathrm{opt}}}\bigl(r_t - \bar r\bigr)^2 .
  \]
  The other \rhoosNonOptDays{} days of $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{oos}}$ enter forecast accuracy only.
  The risk-free return over the half-hour holding period is negligible and is not subtracted.

\subsection{A final refinement: training on the last 30 minutes}
\label{sec:rh_econ_last30}
\label{sec:rh_results_pooled}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The scheme} (the closing-interval model, $\mathcal I = \{c\}$): every model class of
  Section~\ref{sec:rh_methods_models} is estimated on the last 30 minutes of each trading day only, the closing
  interval $i = c$ (15:30--16:00 ET), as the benchmark forecast is.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item One observation per trading day, so a period is a trading day and $\mathcal W_t = \{t - W, \dots, t - 1\}$
    holds $W = \cmPtFitWin$ trading days (Appendix~\ref{sec:rh_exp_schemes}).
  \item Predictor sets, evaluation span and trading rules as before; accuracy in Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos_last30}, on
    the same days as Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}.
    % 2026-10-15 (user): Table 1 split; was "accuracy in Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}, columns ``Last 30 minutes''."
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Training on the last 30 minutes lowers average QLIKE further,} in every model class and on both
  predictor sets: the intraday model's average QLIKE exceeds that of the closing-interval model of the same
  class, $\Delta\% = 100\bigl(\bar L^{\mathrm{pool}}/\bar L^{\mathrm{close}} - 1\bigr) > 0$, with
  $\bar L^{\mathrm{pool}}$ and $\bar L^{\mathrm{close}}$ the average losses of $f_{m,\mathcal X,\{1,\dots,M\}}$ and
  $f_{m,\mathcal X,\{c\}}$:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item linear models: $+\rhrLinBasePoolPctMin$ to $+\rhrLinBasePoolPctMax\%$ on the HAR benchmark ($\DM$ from
    \rhrLinBasePoolDmMin{} to \rhrLinBasePoolDmMax, significant at the 5\% level) and $+\rhrLinAllPoolPctMin$ to
    $+\rhrLinAllPoolPctMax\%$ on the full predictor set (significant for the lasso only);
  \item trees: $+\rhrTreeBasePoolPctMin$ to $+\rhrTreeBasePoolPctMax\%$ on the HAR benchmark and
    $+\rhrTreeAllPoolPctMin$ to $+\rhrTreeAllPoolPctMax\%$ on the full predictor set, each significant at the 5\%
    level.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{The feature result holds:} the full predictor set lowers average QLIKE in all \rhrAfClosePairs{}
  closing-interval model classes, $\Delta\%$ from \rhrAfClosePctMin{} to \rhrAfClosePctMax, none
  significantly at the 5\% level (lasso: $\Delta\% = \rhrAfLassoClosePct$, $\DM = \rhrAfLassoCloseDm$,
  \rhrAfLassoClosePval).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Across the \rhrAfPairs{} predictor-set pairs of the model set (\fqsPairsTree{} tree and
    \fqsPairsPerBarLinear{} linear specifications trained on the last 30 minutes, \fqsPairsPooled{} intraday),
    it lowers average QLIKE in \rhrAfLower, \rhrAfLowerSig{} of them significantly.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{The most accurate forecasts of the paper} are closing-interval models on the full predictor set:
  average QLIKE lasso \rhrLassoAllQ, elastic net \rhrEnetAllQ, ridge \rhrRidgeAllQ, XGBoost \rhrXgbAllQ; the
  benchmark forecast ($\overline{\QLIKE} = \rhrOlsQ$) ranks \rhrOlsRank{} of \rhrRankN.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Against the benchmark forecast, the lasso changes average QLIKE by \rhrVsBmLassoCloseFullPct\%
    ($\DM = \rhrVsBmLassoCloseFullDm$, \rhrVsBmLassoCloseFullPval); none of the four is significantly more accurate
    at the 5\% level.
  \item Of the \rhrNSet{} other forecasts of the model set, \rhrNLower{} have a lower average QLIKE than the benchmark
    forecast, \rhrNLowerSig{} of them significantly, and \rhrNWorseSig{} are significantly less accurate.
  \item Trees versus the closing-interval ridge on the same predictors: the tree has the higher average QLIKE in
    \rhrTrVsRidgeQAbove{} of \rhrTrVsRidgeN{} comparisons, \rhrTrVsRidgeQAboveSig{} of them significantly.
  \end{itemize}

    \rhoosBigDayQLMax\% of their total QLIKE loss.

  \end{itemize}
