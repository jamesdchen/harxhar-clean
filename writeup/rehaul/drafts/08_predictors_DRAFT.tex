\section{Which predictors carry the gain?}
\label{sec:rh_fi}
\label{sec:rh_results_ablation}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Questions and evidence.} Section~\ref{sec:rh_results_channels} splits the gain into an event channel,
  the scheduled FOMC statement days, and an ordinary-day channel. For each channel we ask which predictor groups carry
  it and whether its signal sits in a few series or is spread across many, with three kinds of evidence.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Accuracy: the predictor-group ablations of Table~\ref{tab:rh_ablation}, with the design and frozen
    hyperparameters of Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}: each of the \rhfiNBuckets{} groups added alone to the HAR benchmark set,
    and each removed from the full set, scored on each day type (Table~\ref{tab:rh_channels_abl}).
  \item Concentration: the lasso, which selects, against the ridge and the elastic net, which keep and shrink every
    series \citep[see][]{GiannoneLenzaPrimiceri2021}; the feature-count ladder of
    \citet{MaischZhangUlrichZimmer2026} (Table~\ref{tab:rh_channels_sparse}); and a factor-augmented HAR
    \citep{StockWatson2002} on ordinary days (Table~\ref{tab:rh_factor}).
  \item Use: each model's attribution, its TreeSHAP shares \citep{lundberg2020local} or standardized coefficient
    shares (Appendix~\ref{app:rh_fi_compute}). A share measures how much a series moves the forecast, not how much it
    improves it.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Reading.} Fixed before the day types were scored: a channel is concentrated when one group added alone
  recovers at least three-quarters of the channel's gain and its removal raises the loss significantly; it is spread
  across correlated proxies when the groups' separate gains sum to at least one and a half times the joint gain and
  the removal of at most one group raises the loss significantly.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The event channel: one series}
\label{sec:rh_fi_event}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The FOMC calendar carries it} (Table~\ref{tab:rh_channels_abl}). On statement days, the FOMC series
  added alone to the HAR benchmark set lower the loss by \rhPending{rhchEvAddFomcLinRng}\% (linear) and
  \rhPending{rhchEvAddFomcTreeRng}\% (trees), \rhPending{rhchEvFomcShareRng}\% of the channel's gain, and removing
  them from the full set raises it by \rhPending{rhchEvLoFomcRng}\%. No other group comes close.
\item \textbf{The lasso wins.} On statement days the lasso's full-set loss lies \rhPending{rhchEvLassoVsRidgePct}\%
  below the ridge's ($\DM={}$\rhPending{rhchEvLassoVsRidgeDm}), as a sparse signal predicts, and the lasso's
  top-ranked series, \rhldLassoTopOne, is the proximity to the next statement.
\item \textbf{The models use it on few bars.} Over the span the FOMC calendar holds only
  \rhfipFomcBucketMin--\rhfipFomcBucketMax\% of the trees' attribution; on statement days \rhfipFomcUntil{} holds
  \rhfipLgbmUntilFomc\% of LightGBM's, on other days \rhfipLgbmUntilOther\% (Appendix~\ref{app:rh_fi_cond}).
\item \textbf{A cost on ordinary days for the linear models.} A linear term in the FOMC series also moves the
  forecasts of ordinary days: added alone, the series raise the ordinary-day loss of the penalized linear models by
  \rhPending{rhchOrdAddFomcLinRng}\%, while the trees, which can confine the series' effect to the statement bars,
  pay no such cost (\rhPending{rhchOrdAddFomcTreeRng}\%).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The ordinary-day channel: correlated proxies of a shared volatility signal}
\label{sec:rh_fi_ordinary}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The groups substitute for one another} (Table~\ref{tab:rh_channels_abl}).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Added alone, the futures' realized moments lower the ordinary-day loss by \rhPending{rhchOrdAddRmLinRng}\%
    (linear) and \rhPending{rhchOrdAddRmTreeRng}\% (trees), close to the channel's whole gain; the constituents'
    realized moments, the liquidity measures and the order flow each lower the trees' loss by
    \rhPending{rhchOrdAddOtherTreeRng}\% on their own.
  \item Summed over groups, the separate gains are \rhPending{rhchOrdSumRatioRng} times the joint gain.
  \item Removing any one group but the futures' realized moments changes the loss by at most
    \rhPending{rhchOrdLoOtherMaxAbs}\%, \rhPending{rhchOrdLoOtherNSig}; removing the realized moments raises it by
    \rhPending{rhchOrdLoRmRng}\%.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{The ridge and the elastic net beat the lasso.} On ordinary days the ridge's full-set loss lies
  \rhPending{rhchOrdRidgeVsLassoPct}\% and the elastic net's \rhPending{rhchOrdEnetVsLassoPct}\% below the lasso's
  ($\DM={}$\rhPending{rhchOrdRidgeVsLassoDm} and \rhPending{rhchOrdEnetVsLassoDm}): a signal spread over correlated
  predictors favors methods that average them over one that picks among them.
\item \textbf{A few factors recover it} (Table~\ref{tab:rh_factor}). We add to the HAR benchmark set the first $k$
  principal components of the value averages of every non-FOMC exogenous series, standardized and re-estimated each
  day in the rolling window, and fit by OLS at every bar, as Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}'s benchmark.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item With $k = \rhPending{rhfaKStar}$ components the factor-augmented HAR recovers \rhPending{rhfaRStar}\% of the
    full set's ordinary-day gain (\rhPending{rhfaRStarVsFullDm}); partial least squares, which picks the components
    by their covariance with the target \citep{KellyPruitt2015}, recovers \rhPending{rhfaRPlsStar}\% with
    \rhPending{rhfaKPlsStar}.
  \item The first component, \rhPending{rhfaPcOneVarShare}\% of the block's variance, loads mostly on
    \rhPending{rhfaPcOneTopGroups}: a common measure of market activity and volatility.
  \item Reading: \rhPending{rhfaReading}.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{How many series} (Table~\ref{tab:rh_channels_sparse}). On the feature-count ladder, whose rung $k$ refits
  each model on its top $k$ exogenous series, ranked before the span, the ordinary-day gain accumulates over
  \rhPending{rhchOrdLadderDesc}, while on statement days the rung that adds the FOMC series carries it
  (\rhPending{rhchEvLadderDesc}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Alternative data}
\label{sec:rh_fi_alt}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No incremental gain in either channel.} Removing the StockTwits, volatility-demand or implied-volatility
  groups from the full set changes the loss by at most \rhPending{rhchAltLoMaxAbs}\% on either day type, and added
  alone to the HAR benchmark set they raise the linear models' loss (Table~\ref{tab:rh_channels_abl}).
\item \textbf{On their live windows.} These series start after the data and enter as zero until the rolling window
  holds \rhorWindowDays{} trading days of their history (Section~\ref{sec:rh_predictors}), so over most of the span
  the models see them through their availability shares. Scored only on the bars on which their values are live
  (Table~\ref{tab:rh_ablation}, Panel~E), the gains remain small: the StockTwits series change no class's loss
  significantly from \rhabLiveSentStart, and the volatility-demand series raise the penalized linear models' loss
  between \rhabLiveVdStart{} and \rhabLiveVdEnd{} (\rhabRidgeAddVdLiveVdPct\% for ridge regression).
\item \textbf{Used more while live, without a gain.} In the full years in which their values are live, the trees'
  yearly attribution shares rise: StockTwits from \rhfilStwPreMin--\rhfilStwPreMax\% to
  \rhfilStwLiveMin--\rhfilStwLiveMax\%, volatility demand from \rhfilVdPreMin--\rhfilVdPreMax\% to
  \rhfilVdLiveMin--\rhfilVdLiveMax\% (Figure~\ref{fig:rh_fi_live}). The use buys no measurable accuracy.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Use is not accuracy}
\label{sec:rh_fi_use}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Attribution ranks the channels the wrong way round.} The trees attribute
  \rhfipHarMin--\rhfipHarMax\% of their forecasts to the HAR components and \rhfipMomMin--\rhfipMomMax\% to the
  futures' realized moments, and only \rhfipFomcBucketMin--\rhfipFomcBucketMax\% to the FOMC calendar, which carries
  the event channel; the realized moments, second in attribution, cost only \rhabLgbmLoRmAllPct\% to
  \rhabXgbLoRmAllPct\% when removed, because their information is shared with other groups
  (Appendix~\ref{app:rh_fi_rank}, Table~\ref{tab:rh_fip_shares}). What a series adds to accuracy is measured by the
  ablations, not by its share.
\item \textbf{In short.} The pooled gain has two channels with different structures. On statement days it is
  concentrated: one group, the FOMC calendar, carries it, the lasso wins, and the models use the calendar on few bars.
  On ordinary days it is spread over correlated measures of one volatility signal from the futures and the index
  constituents: each recovers much of the gain alone, none but the futures' realized moments is needed given the
  others, the ridge and the elastic net beat the lasso, and \rhPending{rhfaInShort}. The alternative data add no
  measurable accuracy in either channel.
\end{itemize}
