\section{Which predictors carry the gain?}
\label{sec:rh_fi}
\label{sec:rh_results_ablation}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Questions and evidence.} Section~\ref{sec:rh_results} splits the gain into a part on the scheduled FOMC
  statement days, a part on ordinary days, and what remains beyond the HAR terms, the futures' realized moments and the
  FOMC calendar. For each part we ask which predictor groups carry it and whether its signal sits in a few series or
  is spread across many, with three kinds of evidence.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Accuracy: the predictor-group ablations of Table~\ref{tab:rh_ablation}, with the design and frozen
    hyperparameters of Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}: each of the \rhfiNBuckets{} groups added alone to the HAR benchmark
    set, and each removed from the full set, scored on each day type (Table~\ref{tab:rh_channels_abl}).
  \item Concentration: the lasso, which selects, against the ridge and the elastic net, which keep and shrink every
    series \citep[see][]{GiannoneLenzaPrimiceri2021}; the feature-count ladder of
    \citet{MaischZhangUlrichZimmer2026} (Table~\ref{tab:rh_channels_sparse}); and a factor-augmented HAR
    \citep{StockWatson2002,KellyPruitt2015} on ordinary days (Table~\ref{tab:rh_factor}).
  \item Use: each model's attribution, its TreeSHAP shares \citep{lundberg2020local} or standardized coefficient
    shares (Appendix~\ref{app:rh_fi_compute}). A share measures how much a series moves the forecast, not how much it
    improves it.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Reading.} Fixed before the day types were scored. A part of the gain is concentrated when one group added
  alone recovers at least three-quarters of it and its removal raises the loss significantly; it is spread across
  correlated proxies when the groups' separate gains sum to at least one and a half times the joint gain and the
  removal of at most one group raises the loss significantly. The two can hold at once, when one group carries the
  gain and the others repeat its information; we report both.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The FOMC calendar: one group, few bars}
\label{sec:rh_fi_event}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{It carries the statement days} (Table~\ref{tab:rh_channels_abl}). On statement days, the FOMC series
  added alone to the HAR benchmark set recover \rhPending{rhchEvFomcShareLinRng}\% of the full set's gain in the
  linear models and \rhPending{rhchEvFomcShareTreeRng}\% in the trees, and removing them from the full set raises the
  loss by \rhPending{rhchEvLoFomcRng}\%. The reading is concentrated for both model types; no other group recovers
  more than \rhPending{rhchEvOtherMaxShare}\% of it.
\item \textbf{The lasso wins.} On statement days the lasso's full-set loss differs from the ridge's by
  \rhchLassoRidgeEventPct\% ($\DM={}$\rhchLassoRidgeEventDm) and from the elastic net's by \rhchLassoEnetEventPct\%
  ($\DM={}$\rhchLassoEnetEventDm), as a signal in one series predicts; the lasso's top-ranked series, \rhldLassoTopOne,
  is the proximity to the next statement.
\item \textbf{The models use it on few bars.} Over the span the FOMC calendar holds only
  \rhfipFomcBucketMin--\rhfipFomcBucketMax\% of the trees' attribution; on statement days \rhfipFomcUntil{} holds
  \rhfipLgbmUntilFomc\% of LightGBM's, on other days \rhfipLgbmUntilOther\% (Appendix~\ref{app:rh_fi_cond}).
\item \textbf{A cost on ordinary days for the linear models.} A linear term in the FOMC series also moves the forecasts
  of ordinary days: added alone, the series raise the ordinary-day loss of the penalized linear models by
  \rhPending{rhchOrdAddFomcLinRng}\%, while the trees, which can confine the series' effect to the statement bars, pay
  no such cost (\rhPending{rhchOrdAddFomcTreeRng}\%).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The futures' realized moments: the ordinary days}
\label{sec:rh_fi_ordinary}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{They carry most of the ordinary-day gain} (Table~\ref{tab:rh_channels_abl}). Added alone to the HAR
  benchmark set, the futures' realized moments lower the ordinary-day loss by \rhchRidgeOrdAddRmPct,
  \rhchLassoOrdAddRmPct{} and \rhchEnetOrdAddRmPct\% in the ridge, lasso and elastic net, all or more of the full
  set's ordinary-day gain, and by \rhPending{rhchOrdAddRmTreeRng}\% in the trees, \rhPending{rhchOrdRmShareTreeRng}\%
  of theirs. Removing them from the full set is the only removal that raises the ordinary-day loss significantly in
  every class. The reading is concentrated (\rhchLinOrdNConcTest{} of 3 linear classes, \rhchTreeOrdNConcTest{} of 3
  trees).
\item \textbf{The other groups repeat their information.} The constituents' realized moments, the liquidity measures
  and the order flow each lower the trees' ordinary-day loss on their own (\rhPending{rhchOrdAddOtherTreeRng}\%), and
  the groups' separate gains sum to \rhPending{rhchOrdSumRatioRng} times the joint gain, but removing any one of them
  from the full set costs nothing measurable. The redundancy reading holds as well in \rhchLinOrdNRedTest{} of 3
  linear classes and \rhchTreeOrdNRedTest{} of 3 trees: the groups are correlated proxies of the volatility
  information that the futures' own moments measure most directly.
\item \textbf{Averaging beats selection on ordinary days.} On ordinary days the lasso's full-set loss lies
  \rhchLassoRidgeOrdPct\% above the ridge's and \rhchLassoEnetOrdPct\% above the elastic net's
  ($\DM={}$\rhchLassoRidgeOrdDm{} and \rhchLassoEnetOrdDm), the reverse of the statement days: the realized moments enter as
  several correlated columns, which averaging uses better than selection.
\item \textbf{Not a common factor of all the series} (Table~\ref{tab:rh_factor}). We add to the HAR benchmark set the
  first $k$ principal components, or partial-least-squares components, of the value averages of the \rhfaNSeries{}
  non-FOMC exogenous series, standardized and re-estimated each day in the rolling window, and fit by OLS at every bar.
  With $k \le 3$ the factor-augmented HAR recovers none of the full set's ordinary-day gain (at best $R_k =
  \rhfaBestSmallR$, \rhfaBestSmall), although the first three components hold \rhfaShareCumThree\% of the series'
  variance, and ten components recover $R_{10} = \rhfaPcaTenOrdR$. The leading components largely repeat the HAR
  terms; the gain sits in particular series, the realized moments, rather than in the series' common variation.
  % DRAFT: the RULE's mechanical reading is "\rhfaReading" (R_10 < 0.5); the text gives the reason rather than the label.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What remains}
\label{sec:rh_fi_rest}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A modest gain for the trees.} Beyond the HAR terms, the realized moments and the FOMC calendar, the
  exogenous series lower the ordinary-day loss of LightGBM, XGBoost and the random forest by
  \rhabLgbmFullVsAddRmFomcOrdPct, \rhabXgbFullVsAddRmFomcOrdPct{} and \rhabRfFullVsAddRmFomcOrdPct\% and leave the
  linear models' unchanged (Section~\ref{sec:rh_results_beyond}).
\item \textbf{Spread across groups, used nonlinearly.} No single remaining group is indispensable: removing any one of
  the constituents' moments, liquidity or order flow from the full set changes the trees' loss by at most
  \rhPending{rhchOrdLoOtherTreeMaxAbs}\%. The linear models extract none of this remainder, so the trees' gain comes
  from nonlinear or conditional use of information that is spread across correlated groups.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Alternative data}
\label{sec:rh_fi_alt}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No incremental gain.} Removing the StockTwits, volatility-demand or implied-volatility groups from the
  full set changes the loss by at most \rhPending{rhchAltLoMaxAbs}\% on either day type, and added alone to the HAR
  benchmark set they raise the linear models' loss (Table~\ref{tab:rh_channels_abl}).
\item \textbf{On their live windows.} These series start after the data and enter as zero until the rolling window
  holds \rhorWindowDays{} trading days of their history (Section~\ref{sec:rh_predictors}), so over most of the span
  the models see them through their availability shares. Scored only on the bars on which their values are live
  (Table~\ref{tab:rh_ablation}, Panel~E), the gains remain small: the StockTwits series change no class's loss
  significantly from \rhabLiveSentStart, and the volatility-demand series raise the penalized linear models' loss
  between \rhabLiveVdStart{} and \rhabLiveVdEnd{} (\rhabRidgeAddVdLiveVdPct\% for ridge regression).
\item \textbf{Used more while live, without a gain.} In the full years in which their values are live, the trees'
  yearly attribution shares rise: StockTwits from \rhfilStwPreMin--\rhfilStwPreMax\% to
  \rhfilStwLiveMin--\rhfilStwLiveMax\%, volatility demand from \rhfilVdPreMin--\rhfilVdPreMax\% to
  \rhfilVdLiveMin--\rhfilVdLiveMax\% (Figure~\ref{fig:rh_fi_live}). The use buys no measurable accuracy.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Use is not accuracy}
\label{sec:rh_fi_use}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Attribution ranks the parts the wrong way round.} The trees attribute
  \rhfipHarMin--\rhfipHarMax\% of their forecasts to the HAR terms and \rhfipMomMin--\rhfipMomMax\% to the futures'
  realized moments, and only \rhfipFomcBucketMin--\rhfipFomcBucketMax\% to the FOMC calendar, which carries the
  statement days; the realized moments, second in attribution, cost only \rhabLgbmLoRmAllPct\% to
  \rhabXgbLoRmAllPct\% when removed from the trees' full sets on every bar (Appendix~\ref{app:rh_fi_rank},
  Table~\ref{tab:rh_fip_shares}). What a series adds to accuracy is measured by the ablations, not by its share.
\item \textbf{In short.} The pooled gain has three parts. The FOMC calendar carries it on the statement days, in one
  group and on few bars. The futures' realized moments carry most of it on ordinary days; the constituents' moments,
  liquidity and order flow are correlated proxies of the same information, and a few common factors of all the series
  do not recover it. Beyond these and the HAR terms, the remaining exogenous series lower the trees' loss modestly and
  the linear models' not at all, and the alternative data add no measurable accuracy.
\end{itemize}
