\section{Results}
\label{sec:rh_results}
\label{sec:rh_exp_setup}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Design, benchmark and outline.} Every forecast follows the design, spans and tests of
  Sections~\ref{sec:rh_methods_setup} and~\ref{sec:rh_results_eval}: every bar of $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{oos}}$ is
  scored, and the primary benchmark is the OLS on the HAR benchmark set, the OLS row of Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}, which
  the HAR-set penalized models reproduce. We report accuracy on every bar (Section~\ref{sec:rh_results_oos}), split
  the gain into the days with a scheduled FOMC statement and all other days (Section~\ref{sec:rh_results_channels}),
  compare the full set with augmented HAR models (Section~\ref{sec:rh_results_bench}) and check robustness
  (Section~\ref{sec:rh_results_robust}). Section~\ref{sec:rh_fi} asks which predictors carry each part of the gain.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Accuracy on every bar}
\label{sec:rh_results_oos}
\label{sec:rh_results_predictors}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Overview.} Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos} reports the MSE, the average QLIKE loss and $R^2_{\mathrm{OOS}}$ of
  the six model classes on the HAR benchmark set and on the full predictor set.
\item \textbf{Against the primary benchmark.} The full predictor set lowers the average QLIKE loss of every model
  class below that of the OLS on the HAR benchmark set.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Penalized linear models, whose HAR-set forecast is the benchmark: \rhorVsOlsPctRidgeFull\% (ridge regression),
    \rhorVsOlsPctLassoFull\% (lasso) and \rhorVsOlsPctEnetFull\% (elastic net), with $\DM={}$\rhorDmRidgeB,
    \rhorDmLassoB{} and \rhorDmEnetB.
  \item Tree ensembles: \rhorVsOlsRedTreeRng{}\%, with $\DM={}$\rhorVsOlsDmLgbmFull,
    \rhorVsOlsDmXgbFull{} and \rhorVsOlsDmRfFull{} for LightGBM, XGBoost and the random forest.
  \item The reduction is significant for \rhorVsOlsLowerSig{} of the \rhorAfPairs{} full-set forecasts.
  \item On the HAR benchmark set alone the tree ensembles stay within \rhorVsOlsPctTreeHarMaxAbs\% of the
    benchmark, so the trees' gain comes from the added predictors rather than from a nonlinear use of the HAR terms.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Within each class.} The paired comparison of $f_{m,\mathrm{Full},\mathcal I_{\mathrm{rh}}}$ with
  $f_{m,\mathrm{HAR},\mathcal I_{\mathrm{rh}}}$ (the DM column of Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}) isolates what the exogenous
  predictors add to a given model $m$: the full set has the lower loss in \rhorAfLower{} of the \rhorAfPairs{}
  classes, \rhorAfLowerSig{} of them significantly, and the tree ensembles gain more from the same predictors than the
  linear models (\rhabLgbmFullAllPct\% for LightGBM, \rhabXgbFullAllPct\% for XGBoost and \rhabRfFullAllPct\% for the
  random forest; $\DM={}$\rhorDmLgbmB, \rhorDmXgbB{} and \rhorDmRfB). % the \rhab percentages become patched when the ablation generator reruns (D13)
\item \textbf{Squared-error loss.} Under squared error the comparison reverses: on every bar the full set raises the
  MSE in all \rhorAfPairs{} classes and lowers $R^2_{\mathrm{OOS}}$ (Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}). A handful of days in the
  COVID-19 crash account for the difference (Appendix~\ref{app:rh_results_calib}).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item On 2014--2019 the full set has the lower every-bar MSE in all classes; on 2020--2024 the five days with the
    largest excess squared error, all in March 2020, carry nearly all of the excess.
  \item QLIKE scores an error relative to the forecast and grows only logarithmically in over-prediction, so these
    bars weigh little in it; robust to noise in the realized-variance proxy \citep{Patton2011}, it remains the
    primary loss.
  \end{itemize}

  % check) move to Appendix app:rh_results_calib, which already reports them.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Two channels: FOMC statement days and ordinary days}
\label{sec:rh_results_channels}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Day types.} The Federal Reserve publishes its meeting calendar in advance, so whether a day carries a
  scheduled FOMC statement is known before every forecast of that day. We split $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{oos}}$ into the
  \rhPending{rhchNEventDays} statement days and the \rhPending{rhchNOrdDays} other days, the ordinary days, and score
  each part with the tests of Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}. The split is set by the calendar, not by the losses, and every
  class and predictor group is reported under it (Appendix~\rhPending{app:rh_channels}).
\item \textbf{Where the gain comes from} (Figure~\ref{fig:rh_channels}).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item The bars from 14:30 to 16:00~ET of the statement days, \rhPending{rhchEventPmBarShare}\% of the scored bars,
    carry \rhPending{rhchEventPmGainShareLinRng}\% of the full set's every-bar QLIKE gain in the penalized linear
    models and \rhPending{rhchEventPmGainShareTreeRng}\% in the tree ensembles.
  \item The statement is released at 14:00~ET. The HAR benchmark, which knows the weekday and the bar but not the
    calendar, cannot anticipate the rise in variance that follows, and QLIKE penalizes under-prediction heavily, so a
    few hundred bars move the average.
  \item On ordinary days the cumulative loss differential declines steadily, without the jumps of the statement days
    (Figure~\ref{fig:rh_channels}, panel a).
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{What the other predictors add beyond the FOMC calendar} (Table~\ref{tab:rh_channels_corr}). Three
  corrections ask the same question in different ways.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Against HAR\,+\,FOMC, the HAR benchmark set with the FOMC series added, the full set lowers the every-bar loss
    by \rhPending{rhchVsHarFomcLinRng}\% in the penalized linear models and \rhPending{rhchVsHarFomcTreeRng}\% in the
    tree ensembles, significantly in \rhPending{rhchVsHarFomcNSig} of the \rhorAfPairs{} classes.
  \item Refitted without the FOMC series, the full set lowers the every-bar loss relative to the HAR benchmark by
    \rhPending{rhchNoFomcLinRng}\% and \rhPending{rhchNoFomcTreeRng}\%.
  \item On ordinary days alone, its loss lies \rhPending{rhchOrdLinRng}\% (linear) and \rhPending{rhchOrdTreeRng}\%
    (trees) below the HAR benchmark's, significantly in \rhPending{rhchOrdNSig} of the \rhorAfPairs{} classes.
  \item The three agree within two percentage points in \rhPending{rhchNAgree} of the \rhorAfPairs{} classes. The
    pooled gain does not depend on the FOMC calendar; the calendar adds an event component on top of it, concentrated
    on the statement afternoons.
  \end{itemize}

  % exploratory: yes); reword from the RULE item-1 reading if not.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Augmented HAR benchmarks}
\label{sec:rh_results_bench}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Design} (Table~\ref{tab:rh_ablation}, Panels~B--D; Appendix~\ref{app:rh_results_ablation}). The design
  of Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}, including the full set's frozen hyperparameters, with only the columns changed. Panel~C
  fits by OLS the semivariance HAR of \citet{PattonSheppard2015}, the HARQ model of
  \citet{BollerslevPattonQuaedvlieg2016} and the leverage HAR of \citet{CorsiReno2012}, built from the same one-minute
  E-mini returns as the target.
\item \textbf{The gain survives an augmented HAR.} The futures' realized moments, computed from the same one-minute
  returns that define the target, are the natural augmented HAR.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Added to the HAR benchmark set they lower the loss in every class (Panel~B, first row;
    \rhabOlsAddRmAllPct\% for the OLS).
  \item Yet the full set is significantly more accurate than the same class's HAR benchmark set plus realized moments
    in \rhabNClassesBeatAddRm{} of the \rhabNClassesAddRm{} classes, by \rhabRidgeFullVsAddRmAllPct\% to
    \rhabLassoFullVsAddRmAllPct\% for the linear models and by \rhabRfFullVsAddRmAllPct\% to
    \rhabLgbmFullVsAddRmAllPct\% for the trees (Panel~D). % RECHECK (D13) after the ablation rerun: endpoints by class
  \item The classical extensions gain little over the benchmark: \rhabOlsSharAllPct\% for the semivariance HAR,
    \rhabOlsHarqAllPct\% for HARQ, \rhabOlsLharAllPct\% for the leverage HAR and \rhabOlsSharqlAllPct\% for the three
    together (Panel~C), and every class's full set is significantly more accurate than the last of these ($\DM$ from
    \rhabLassoFullVsOlsSharqlAllDm{} for the lasso to \rhabLgbmFullVsOlsSharqlAllDm{} for LightGBM).
  \item The exogenous predictors thus carry information that richer functions of the futures' own returns do not.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Robustness}
\label{sec:rh_results_robust}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Estimation window.} The window length $W$ of \rhorWindowDays{} trading days was chosen after the
  \rhwsNWinWord{} candidate windows from \rhwsWinMin{} to \rhrbWinLong{} trading days had been scored out of sample on
  $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{oos}}$; it is not a pre-sample choice. Across the windows the every-bar gain of the full-set
  elastic net over its HAR-set forecast lies between \rhwsEnetBarPctMin\% and \rhwsEnetBarPctMax\%, significant at
  each, and with a window of \rhrbWinLong{} days the full set remains significantly more accurate on every bar in
  \rhrbNFullLowerSigBarLong{} of the \rhrbNModelsLong{} classes (Table~\ref{tab:rh_robust_w3500};
  Appendix~\ref{app:rh_robust_window}).
\item \textbf{Multiple comparisons.} Adjusting for the number of comparisons leaves the every-bar result intact: under
  QLIKE the 90\% model confidence set \citep{HansenLundeNason2011} holds \rhmcsBarQlikeTmaxNNinety{} of the
  \rhmcsNModels{} distinct pooled forecasts (\rhmcsBarQlikeTmaxSetNinety), and StepM \citep{RomanoWolf2005} rejects
  equal accuracy of the full and HAR benchmark sets in \rhmcsBarQAfNRej{} of the \rhmcsNAlt{} classes (SPA
  $p$-value \rhmcsBarQAfSpaC; \citealp{Hansen2005}), against \rhmcsBarMAfNRejWord{} under squared error
  (Tables~\ref{tab:rh_mcs} and~\ref{tab:rh_mcs_spa}).
\end{itemize}
