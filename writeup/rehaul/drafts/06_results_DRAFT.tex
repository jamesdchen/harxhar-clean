\section{Results}
\label{sec:rh_results}
\label{sec:rh_exp_setup}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Design, benchmark and outline.} Every forecast follows the design, spans and tests of
  Sections~\ref{sec:rh_methods_setup} and~\ref{sec:rh_results_eval}: every bar of $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{oos}}$ is
  scored, and the primary benchmark is the OLS on the HAR benchmark set, the OLS row of Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}, which
  the HAR-set penalized models reproduce. We report accuracy on every bar (Section~\ref{sec:rh_results_oos}), locate
  the gain on the days with a scheduled FOMC statement and on all other days (Section~\ref{sec:rh_results_channels}),
  measure what the exogenous series add once the HAR benchmark, the futures' realized moments and the FOMC calendar
  are accounted for (Section~\ref{sec:rh_results_beyond}) and check robustness (Section~\ref{sec:rh_results_robust}).
  Section~\ref{sec:rh_fi} asks which predictors carry each part of the gain.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Accuracy on every bar}
\label{sec:rh_results_oos}
\label{sec:rh_results_predictors}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Overview.} Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos} reports the MSE, the average QLIKE loss and $R^2_{\mathrm{OOS}}$ of
  the six model classes on the HAR benchmark set and on the full predictor set.
\item \textbf{Against the primary benchmark.} The full predictor set lowers the average QLIKE loss of every model
  class below that of the OLS on the HAR benchmark set.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Penalized linear models, whose HAR-set forecast is the benchmark: \rhorVsOlsPctRidgeFull\% (ridge regression),
    \rhorVsOlsPctLassoFull\% (lasso) and \rhorVsOlsPctEnetFull\% (elastic net), with $\DM={}$\rhorDmRidgeB,
    \rhorDmLassoB{} and \rhorDmEnetB.
  \item Tree ensembles: \rhorVsOlsRedTreeRng{}\%, with $\DM={}$\rhorVsOlsDmLgbmFull,
    \rhorVsOlsDmXgbFull{} and \rhorVsOlsDmRfFull{} for LightGBM, XGBoost and the random forest.
  \item The reduction is significant for \rhorVsOlsLowerSig{} of the \rhorAfPairs{} full-set forecasts.
  \item On the HAR benchmark set alone the tree ensembles stay within \rhorVsOlsPctTreeHarMaxAbs\% of the
    benchmark, so the trees' gain comes from the added predictors rather than from a nonlinear use of the HAR terms.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Within each class.} The paired comparison of $f_{m,\mathrm{Full},\mathcal I_{\mathrm{rh}}}$ with
  $f_{m,\mathrm{HAR},\mathcal I_{\mathrm{rh}}}$ (the DM column of Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}) isolates what the exogenous
  predictors add to a given model $m$: the full set has the lower loss in \rhorAfLower{} of the \rhorAfPairs{}
  classes, \rhorAfLowerSig{} of them significantly, and the tree ensembles gain more from the same predictors than the
  linear models (\rhabLgbmFullAllPct\% for LightGBM, \rhabXgbFullAllPct\% for XGBoost and \rhabRfFullAllPct\% for the
  random forest; $\DM={}$\rhorDmLgbmB, \rhorDmXgbB{} and \rhorDmRfB).
\item \textbf{Squared-error loss.} Under squared error the comparison reverses: on every bar the full set raises the
  MSE in all \rhorAfPairs{} classes and lowers $R^2_{\mathrm{OOS}}$ (Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}). A handful of days in the
  COVID-19 crash account for the difference (Appendix~\ref{app:rh_results_calib}).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item On 2014--2019 the full set has the lower every-bar MSE in all classes; on 2020--2024 the five days with the
    largest excess squared error, all in March 2020, carry nearly all of the excess.
  \item QLIKE scores an error relative to the forecast and grows only logarithmically in over-prediction, so these
    bars weigh little in it; robust to noise in the realized-variance proxy \citep{Patton2011}, it remains the
    primary loss.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Where the gain comes from: FOMC statement days and ordinary days}
\label{sec:rh_results_channels}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Day types.} The Federal Reserve publishes its meeting calendar in advance, so whether a day carries a
  scheduled FOMC statement is known before every forecast of that day. We split $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{oos}}$ into the
  \rhchNEventDays{} statement days and the \rhchNOrdDays{} other days, the ordinary days, and score each part with the
  tests of Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}. The split is set by the calendar, not by the losses, and every class and predictor
  group is reported under it (Appendix~\rhPending{app:rh_channels}).
\item \textbf{The statement afternoons} (Figure~\ref{fig:rh_channels}).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item The bars from 14:30 to 16:00~ET of the statement days, \rhchEventPmBarPct\% of the scored bars, carry
    \rhchLinShareEventPmMin--\rhchLinShareEventPmMax\% of the full set's every-bar QLIKE gain in the penalized linear
    models and \rhchTreeShareEventPmMin--\rhchTreeShareEventPmMax\% in the tree ensembles.
  \item The statement is released at 14:00~ET. The HAR benchmark, which knows the weekday and the bar but not the
    calendar, cannot anticipate the rise in variance that follows, and QLIKE penalizes such under-prediction heavily,
    so a few hundred bars move the average; the largest differential is at the 14:30 bar.
  \item On ordinary days the cumulative loss differential declines steadily, without the jumps of the statement days
    (Figure~\ref{fig:rh_channels}).
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{The gain does not depend on the FOMC calendar} (Table~\ref{tab:rh_channels_corr}). Three corrections ask
  what the other predictors add beyond the calendar: the full set against HAR\,+\,FOMC, the HAR benchmark set with the
  FOMC series added; the full set refitted without the FOMC series against the HAR benchmark; and the full set against
  the HAR benchmark on ordinary days alone.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item They lower the loss by \rhPending{rhchCorrLinRng}\% in the penalized linear models and
    \rhPending{rhchCorrTreeRng}\% in the tree ensembles, each significantly, and agree within \rhchAgreePp{}
    percentage points in each of the \rhchNCorrAgree{} classes in which all three were run.
  \item The FOMC calendar thus adds an event component of its own, concentrated on the statement afternoons, to a
    gain that is present without it.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Beyond the HAR, the realized moments and the FOMC calendar}
\label{sec:rh_results_beyond}
\label{sec:rh_results_bench}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The reference.} Three kinds of predictors are well studied: the HAR terms; the futures' realized moments,
  computed from the same one-minute returns that define the target, the natural augmented HAR
  \citep[e.g.][]{PattonSheppard2015,BollerslevPattonQuaedvlieg2016,CorsiReno2012}; and the calendar of scheduled
  announcements. We ask what the other exogenous series, the constituents' trading, liquidity and order flow,
  option-implied volatility and the alternative data, add once all three are in the model. The comparison uses
  HAR\,+\,RM\,+\,FOMC, the HAR benchmark set with the realized moments and the FOMC series added, fitted with the
  design and frozen hyperparameters of Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos} (Table~\ref{tab:rh_ablation}, Panel~D;
  Appendix~\ref{app:rh_results_ablation}).
\item \textbf{The realized moments are a strong augmentation.} Added to the HAR benchmark set they lower the loss in
  every class (\rhabOlsAddRmAllPct\% for the OLS on every bar), and by more on ordinary days, whereas the semivariance,
  realized-quarticity and leverage extensions of the HAR model, estimated together by OLS, change the benchmark's loss
  by only \rhabOlsSharqlAllPct\% (Panel~C).
\item \textbf{What the other exogenous series add} (Table~\ref{tab:rh_ablation}, Panel~D).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Penalized linear models: against HAR\,+\,RM\,+\,FOMC the full set changes the every-bar loss by
    \rhabRidgeFullVsAddRmFomcAllPct, \rhabLassoFullVsAddRmFomcAllPct{} and \rhabEnetFullVsAddRmFomcAllPct\% in the
    ridge, lasso and elastic net ($\DM={}$\rhabRidgeFullVsAddRmFomcAllDm, \rhabLassoFullVsAddRmFomcAllDm{} and
    \rhabEnetFullVsAddRmFomcAllDm), none significantly.
  \item Tree ensembles: by \rhabLgbmFullVsAddRmFomcAllPct, \rhabXgbFullVsAddRmFomcAllPct{} and
    \rhabRfFullVsAddRmFomcAllPct\% in LightGBM, XGBoost and the random forest ($\DM={}$\rhabLgbmFullVsAddRmFomcAllDm,
    \rhabXgbFullVsAddRmFomcAllDm{} and \rhabRfFullVsAddRmFomcAllDm), and on ordinary days by
    \rhabLgbmFullVsAddRmFomcOrdPct, \rhabXgbFullVsAddRmFomcOrdPct{} and \rhabRfFullVsAddRmFomcOrdPct\%
    ($p = \rhabLgbmFullVsAddRmFomcOrdP$, \rhabXgbFullVsAddRmFomcOrdP{} and \rhabRfFullVsAddRmFomcOrdP).
  \item The well-studied predictors thus account for nearly all of the gain of Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}: HAR\,+\,RM\,+\,FOMC
    alone lowers the every-bar loss below the HAR benchmark's by \rhabRidgeAddRmFomcVsHarAllPct\% (ridge) to
    \rhabXgbAddRmFomcVsHarAllPct\% (XGBoost).
  \end{itemize}
  % 2026-10-26: requested by the author after the channel results; not pre-registered (ablation RULE addendum F).
\item \textbf{In sum.} Beyond the well-studied predictors, the other exogenous series improve the gradient-boosted
  trees' forecasts modestly and significantly, almost all of it on ordinary days, the random forest's marginally, and
  the penalized linear models' not at all.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Robustness}
\label{sec:rh_results_robust}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Estimation window.} The window length $W$ of \rhorWindowDays{} trading days was chosen after the
  \rhwsNWinWord{} candidate windows from \rhwsWinMin{} to \rhrbWinLong{} trading days had been scored out of sample on
  $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{oos}}$; it is not a pre-sample choice. Across the windows the every-bar gain of the full-set
  elastic net over its HAR-set forecast lies between \rhwsEnetBarPctMin\% and \rhwsEnetBarPctMax\%, significant at
  each, and with a window of \rhrbWinLong{} days the full set remains significantly more accurate on every bar in
  \rhrbNFullLowerSigBarLong{} of the \rhrbNModelsLong{} classes (Table~\ref{tab:rh_robust_w3500};
  Appendix~\ref{app:rh_robust_window}).
\item \textbf{Multiple comparisons.} Adjusting for the number of comparisons leaves the every-bar result intact: under
  QLIKE the 90\% model confidence set \citep{HansenLundeNason2011} holds \rhmcsBarQlikeTmaxNNinety{} of the
  \rhmcsNModels{} distinct pooled forecasts (\rhmcsBarQlikeTmaxSetNinety), and StepM \citep{RomanoWolf2005} rejects
  equal accuracy of the full and HAR benchmark sets in \rhmcsBarQAfNRej{} of the \rhmcsNAlt{} classes (SPA
  $p$-value \rhmcsBarQAfSpaC; \citealp{Hansen2005}), against \rhmcsBarMAfNRejWord{} under squared error
  (Tables~\ref{tab:rh_mcs} and~\ref{tab:rh_mcs_spa}).
\end{itemize}
