\subsection{Closing-interval standardization}
\label{sec:rh_close_scale}
The closing-interval model of Section~\ref{sec:rh_econ_last30} standardizes the averages of series values on its
own window $\mathcal{W}_t$ (Section~\ref{sec:rh_exp_schemes}). Rows $t'$ are trading days ($i = c$ suppressed):
\[
  z^{(k)}_{t} = \frac{\bar u^{(k)}_{t} - \operatorname{med}_{t' \in \mathcal{W}_t} \bar u^{(k)}_{t'}}
    {\max\bigl\{\mathrm{IQR}^{(k)}_{t},\ 0.01 \max_{t' \le t} \mathrm{IQR}^{(k)}_{t'},\
      \mathrm{IQR}^{\ast(k)}_{t}\bigr\}},
  \qquad \mathrm{IQR}^{(k)}_{t} = \mathrm{IQR}\bigl\{\bar u^{(k)}_{t'} : t' \in \mathcal{W}_t\bigr\}.
\]
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{IQR}^{\ast(k)}_{t}$: IQR of $\bar u^{(k)}_{t'}$, $t' < t$, over rows whose $k$-interval averaging
  window is fully observed.
\item Column whose mode accounts for at least 20\% of rows: $\mathrm{IQR}^{\ast(k)}_{t}$ also excludes rows at
  the mode.
\item $\mathrm{IQR}^{\ast(k)}_{t} = 1$ until 8 such rows exist.
\item $\bar a^{(k)}$, $\bar\chi^{(k)}$: not standardized. HAR components, calendar dummies:
  $\mathrm{IQR}^{\ast(k)}_{t} = 0$.
\item $t < W$: the first window's statistics.
\end{itemize}

\section{Data and predictors}
\label{app:rh_data}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Sources and spans.} Eight groups, in Section~\ref{sec:rh_data}. Closing-interval models use all
  \rhfiNExog{} exogenous series. Intraday models use the \rhdmNSeriesPooled{} series in every group except FOMC.
\item \textbf{Predictor dictionary.} Appendix Table~\ref{tab:rh_dm_dictionary} defines each series. Trailing
  averages, activity indicators, and calendar columns are added in Section~\ref{sec:rh_predictors}.
\item \textbf{Missing and stale values.} For an exogenous series $u$, the availability indicator
  $a_{t,i} = \mathbf{1}\{u \text{ observed at } (t,i)\}$ is recorded before any filling
  (Section~\ref{sec:rh_data}). The series is then forward-filled with no staleness limit. On every interval with
  $a_{t,i} = 0$ the stored regressor is the imputation of Section~\ref{sec:rh_predictors}, step 4: the median of
  the series' observed adjusted values on earlier retained intervals, or the adjusted value on its first observed
  interval when no earlier observation exists.
\item \textbf{Duplicate and constant predictors.} At each estimation the window screen of
  Section~\ref{sec:rh_predictors} drops a column that is constant on $\mathcal W_{t,i}$ or an exact bitwise copy
  of an earlier kept column. The first copy is kept. On the closing-interval model with the full predictor set
  the screen keeps a median of \cmKeptAll{} of the \cmPNewAll{} columns.
\item \textbf{SPXW chain.} Quotes on S\&P 500 Weeklys that expire the same day, every 30 minutes: bid, ask,
  mid-quote, underlying level, implied volatility, and delta. The file covers \rhdmSpxwExpirations{} expirations,
  2020--2025. The straddle in Section~\ref{sec:rh_econ_strategy} uses the 15:30 quotes. A day is dropped when the
  wider of the two strikes is more than 10 index points from $S_t$, or when fewer than 10 contracts are quoted
  % 2026-10-17 (user, E18): parked -- the two prices are now named. Was: Which price is $S_t$, and which price is the official close, is the open question in note E18.
  at that stamp. $S_t$ is the 15:30 underlying print on the chain, and the official close is the S\&P~500 close.
\item \textbf{Implied-volatility inversion.} The vendor quote on each leg is a volatility per hour, from a
  geometric bisection. A solve that does not converge returns a bracket node of that bisection; those nodes are
  set to missing before the two legs are averaged, and a missing leg makes the package's implied variance
  missing. The 16:00 stamp is not used. The conversion from the hourly quote to the closing half hour is the
  display in Section~\ref{sec:rh_econ_strategy}.

\section{Hyperparameter selection by model}
\label{app:rh_hyper}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Penalty parameters.} Ridge, lasso, and elastic net minimize the raw sum of squares plus the
  penalty in Section~\ref{sec:rh_methods_models}. $\lambda$ is the coefficient on that penalty for ridge. For
  lasso and elastic net the printed grid is the scikit-learn parameter, and the coefficient in the sum is $2n$
  times the printed value. Lasso is $\alpha = 1$. Elastic net is $\alpha = 0.5$. The block weights $\omega_j$
  are $\rhfiBackbonePf$ on the HAR benchmark, $1$ on an additional predictor, and $0$ on the intercept. The
  HAR benchmark fitted alone uses $\omega_j = 1$ on every predictor.
\item \textbf{Where $\lambda$ is chosen.} One grid value per decade, Table~\ref{tab:rh_dm_hyper}. The pre-sample
  is \cmPtFolds{} folds of \cmPtFoldLen{} trading days, \cmPtValFirst{} to \cmPtValLast, and the criterion is
  validation MSE (Section~\ref{sec:rh_methods_tuning}). A light re-tune every \cmPtBlock{} trading days lets the
  neighboring grid points challenge the incumbent. The best challenger replaces it only when the
  Diebold--Mariano statistic on the paired squared errors is significantly negative. The full predictor set
  stays at the top of its grid. The HAR-benchmark column of the table is the pre-sample value; the four
  replacements are note E23.

  Section~\ref{sec:rh_methods_tuning} produced a tree cell of Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos} is note E22.
