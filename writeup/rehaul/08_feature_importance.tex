\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Questions.} Which predictors carry the pooled intraday forecasts, and does that change over time? Is the
  exogenous signal dense and weak, spread thinly over many series, or concentrated in a few? Do the series that matter
  do so only on some days, at some bars of the day, or through interactions with other predictors? And how do the
  nonlinear models, the tree ensembles, respond to the non-standard series, the ones outside the HAR benchmark, the
  futures' own realized moments and trading activity, and the VIX?
\item \textbf{What is computed.} The pooled intraday models of the final design (rolling window of
  \rhfipWindowDays{} trading days, \rhfipWindowBars{} bars; full predictor set of \rhfipNCols{} predictors in
  \rhfipNGroups{} groups: each of the \rhfipNExog{} exogenous series with its \rhfipNPerSeries{} predictors, the HAR
  components and the calendar dummies), explained at monthly origins.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Origins $k = 1, \dots, K$, $K = \rhfipNOrigins$: one every \rhfipOriginEvery{} trading days, at the
    \rhfipBarFirst{} bar, from \rhfipFirstOrigin{} to \rhfipLastOrigin. Origin $k$'s model forecasts its block $\mathcal O_k$,
    the \rhfipOriginEvery{} trading days up to the next origin (the last block: \rhfipLastBlock{} days); the blocks
    partition all $|\mathcal O| = \rhfipNRows$ bars from \rhfipFirstOrigin{} to \rhfipLastDay. The penalties and the
    tree configurations were chosen on the last pre-sample fold, which ends on \rhfipTuneLast, so no explained bar
    lies in a tuning fold.
  \item Tree ensembles (LightGBM, XGBoost, random forest): at each origin the model is refit on the
    \rhfipWindowBars{} bars before it, as its every-bar walk refits at that bar, and held for the block.
    Path-dependent TreeSHAP values \citep{lundberg2020local} of every bar $(t,i) \in \mathcal O_k$, on the scale of
    the forecast, add up to it, and a group's value sums its predictors' values:
    \[
      \hat y_{t,i} = \phi_{k,0} + \sum_{j=1}^{p} \phi_{t,i,j}, \qquad
      \phi_{t,i,A} = \sum_{j \in A} \phi_{t,i,j}, \qquad
      s_A = \frac{I_A}{\sum_{B} I_B}, \quad I_A = \frac{1}{|\mathcal O|} \sum_{(t,i) \in \mathcal O} |\phi_{t,i,A}| ,
    \]
    with $\phi_{k,0}$ the model's expected forecast over its own training sample (the trees' node covers), so no
    background sample is chosen.
  \item Linear models (ridge, lasso, elastic net): the walk's own coefficients at each origin on the
    window-standardized predictors, which the penalty acts on, $\gamma_{k,j} = \hat\beta_{k,j}\,\mathrm{sd}_{k,j}$,
    and a group's share $S_A = \sum_k \sum_{j \in A} |\gamma_{k,j}| / \sum_k \sum_{j} |\gamma_{k,j}|$; penalties as
    frozen for this window (ridge \rhfipRidgePen, lasso \rhfipLassoPen, elastic net \rhfipEnetPen), with the penalty
    factor \rhfipHarPf{} on the HAR components and the calendar dummies.
  \item Two classes, fixed before any SHAP value was computed. Standard (\rhfipNStd{} groups): the HAR components,
    the calendar dummies, the futures' \rhfipNMom{} realized moments, the VIX, and the futures' volume and trade count.
    Non-standard: the other \rhfipNNonStd{} series (constituent liquidity, the equal- and value-weighted market
    moments, StockTwits sentiment, volatility demand, the FOMC calendar, VVIX and VIX3M).
  \item Intervals: \rhfipCiLevel\% moving-block bootstrap over the origins (blocks of \rhfipBootBlock{} origins, about a year;
    \rhfipBootB{} resamples), paired across models. They measure the variation over the span's origins, not the
    sampling error of one fit.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Scope.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item The trees and the linear models are measured differently (mean absolute SHAP value against $|\gamma|$), so
    the comparison is of rankings and of shares of each model's own measure, not of magnitudes. With the penalty
    factor on the HAR block, the linear models' HAR shares are not comparable with the trees'; comparisons across
    model types use the exogenous total.
  \item A SHAP share measures how much a series moves the forecast, not how much it improves its accuracy.
  \item Between correlated predictors (the equal- and value-weighted versions of a series, a series' value and
    availability averages) the credit follows the trees' split choices; summing over a group removes the split
    within the group, not the split between groups.
  \item SHAP explains each origin's refit held for its block, whereas the walks refit at every bar; the two
    coincide at the origin bars. There the refits equal the walks' forecasts bit for bit at \rhfipLgbmGateEq,
    \rhfipXgbGateEq{} and \rhfipRfGateEq{} of the \rhfipNOrigins{} origins (LightGBM, XGBoost, random forest). The
    linear coefficients are the walks' own (their replayed forecasts match to \rhfipLinGateMax).
  \item XGBoost computes SHAP values in single precision: they add up to the forecast within \rhfipXgbAddMax{}
    (tolerance \rhfipXgbAddTol); LightGBM's and the forest's within \rhfipAddMaxDouble.
  \item Not covered yet: the MinGRU network (parked), and the ablations (leave one category out, then one series out
    within the categories that matter), which are not run.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Which series matter} (Table~\ref{tab:rh_fip_shares}, Figure~\ref{fig:rh_fip_rank}).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item In the trees the HAR components carry \rhfipHarMin--\rhfipHarMax\% of the total attribution and the
    futures' realized moments \rhfipMomMin--\rhfipMomMax\%. The two leading exogenous series are the same in all
    three, \rhfipTreeTopOne{} and \rhfipTreeTopTwo; \rhfipTreeTopOne{} alone holds \rhfipLgbmTopOneEx,
    \rhfipXgbTopOneEx{} and \rhfipRfTopOneEx\% of the exogenous total.
  \item The non-standard series hold \rhfipLgbmNs\% [\rhfipLgbmNsLo, \rhfipLgbmNsHi], \rhfipXgbNs\%
    [\rhfipXgbNsLo, \rhfipXgbNsHi] and \rhfipRfNs\% [\rhfipRfNsLo, \rhfipRfNsHi] of the total (LightGBM, XGBoost,
    random forest), \rhfipNsExMin--\rhfipNsExMax\% of the exogenous total. Their weight sits in a few series; the five
    largest by the mean over the three trees are \rhfipNsOne{} (\rhfipNsOneMin--\rhfipNsOneMax\% of the total), \rhfipNsTwo{}
    (\rhfipNsTwoMin--\rhfipNsTwoMax\%), \rhfipNsThree{} (\rhfipNsThreeMin--\rhfipNsThreeMax\%), \rhfipNsFour{}
    (\rhfipNsFourMin--\rhfipNsFourMax\%) and \rhfipNsFive{} (\rhfipNsFiveMin--\rhfipNsFiveMax\%). The rest carry

  \item The ridge ranks the series differently. Its largest exogenous series is \rhfipRidgeTopOne{}
    (\rhfipRidgeTopOneEx\% of its exogenous total), and it puts the FOMC calendar near the top: \rhfipFomcUntil{} at
    rank \rhfipRidgeRankFomcUntil{} and \rhfipFomcDay{} at rank \rhfipRidgeRankFomcDay{} of \rhfipNExog, where the
    trees rank them \rhfipTreeRankFomcUntilMin--\rhfipTreeRankFomcUntilMax{} and
    \rhfipTreeRankFomcDayMin--\rhfipTreeRankFomcDayMax. Spearman rank correlation of the \rhfipNExog{} shares with the
    ridge's: \rhfipLgbmSpRidge, \rhfipXgbSpRidge, \rhfipRfSpRidge{} (the two top-\rhfipTopN{} lists share
    \rhfipLgbmTopRidge, \rhfipXgbTopRidge{} and \rhfipRfTopRidge{} series); among the trees: \rhfipSpLgbmXgb{} (LightGBM, XGBoost),
    \rhfipSpLgbmRf{} and \rhfipSpXgbRf{} (each against the forest).
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Dense and weak, or concentrated?}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Linear models across windows (Table~\ref{tab:rh_fip_window}). If the exogenous signal were dense and weak,
    more observations per coefficient would bring more series in: a longer window would select more exogenous series
    (P1) and spread the weight over more of them (P2). The test refits nothing new: the walks at $W$ from
    \rhfipWMin{} to \rhfipWMax{} trading days, at the same \rhfipNOrigins{} origins, with the predictions and the rule
    fixed before computing. At a penalty held fixed across windows, none of the \rhfipSOneMeasures{} statistics
    rises with $W$ (\rhfipSOneFalls{} fall, \rhfipSOneFlat{} stay flat); for example the lasso at \rhfipLassoSOneLo{}
    selects \rhfipLassoSOneVWMin{} exogenous series on average at $W = \rhfipWMin$ and \rhfipLassoSOneVWMax{} at
    \rhfipWMax. At the penalties tuned for each window, P1 is ``\rhfipEnetPOneWalk'' for the elastic net and
    ``\rhfipLassoPOneWalk'' for the lasso, but the counts rise only where the tuned penalty falls tenfold (elastic
    net: from \rhfipEnetPenWMin{} at $W = \rhfipWMin$ to \rhfipEnetPenWNext{} at \rhfipWNext); the ridge's weight does not
    spread ($N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ \rhfipRidgeNeffWMin{} at \rhfipWMin{} and \rhfipRidgeNeffWMax{} at \rhfipWMax{} days,
    slope \rhfipRidgeNeffSlope{} per \rhfipSlopeUnit{} days [\rhfipRidgeNeffSlopeLo, \rhfipRidgeNeffSlopeHi]). The dense-and-weak
    reading is not supported for the linear models. A falling count is also what a signal that changes over time
    would produce, since a longer window averages over more regimes; the study does not test that, so it does not
    show a sparse and strong signal either.
