# Keep the claim rules, script the checks

Our `paper-prose` skill beats every published writing skill, agent rules file and economics style guide on the rules that matter most for a JFEc results paper. It trails the best of them on the machinery that would show those rules are followed. **Nothing public ties a word to a computed value:** V2 (numbers only from macros), V4 (verdict words from predicates), V5 (Rule NT) and V6 (no clipped verdict tail) have at most partial analogues, and no other skill grades the evidence behind its rules. But all of this is enforced by an 18-line self-check that the drafting agent runs on its own output, after reading **about 6,000 to 6,900 words of patterns per branch**. That is the setting where instruction-following studies find silent omissions, and where a model correcting its own work is weakest. The skills that do better win on mechanism: scripted checks with rule tests, severity tied to action, read-only critics in fresh contexts, and fixture and trigger evals. The economics and general guides win where our skill does not reach: exhibits the text must discuss, the abstract, introduction and conclusion, the economic size of results, and an old-before-new information order that V1's wording contradicts. Borrow in this order: hygiene first (the description is **1,060 characters against a 1,024-character maximum**), then one check script with tests, a read-only critic, incident fixtures, and only then new patterns. Avoid what the most popular skills sell: detector evasion, "vary sentence length" and "sounds human" targets, LLM quality scores, generic word lists and blanket active-voice rules; the evidence counts against each of them.

## 1. No public skill ties a word to a computed value

The comparison read the published Claude Code and Agent Skills for writing, the rules files of other agent ecosystems, economists' own setups and the major style guides. It set them against our 103-line router `.claude/skills/paper-prose/SKILL.md`, the index `writeup/style/voice.md` and the 18 pattern files under `writeup/style/patterns/`, about 8,760 words in all (repo, 29 Oct 2026).

The most popular skill, **blader/humanizer (62,387 GitHub stars on 29 Oct 2026)**, rewrites text "without changing what it says". It works through 26 patterns grouped by how prose sounds: staging, rhythm, inflation and formatting ([humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer/blob/main/SKILL.md)).

The academic pipelines put their rigor into provenance rather than sentences. K-Dense's scientific-writing skill requires that "Every factual or numeric manuscript claim must map to verified evidence IDs". It also re-verifies the stored hash of a claim's text after any rewording ([K-Dense scientific-writing](https://github.com/K-Dense-AI/scientific-agent-skills/blob/main/skills/scientific-writing/SKILL.md)). Its linter comes closest to a verdict check, with a regex for "proves", "definitively", "universally" and "highly significant" ([lint_manuscript.py](https://github.com/K-Dense-AI/scientific-agent-skills/blob/main/skills/scientific-writing/scripts/lint_manuscript.py)).

Economists state the right goals, but only as advice. Eric Luo writes "Effects get a magnitude, a direction, and a benchmark" ([Luo CLAUDE.md](https://github.com/ericluo04/claude-academic-workflow/blob/main/CLAUDE.md)). Pedro Sant'Anna's output style says "Every number carries its units, its uncertainty, and its source" ([academic-writing.md](https://github.com/pedrohcgs/claude-code-my-workflow/blob/main/.claude/output-styles/academic-writing.md)). Geoff Boeing's template asks the agent to "Verify paper text's claims against the figures/tables' numbers, signs, rankings" ([Boeing AGENTS.md](https://github.com/gboeing/project-template/blob/main/AGENTS.md)).

**None of them checks a comparison or verdict word against a test outcome.** No skill, rules file or guide we found has a counterpart to V4's predicate rule. None has one for V5's two-step test either: delete a universal the context already implies, and back any word that survives with a count or an argmax.

Where outside authors arrived at one of our rules, they did so independently and for a different reason. humanizer's first pattern covers "a clipped negative tail ('..., no guessing')" ([humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer/blob/main/SKILL.md)). Luo bans trailing editorial clauses with V6's own recipe: "If the point is worth making it gets its own sentence. If not, delete it" ([Luo CLAUDE.md](https://github.com/ericluo04/claude-academic-workflow/blob/main/CLAUDE.md)). Both object to how such a tail sounds. V6 objects to what it hides. Section 8's ", none significantly" was false while LightGBM's sentiment removal had p = 0.035, and no one checked it because it carried no number (`writeup/style/patterns/claims/V6-no-after-the-fact-tails.md`). The external lists judge sound; ours also judge truth. Truth is the failure that costs a JFEc paper its referee's trust.

The provenance gate is also ahead. Step 0 asks whose text it is before any rule applies, and V13 and V15 keep the author's and collaborators' words out of every pass. The nearest equivalents are weaker. better-writing has a light-edit mode in which "Every word starts as kept", and it applies its contrast budget only "to text the skill writes, not to a writer's own" ([better-writing evals](https://github.com/forjd/better-writing/blob/main/evals/README.md)). humanizer lets a user's writing sample override "the patterns below, including the dash rule" ([humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer/blob/main/SKILL.md)).

The evidence supports keeping V13 and V15 absolute. Twenty-four researchers revised their own abstracts using ChatGPT-4's suggestions, and blinded raters scored the results above the originals (mean difference 1.10, 95% CI 0.54–1.66). ChatGPT-only drafts scored lowest, with summary means of **65 against 82 for the originals and 90 for the author-revised versions**, and its rewrites contained 10 minor and 3 major factual errors ([Gravel et al.](https://medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.02.09.24302591v1.full.txt)). Writers also feel less ownership of a text the less they take part in writing it ([Draxler et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03283v1)).

Three more of our choices are ahead of practice. First, the field whitelist (leverage effect, robust standard errors, significant) has no equal. anywhere-agents denies any write that contains one of about 45 words, among them "undermine", "gauge", "acquire" and "unprecedented" ([anywhere-agents](https://github.com/yzhao062/anywhere-agents/blob/main/AGENTS.md)). agent-style rewrites "leverage" as "use" ([agent-style RULES.md](https://github.com/yzhao062/agent-style/blob/main/RULES.md)). Only Sant'Anna's "(in non-finance contexts)" and Luo's "'Significantly' stays when it means statistically significant" exempt domain words.

Second, V12's ban on global style targets matches the largest study of rules files. It covered 679 files, 25,532 rules and more than 5,000 Claude Code runs, and **every rule that helped on its own was a negative constraint, while every rule that hurt was a positive directive** such as "follow code style" ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11088)). Third, no other skill grades the evidence for its rules. humanizer cites a single source, Wikipedia's catalog. Orchestra's paper-writing skill cites authorities without evidence that their rules work ([ml-paper-writing](https://github.com/Orchestra-Research/AI-Research-SKILLs/blob/main/20-ml-paper-writing/ml-paper-writing/SKILL.md)).

Being first has a cost. No outside system implements a predicate check, so nothing outside this repo shows that V4 or V5 works with an LLM drafter. better-writing's eval exposes a failure our rules only half cover. Its ranking-claims fixture exists because "Trimming a flourish or a qualifier often takes a ranking word with it, and each one is a claim" ([better-writing evals](https://github.com/forjd/better-writing/blob/main/evals/README.md)). V5's NEEDED? step deletes universals by design, and V16 exempts "the operation's own target". The only guard against deleting a universal that *is* the claim is therefore V5's clause (c), plus a deletion judge that does not exist yet. The rules that make our skill distinctive are also its least tested.

## 2. An 18-line self-check cannot carry 6,000 words of rules

The skill is candid about how it enforces its rules. Step 2 says "Most automatic checks (C2, C5, C18, C25, C26, `scrub.py`) are not built yet, so this checklist is the check" (`.claude/skills/paper-prose/SKILL.md`). Branch A, drafting a claim, sends the agent through SKILL.md and nine pattern files, about 6,000 words. Branch F, the edit-stage pass, reads eleven files, about 6,860 words (repo, `wc -w`). The drafter then ticks 18 checklist lines on its own text, in its own context.

Studies of instruction following predict how this setup fails. IFScale had 20 models write a report under 10 to 500 keyword instructions. **claude-opus-4 scored 100% at 50 instructions, 94.6% at 100, 67.9% at 250 and 44.6% at 500.** Instructions later in the list were broken more often than earlier ones, and nearly all errors were silent omissions rather than failed attempts ([IFScale](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11538v1)). The chance of meeting every constraint at once falls even when there are only a few ([Harada et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.21051)). Models lose 39% of their performance on average when instructions arrive over several turns instead of one ([Laban et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06120)). IFScale's instructions are keywords, not prose rules, and no study tests adherence to a long style guide, so the size of the effect on this skill is unknown. The direction is not in doubt.

The skill's own ordering adds to the risk. SKILL.md says "claims come before numbers and sentences because a wrong claim matters more than its wording". Yet branch A opens with V1, a sentence rule, and only then reads the claim rules V4, V6 and V5. Step 2 lists them in the same order.

A self-check by the same model is the weak form of critique. Without outside feedback, models "struggle to self-correct their responses ... and at times, their performance even degrades after self-correction" ([Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01798v2)). LLM evaluators also rate their own output above others' ([Panickssery et al.](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/7f1f0218e45f5414c79c0679633e47bc-Abstract-Conference.html)).

Critique works when each item can be checked against something outside the model. Yes/no checklists written for each instruction raised the agreement between LLM judges and human preferences from 46.4% to 52.2%, and self-refinement against such a checklist gained 7.8 points ([TICK](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03608v1)). Replacing rating scales with separate yes/no questions raised agreement across 12 evaluator models by 0.45 ([CheckEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.18771v2)). The fix, then, is to turn as many checklist lines as possible into answers about a script's output. Most of them already can be. The table sorts Step 2's lines by whether code can check them with what the repo has today (this report's classification).

| Step 2 line | Can code check it now? | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| V2 typed digits, V10 inanimate subjects with evaluative verbs, AP-V, AP-S | Yes | The greps already written in each pattern's Detection section |
| V6 clipped tails, V9 titles with a colon, question or clause | Yes, as candidates for review | The V6 grep; a grep over `\section` arguments |
| V3 one name per estimator | Yes, for listed terms | Grep the left column of the notation guide's §5 and the clashes listed in its addenda |
| V13 author words, V14 nothing outside the target, V16 content kept | Yes | Byte comparison against the dated record; a sentence diff outside the target; macro, `\cite` and `\ref` multisets plus a diff of negations, modals and direction words |
| V12 named operations | Yes | Every edit logged with a rule id |
| V5 Rule NT, V15 Overleaf text | Partly | A lexicon grep finds candidates, but NEEDED? stays a judgment; for V15, the pull report and `git blame` until the provenance sidecar exists |
| V1, V4, V7, V8, V11 | No | Judgment, until predicates (C5), the qualifier diff (C19) and corpus n-grams (S8) exist |

**Code can check eleven of the 18 lines today and two more in part. Five need judgment.** The skills that enforce their rules well put the mechanical part in code.

dvdsosa's sciwrite-prose ships three checking scripts: `prose_audit.py`, `keyword_consistency.py` (our V3) and `numeric_consistency.py` (our V2). Its principle is "The scripts do the grep so you can do the judgment" ([sciwrite-prose](https://github.com/dvdsosa/scientific-writing-skills/blob/master/skills/sciwrite-prose/SKILL.md)).

Vale, the mature prose linter, gives each rule its own file. Each file holds an id, a level (suggestion, warning or error), a message that names the fix, and support for switching the rule off inline by its id. "Only errors produce a non-zero exit code" ([Vale styles](https://docs.vale.sh/topics/styles)). Since version 3.24, rules carry tests that must fire and must not fire, on the view that "a rule that matches nothing fails silently" ([Vale docs](https://docs.vale.sh/llms-full.txt)).

Vale cannot read LaTeX. Our detex step (S1 in the design report's §8.4) will have to report each warning at its line in the `.tex` source. TeXtidote already solves that problem ([TeXtidote](https://github.com/sylvainhalle/textidote)).

anywhere-agents runs its word list as a hook that fires before every write. The hook blocks a write that contains a listed word and returns "a `Suggested rewrite:` line", and it exempts files that quote the banned words ([anywhere-agents](https://github.com/yzhao062/anywhere-agents/blob/main/AGENTS.md)). Claude Code's own documentation says that a rule which must hold every time belongs in a hook ([Claude Code skills](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/skills)).

Severity is the second mechanism we lack. humanizer marks which patterns "justify an edit on one sighting" and which are "weak alone" ([humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer/blob/main/SKILL.md)). avoid-ai-writing grades rules from P0 to P2 and holds that "Detection alone never authorizes rewriting" ([avoid-ai-writing](https://github.com/conorbronsdon/avoid-ai-writing/blob/main/SKILL.md)). Sant'Anna's auditor weights hits by density and position. Transition words rate HIGH "if > 1 per 1000 words", and cliché words rate HIGH on the "first three pages (abstract, intro, opening of methods)" ([humanize-auditor](https://github.com/pedrohcgs/claude-code-my-workflow/blob/main/.claude/agents/humanize-auditor.md)). Our AP files have a Flag column, but the V files have no severity field, so Step 2 cannot say which lines block a return and which only inform the critic.

The critic is the third gap. Branch G says "Do not rewrite" in prose, and it runs wherever it is invoked, often in the drafter's own context. Sant'Anna enforces review-only with tools: his humanize skill sets `disallowed-tools: ["Edit","MultiEdit"]` ([humanize](https://github.com/pedrohcgs/claude-code-my-workflow/blob/main/.claude/skills/humanize/SKILL.md)). His auditor also has a written boundary: it reviews neither grammar nor substance, and it flags instead of rewriting ([humanize-auditor](https://github.com/pedrohcgs/claude-code-my-workflow/blob/main/.claude/agents/humanize-auditor.md)).

Anthropic's doc-coauthoring skill adds the one mechanism that tests prose against a reader rather than a rule list. A fresh subagent sees only the document and answers the questions a reader would ask. The loop ends when "Reader Claude consistently answers questions correctly" ([doc-coauthoring](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/doc-coauthoring/SKILL.md)). sciwrite-prose has a line our critic needs: "If the text is already good, say so and stop. Manufacturing findings to look thorough wastes the author's time" ([sciwrite-prose](https://github.com/dvdsosa/scientific-writing-skills/blob/master/skills/sciwrite-prose/SKILL.md)). The design report already requires critics in fresh contexts, and from another model family at milestones (§8.6). The skill does not yet implement either.

## 3. The best skills measure themselves; ours has never met a fixture

better-writing is the clearest model. Its harness runs 22 fixtures, each "a text seeded with known AI tells and known facts", with strings the output must keep and strings it must drop. Nine fixtures are held out and stay "never-read" during development. A second model call lists any claims the rewrite invented. A corpus of about 1,900 words of pre-ChatGPT human prose must leave every check silent ([better-writing evals](https://github.com/forjd/better-writing/blob/main/evals/README.md)).

Its results carry three lessons for us. First, loading reference files on demand rather than up front **passed 77 of 110 checks against 93 of 110, a paired difference of −0.15 [−0.24, −0.05]**: an agent told to read reference files often does not. Second, its trigger eval fired on 30 of 30 prompts that should trigger the skill and on 0 of 30 near misses. Third, its em-dash ban fired eleven times on Jerome K. Jerome, so the ban stayed tied to one fixture rather than applying everywhere.

Imbad0202's academic-research-skills runs gold-set evals behind a CI "ranking-lift gate" ([ARS evals](https://github.com/Imbad0202/academic-research-skills/blob/main/evals/README.md)). Every number in this paragraph and the two before it is reported by the skills' own authors. No independent benchmark of any writing skill exists, and an August 2026 survey of the top five writing skills "reports no measured outcomes" ([Analytics Vidhya](https://www.analyticsvidhya.com/blog/2026/08/top-5-claude-writing-skills/)).

Measuring matters because much of a rules file's gain does not come from its rules. In coding agents, random rules helped as much as expert-curated ones (13.8 points on a discriminative subset of tasks), which the authors attribute to "context priming" ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11088)). A skill never compared with a no-skill baseline cannot tell what its patterns do from what any long prompt does.

Anthropic's guidance says to build evals first: "Create evaluations BEFORE writing extensive documentation", with at least three, each run with and without the skill ([best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/best-practices)). skill-creator tunes descriptions on 20 queries. Half should trigger the skill and half are near misses, which it calls "the most valuable ones". It splits the queries 60/40 into training and held-out sets and runs each one three times ([skill-creator](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/skill-creator/SKILL.md)).

`paper-prose` has no eval of any kind. Yet its triggering is a recorded author decision (Q-2026-10-29-25). And every branch depends on agents opening between two and eleven pattern files, which is exactly the on-demand loading that cost better-writing 0.15.

The eval must avoid one trap. **LLM judges are close to chance on writing quality.** On a benchmark of 4,729 writing-quality judgments, "most competitive baselines, including state-of-the-art LLMs" did little better than random, while a reward model trained on expert edits reached 74% ([Chakrabarty, Laban & Wu](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07532v3)). Judges rank fluent answers that contain factual errors above short or ungrammatical ones ([Wu & Aji](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03025v3)). They must be validated against human labels for each task before use ([Judge-Bench](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-short.20)).

The credible eval for this skill therefore measures violations, not quality, and it has five parts. It starts with **a labeled fixture of sentences**, scored for recall and precision on each pattern. It adds **two counts that should be zero**: V16 failures, and sentences changed outside an edit's target. The second is the over-correction measure from grammar-correction research, where GPT models "make extraneous fluency edits" ([Coyne et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.14342)). A **three-arm ablation** then compares the skill, no skill and greps alone in violations per 1,000 words. For quality it relies on **the author**. A blinded A/B test needs about 29 pairs to detect a true 75% preference and about 85 to detect 65% (this report's arithmetic: a normal approximation to the sign test, two-sided α = 0.05, power 0.8), so 30 pairs can show only a large effect. The long-run signal that no judge can game is **the author's own corrections** per 1,000 words on each locked layer, tagged by pattern id.

The skill also breaks Anthropic's skill-hygiene rules in small ways. Its description is **1,060 characters, over the 1,024-character maximum**. The spec states that limit and skill-creator's validator enforces it ([best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/best-practices); [quick_validate.py](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/skill-creator/scripts/quick_validate.py)). Claude Code accepted the description, so the overrun is silent today, but the skill would fail validation on other platforms.

Length also costs triggering. When the skill listing exceeds Claude Code's character budget, it "drops descriptions starting with the skills you invoke least" ([Claude Code skills](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/skills)). This workspace lists dozens of skills, so a long description is a real risk.

The description also summarizes the workflow ("It routes each task ... and ends with a self-check"). obra's meta-skill warns that an agent may then follow the summary instead of reading the skill, on the evidence of one case ([obra discovery](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/blob/main/skills/writing-skills/references/discovery.md)). Anthropic asks for "what the Skill does and when to use it". A description of triggers and scope exclusions satisfies both.

Dated state is the next problem. Branch F says "On 29 Oct 2026 the file does not exist", V14 repeats it, and the docs say "Don't include information that will become outdated" ([best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/best-practices)).

Last, the skill relies on coordinators to name it in every drafting prompt, because subagents do not see skills invoked in the main session. Claude Code can instead preload it: a subagent's `skills` field injects "The full content of each listed skill" into the subagent's context at startup, though "Built-in agents don't preload skills" ([Claude Code sub-agents](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents)). The repo has no `.claude/agents/` directory yet.

## 4. Economics guides work above the sentence, where the skill is silent

Mapped one by one, the economics and finance guides give 52 operational rules. **Our patterns cover 13, cover 14 in part, miss 22 and conflict with 3** (the research notes' classification). The skill took the guides' phrase bans: AP-S5 ("It should be noted that", "In other words") and AP-V3 ("utilize", adjectives on one's own results). It also took their sentence rules, such as V1's subject-verb-object order. What it lacks sits where a JFEc referee reads first: the exhibits, the summary sections and the economic reading of a result.

**Exhibits are the largest gap.** Cochrane holds that "No number should appear in a table that is not discussed in the text ... 'Table 5 shows summary statistics' (period) is not ok" ([Cochrane, p. 6](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e6033a4ea02d801f37e15bb/t/5eda74919c44fa5f87452697/1591374993570/phd_paper_writing.pdf)). McCloskey's rule 15 asks that tables, graphs and labels be "Readable by Themselves" ([McCloskey, table of contents](https://books.google.com/books/about/Economical_Writing_Third_Edition.html?id=j9SWDwAAQBAJ)).

Cochrane's rule is V2 turned around. V2 makes every number in the text come from the results; Cochrane makes every number in the results reach the text. Our skill does not ask for the second half. V10 treats "Table~\ref{tab:rh_ablation} reports the removals" as the expected JFEc form, with no finding required. Branch C, for captions and table notes, never sends the agent to the notation guide's §8 on tables. This paper is built from ablation tables, so a rule that every `\ref{tab:…}` site carries a sentence stating a finding with a macro is cheap to check and directly useful.

**The economic content of a claim is the second gap.** Cochrane asks writers to "Explain the economic magnitude of the central numbers, not just their statistical significance". He also asks them to "Give the stylized facts in the data that drive your result, not just estimates and p values" ([Cochrane, p. 10](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e6033a4ea02d801f37e15bb/t/5eda74919c44fa5f87452697/1591374993570/phd_paper_writing.pdf)). Goldsmith-Pinkham converts coefficients "into dollar terms when discussing economic significance" ([Goldsmith-Pinkham](https://paulgp.substack.com/p/writing-and-thinking-with-ai-assistance)). V4 and the notation guide keep "significant" apart from "economically large", but nothing asks for the economic reading.

In this paper that reading takes a direct form. A headline loss reduction would sit next to the straddle strategy's return or Sharpe ratio, given as a macro. The loss differences behind each Diebold–Mariano test would appear in an exhibit the text discusses.

Harvey, who handled about 7,500 submissions as editor of the Journal of Finance, wants empirical papers to "convince the reader that there has been minimal data mining" ([Harvey, p. 8](https://faculty.fuqua.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Working_Papers/W111_Reflections_on_editing.pdf)). The repo's pre-registered `RULE_*.txt` files make that sentence true and checkable, yet no pattern asks for it.

**The summary sections are the third gap.** The skill has no branch for an abstract, introduction or conclusion, and the guides disagree about all three. Cochrane puts the contribution first, rejects throat-clearing openers, calls a roadmap optional, and wants a short conclusion that does not restate the findings ([Cochrane, pp. 1–4](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e6033a4ea02d801f37e15bb/t/5eda74919c44fa5f87452697/1591374993570/phd_paper_writing.pdf)). Head's formula for the introduction runs hook, question, antecedents, "approximately 3 contributions" and roadmap ([Head](https://blogs.ubc.ca/khead/research/research-advice/formula)). Shapiro's reaches the paragraph that presents "This Paper" only at paragraph 5 ([Shapiro, p. 3](https://ignaciomsarmiento.github.io/teaching/seminar/foursteps.pdf)). Bellemare's conclusion adds limitations and implications ([Bellemare](https://marcfbellemare.com/wordpress/12060)).

Where the guides agree, a pattern can follow them. The abstract says "what you find, not what you look for" and cites no literature ([Cochrane, p. 1](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e6033a4ea02d801f37e15bb/t/5eda74919c44fa5f87452697/1591374993570/phd_paper_writing.pdf)), within JFEc's limit of 100 words ([JFEc General Instructions](https://academic.oup.com/jfec/pages/General_Instructions)). The introduction is "a contract between you and the reader" and claims only what the paper delivers ([Shapiro, p. 2](https://ignaciomsarmiento.github.io/teaching/seminar/foursteps.pdf)). Contributions are stated against the two or three closest papers, without slights to them ([Head](https://blogs.ubc.ca/khead/research/research-advice/formula); [Stulz](https://www.wu.ac.at/fileadmin/wu/d/i/ifr/Stulz-Tips_for_authors.pdf)). The abstract and introduction are "pitched to the general reader, not a specialist" ([Harvey, p. 7](https://faculty.fuqua.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Working_Papers/W111_Reflections_on_editing.pdf)).

The order of sections and the roadmap are the author's choices. They belong in the deliberate queue, not in a pattern. Head's hook needs a constraint before an LLM drafts it, because "Y matters" and "Y is big" are the importance assertions AP-S10 removes. A first sentence that states the contribution, or a fact with a number, meets both Head's formula and AP-S10.

Smaller rules are cheap greps. From Cochrane: "Previews and recalls are a good sign of poor organization"; "'It is easy to show that' means that it isn't"; "Clothe the naked 'this'"; use one direction for signs instead of "goes up (down)"; and strike "I leave x for future research" and initials for authors ("FF show that") ([Cochrane, pp. 5–9](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e6033a4ea02d801f37e15bb/t/5eda74919c44fa5f87452697/1591374993570/phd_paper_writing.pdf)). From Thomson: "Do not start a sentence with a piece of mathematical notation" ([Thomson, p. 180](https://static.uni-graz.at/fileadmin/_Persoenliche_Webseite/wendner_ronald/write_a_thesis/Thomson.pdf)). Each fits one AP-S row. A rough grep of `writeup/rehaul/*.tex` on 29 Oct 2026 found none except one naked "This is" (`A_econ.tex:433`), so these rules would mainly stop regressions.

Thomson's naming rule also extends V3 usefully: "Refer to a given concept by only one name or phrase", and give the literature's other terms once, "in parentheses next to your definition" ([Thomson, p. 167](https://static.uni-graz.at/fileadmin/_Persoenliche_Webseite/wendner_ronald/write_a_thesis/Thomson.pdf)). V3 covers only estimators, sets, samples and tests, so everyday synonyms ("loss", "forecast error", "accuracy") still drift.

Three guide rules conflict with the repo. First, Cochrane and Thomson prefer the present tense even for prior work ([Cochrane, p. 7](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e6033a4ea02d801f37e15bb/t/5eda74919c44fa5f87452697/1591374993570/phd_paper_writing.pdf); [Thomson, p. 180](https://static.uni-graz.at/fileadmin/_Persoenliche_Webseite/wendner_ronald/write_a_thesis/Thomson.pdf)). The notation guide's §6 asks for the past tense "for what others found". Second, Thomson's "Never use abbreviations in a section heading" ([Thomson, p. 161](https://static.uni-graz.at/fileadmin/_Persoenliche_Webseite/wendner_ronald/write_a_thesis/Thomson.pdf)) catches V9's own example, `\subsubsection{FOMC calendar}`. The objection is weak for a standard acronym like FOMC and fair for one the paper coins. Third, the AER bans significance stars ([AER](https://www.aeaweb.org/journals/aer/style-guide)), but its rules do not bind a JFEc paper.

V1's evidence line needs a fix of its own. It cites "Goldsmith-Pinkham's style guide" as part of its basis. But his own habits are to "use subordinate clauses" and "a lot of em-dashes" ([Goldsmith-Pinkham](https://paulgp.substack.com/p/writing-and-thinking-with-ai-assistance)), the opposite of V1 and AP-S1. What carries over is his practice ("Ask for comments, not rewrites"), not his rules.

All of these guide rules are prescriptive. The evidence that writing matters is general. Economists rated language-edited PhD papers higher in quality and more likely to be accepted, without seeing both versions of any paper ([Feld, Lines & Ross 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jeborg/v217y2024icp378-397.html)). Readable abstracts and introductions correlate with citations ([Dowling, Hammami & Zreik](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecolet/v173y2018icp100-103.html); [McCannon](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecolet/v180y2019icp76-79.html)), but other factors confound these correlations. One old experiment even found faculty rating harder-to-read versions as more competent ([Armstrong 1980](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:orinte:v:10:y:1980:i:2:p:80-86)). No study tests any single economics-writing rule. The guide rules therefore enter the catalog at grade P. The corpus should decide matters of register (tense, roadmaps); the guides should decide throat-clearing.

## 5. General guides supply the information order V1 gets backwards

Gopen and Swan's seven "reader expectation" principles give the skill the account of sentence structure it lacks. Four of them matter here. A writer should "Follow a grammatical subject as soon as possible with its verb" and "Place in the stress position the 'new information' you want the reader to emphasize", the stress position being the end of the sentence. Old, linking information belongs in the topic position at the start, and the writer should "provide context for your reader before asking that reader to consider anything new" ([Gopen & Swan](https://wstomv.win.tue.nl/quotes/science-of-scientific-writing.html)). The authors call these expectations, not rules, and cite no experiments. But old-before-new has measured support: adults understand sentence pairs faster when the given information comes before the new ([review citing Haviland & Clark 1974](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4266472)).

V1 agrees with the first principle and contradicts the others. Its structure line, "Subject and main verb early; scope, conditions and tests after them", pushes an old frame such as a day type into the stress position, where readers put their emphasis. V1's own example does this: "The FOMC calendar carries the gain on statement days." V10's rewrite already uses the reader-expectation order: "On statement days the FOMC calendar lowers the loss by …". V7's first rewrite has a worse fault. Its gerund subject runs about 13 words after a 7-word opener, so the reader holds some 20 words before learning what happens.

The same principles explain rules the skill states separately. A clipped tail such as ", none significantly" sits in the stress position. That is why V6's tails "punch": they give an unbacked verdict the sentence's strongest emphasis.

Pullum's critique of Strunk and White shows that the choice of passive voice is also a question of information order: "a passive is always a stylistic train wreck when the subject refers to something newer and less established in the discourse than the agent" ([Pullum 2009](https://pullum.ppls.ed.ac.uk/50years.pdf)). That yields a voice criterion for each sentence: use the passive when it puts old information first, and the active when the agent is old. The criterion can be checked now, and it pushes the passive share neither up nor down. It is a better answer than the notation guide's blanket "'We' and active voice" (§6). That rule pushes LLM text further in the direction it already drifts: GPT-4o used agentless passives at about half the human rate ([Reinhart et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107v2)).

Williams turns these principles into revision moves, and one transfers cleanly. Nominalizations that hide a sentence's action slow reading. International scientists read nominal style more slowly than active style, with similar comprehension (abstract summary; [Bostian & Thering 1987](https://doi.org/10.2190/Q4YK-UMCU-D6BW-3AGE)). In an eye-tracking study, full-argument nominalizations caused longer fixations and more regressions ([Venagli et al. 2025](https://tmalsburg.github.io/ComprehensionWorkshop/abstracts/VenagliEtAl2025)). LLM text uses about 2.1 times the human rate of nominalizations ([Reinhart et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107v2)).

Expert academic prose, though, is compressed into noun phrases by design. That style is "efficient for expert readers, who can quickly extract large amounts of information" ([Biber & Gray 2010](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/challenging-stereotypes-about-academic-writing-complexity-elabora/)), so converting every nominalization back into a verb would fight the register. A rule limited to light-verb constructions satisfies all three findings. It targets phrases such as "perform an estimation of", "provide an improvement in" and "achieve a reduction". It keeps nominalizations that name a defined object, such as QLIKE loss or the evaluation span.

Pinker and the reading research confirm three existing rules and correct one. Pinker's view that a good writer's "hedging is a choice, not a tic" is V7, made checkable ([Pinker](https://davidlabaree.com/2021/07/08/pinker-why-academics-stink-at-writing/)). V7 also guards against the opposite failure, LLM over-certainty, which no classic guide addresses. Pullum calls "Omit needless words" useless, because "The students who know which words are needless don't need the instruction" ([Pullum 2009](https://pullum.ppls.ed.ac.uk/50years.pdf)). V5 answers him: its NEEDED? test names three reasons to keep a word and logs a reason code for each deletion.

The correction concerns signposting. Pinker calls metadiscourse "verbiage about verbiage". But structural signals improve recall when a text's topic structure is complex, and do nothing when it is simple ([Lorch 1989](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/BF01320135); [Lorch & Lorch](https://scholars.uky.edu/es/publications/effects-of-organizational-signals-on-free-recall-of-expository-te/)). A ten-section results paper is complex. AP-S5 should therefore state that a section roadmap and a pointer to an exhibit are not signposting in its sense.

Pinker's curse of knowledge (forgetting what the reader does not know) adds two cheap checks. Jargon lowered processing fluency even when definitions were supplied ([Bullock et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31354058/)). The checklist should therefore require each symbol, acronym and named set to be defined at or before its first use, and should prefer a plain term to a coined one. And a fresh-context reader test (section 2) is the only way the author's own agents can find what a reader does not yet know.

## 6. Borrow in this order: hygiene, scripts, evals, then patterns

The order follows cost and dependency. **Hygiene fixes** take hours and remove known defects. **Scripts** come before new patterns, because every rule a script checks is one the drafter no longer has to remember, which is what the evidence on rule load asks for, while every new pattern adds reading to each branch that lists it. **Evals** come before new patterns for the same reason: the fixture is what shows whether a pattern fires and whether drafters read it. A new pattern should enter the catalog only with a Detection section a script can run, or a fixture showing that the critic catches it. ## 7. Avoid what the popular skills sell

The most popular writing skills sell two things this paper must not buy.

**The first is detector evasion.** One published skill sends the user's text to an unauthenticated third-party API so that it will "pass AI detection tools like GPTZero, ZeroGPT, Turnitin" ([diaiq](https://github.com/diaiq/claude-skill-humanizer/blob/main/SKILL.md)). For us, that would mean sending an unpublished manuscript to an unknown service. The trade press described humanizer itself as a plugin that "tells the model to avoid the traits that detectors use" ([OSFY](https://www.opensourceforu.com/?p=96392)). A later humanizer release stated that getting past detectors is not a goal ([humanizer CHANGELOG](https://github.com/blader/humanizer/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)).

The evidence makes evasion both pointless and harmful. **Detectors are unreliable**: seven of them flagged essays by non-native English writers as AI-written at an average false-positive rate of 61.3%, because their scores track perplexity, not authorship ([Liang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.02819v3)). **Experts are not fooled**: expert readers kept detecting 100% of "humanized" text ([Russell, Karpinska & Iyyer](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15654v2)). **Surface edits fail**: Sant'Anna reports an article polished through "several rounds of surface-level de-AI-ing" that still scored "100 % AI-written" ([Sant'Anna writing-with-ai](https://github.com/pedrohcgs/claude-code-my-workflow/blob/main/.claude/rules/writing-with-ai.md)). And **concealment costs more than disclosure**: OUP requires disclosure for JFEc, and being caught in undisclosed use costs more trust than disclosing it ([Schilke & Reimann](https://news.arizona.edu/news/disclosing-ai-use-can-backfire-research-shows)). The skill should keep stating that it edits for register and information, not to avoid detection.

**The second is global style targets.** humanizer tells the model to "Vary sentence length; real writing alternates short and long" ([humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer/blob/main/SKILL.md)). stop-slop says "Vary rhythm" and "Two items beat three". It also has the model score its own draft out of 50, with "Authenticity: Sounds human?" as one criterion, and revise below 35 ([stop-slop](https://github.com/hardikpandya/stop-slop/blob/main/SKILL.md)).

The evidence runs against such targets. Positive style directives were the harmful rules in the rules-file study ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11088)). Global "correct" or "improve" prompts produce extra edits nobody asked for ([Coyne et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.14342)). AI suggestions pull writers toward a dominant style ([Agarwal, Naaman & Vashistha](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11360v3)). And across about 2.1 million preprints, complex prose in LLM-assisted papers was *less* likely to be accepted, the reverse of the pattern for human-written papers ([Kusumegi et al.](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/12/251224032347.htm)). V12 already bans these targets. The comparison adds reasons to keep the ban and none to relax it.

Two lessons about how rules are worded apply to our own text.

**State the forbidden form and its rewrite, then grep.** The evidence on wording looks contradictory. Rules phrased as constraints helped coding agents ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11088)), while negated instructions ("do not …") are followed less reliably at the token level ([Jang et al.](https://mlanthology.org/neuripsw/2022/jang2022neuripsw-large)). The form that fits both results names the forbidden construction, gives the rewrite, and leaves enforcement to a grep. Our Instead-of / Write tables already take this form, and B6 adds the grep.

**Make exemptions visible to scripts.** obra's meta-skill reports that "'Don't X unless it matters' reopens the negotiation" and that "Exemption clauses don't scope" ([obra writing-skills](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/blob/main/skills/writing-skills/SKILL.md)). Several of our lines take that form, such as "outside the conclusion where a plain form exists". obra's evidence is unpublished, but the remedy fits B6 and B9 anyway. Exemptions should rest on provenance a script can see (author spans, lines pulled from Overleaf, whitelisted terms), not on clauses a drafter must weigh.

## Conclusion

The comparison reverses the usual worry about a house style guide. The parts of `paper-prose` that look idiosyncratic are numbers only from macros, verdict words from predicates, Rule NT and the gate on author text. They are the parts no one else has, and the parts a JFEc referee would punish if they failed. The parts that look like everyone else's, the AI-tell lists, are where the skill is merely competent, and where it should borrow what others do well: whitelists, rate-based flags and tests for false positives. The catalog does not need more generic rules. It needs its distinctive rules made checkable and measured. A rule that no script checks and no fixture tests is one the drafter will drop silently, somewhere in the sixth thousand words of its instructions.

The guides add a second shift: the skill works at too small a scale. It governs sentences, beats and titles, but a referee reads the abstract, the introduction and the tables first. Cochrane's most useful rule for this paper, that every number in a table reaches the text, has no pattern yet. The next patterns belong at the level of exhibits and summary sections. Each should be built in the order that lets it prove it fires: a script, then a test, then a fixture, then the rule. The one quality signal no judge can game is the author's own correction rate on each locked layer, and it should become the skill's scoreboard.
