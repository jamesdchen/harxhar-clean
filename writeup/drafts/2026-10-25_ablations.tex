\subsection{Which predictors carry the gain?}
\label{sec:rh_results_ablation}

The comparison of Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos} sets the full predictor set against the HAR benchmark.  Two questions remain:
whether the gain comes from data beyond the futures contract itself, and whether a richer volatility model built from
the same one-minute E-mini returns would close the gap.  We answer both with the design of Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}
unchanged---the same rows, window, re-estimation at every bar, scored bars and test---and only the columns changed.

\paragraph{Design.}  The 46 exogenous series form the eight groups of Table~\ref{tab:rh_dm_dictionary}.  For each group
$g$ we fit two models in every class: the full set without $g$ (all of $g$'s trailing averages $u^{(k)}$ and
availability shares $\bar a^{(k)}$ removed) and the HAR benchmark with $g$ added.  The first measures what $g$
contributes given everything else; the second what it contributes on its own.  Hyperparameters stay those of the full
set: the linear penalties chosen on the 2013 pre-sample fold for the full predictor set (with the HAR block
penalized at one hundredth of the rate of the exogenous columns), and the single frozen configuration of each tree
method.  The random forest, the most expensive class, is fitted for the eight additions and for the removal of the
realized moments.  Panel~D of Table~\ref{tab:rh_ablation} restates the full-set row of Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}.  The
design is the final one, with the corrected FOMC calendar; the trees' removals of the groups other than the FOMC
calendar were fitted before that correction and are compared with the full set fitted on the same uncorrected design
(marked in the table).

\paragraph{Augmented HAR benchmarks.}  The first group, the futures' own realized moments, is the natural
``augmented HAR'': it adds the return, absolute variation, skewness, quarticity, positive semivariance, bipower
variation and autocovariance of the same one-minute E-mini returns that define the target.  We also fit the three
classical extensions of the HAR model by OLS, each built from the same one-minute data and passed through the same
diurnal adjustment, variance-stabilizing transform and trailing averages as every other series: the semivariance HAR
of \citet{PattonSheppard2015}, which adds the positive and negative realized semivariances $\mathrm{RS}^{\pm}$ of the
last half hour; the HARQ model of \citet{BollerslevPattonQuaedvlieg2016}, which adds the interaction of the last
half hour's realized variance with the square root of its realized quarticity $\mathrm{RQ} = (N/3)\sum_k r^4_{t,i,k}$
(in the volatility units of the design, $\sqrt{\RV\,\mathrm{RQ}^{1/2}}$); and the leverage HAR of
\citet{CorsiReno2012}, which adds the negative part of the half-hour return, $\min(r_{t,i}, 0)$, at every HAR horizon.
A fourth model adds all three.  The negative semivariance is $\mathrm{RS}^{-} = \RV - \mathrm{RS}^{+}$, exact on every
bar of the data.

\paragraph{Late-starting series.}  The implied-volatility, sentiment and volatility-demand series start after the
data, and a series enters as zero until the rolling window holds 3,000 trading days of its history
(Section~\ref{sec:rh_predictors}).  In the evaluation sample this rule binds for most of the span: the VIX enters on
\rhabLiveVixFromKone{} (its longest average on \rhabLiveVixFromKlast{}), VIX3M on \rhabLiveVixthreemFromKone{},
StockTwits attention on \rhabLiveAttnFromKone{} and the other StockTwits series on \rhabLiveSentFromKone{}, the
volatility-demand series on \rhabLiveVdSpxOcFromKone{} (their feed stops on \rhabLiveVdSpxOcLast{}),
and VVIX on \rhabLiveVvixFromKone{}.  Before those dates the groups contribute only their availability shares, so
the ablation rows of these groups measure the series over the later part of the sample only.

\paragraph{The gain survives the augmented HAR.}  Panel~D of Table~\ref{tab:rh_ablation} sets each class's full set
against its own HAR benchmark plus the futures' realized moments.  Over every bar the full set keeps a lower QLIKE in
all \rhabNClassesBeatAddRm{} classes: by \rhabLgbmFullVsAddRmAllPct\% for LightGBM ($\DM = \rhabLgbmFullVsAddRmAllDm$),
\rhabXgbFullVsAddRmAllPct\% for XGBoost and \rhabRfFullVsAddRmAllPct\% for the random forest, and by
\rhabRidgeFullVsAddRmAllPct\% to \rhabLassoFullVsAddRmAllPct\% for the penalized linear models
($\DM = \rhabRidgeFullVsAddRmAllDm$ to $\rhabLassoFullVsAddRmAllDm$).  It also beats the HAR benchmark plus the FOMC
calendar, the strongest single addition for the trees (Panel~B): by \rhabLgbmFullVsAddFomcAllPct\% for LightGBM
($\DM = \rhabLgbmFullVsAddFomcAllDm$), \rhabXgbFullVsAddFomcAllPct\% for XGBoost and \rhabRfFullVsAddFomcAllPct\% for the
forest ($\DM = \rhabRfFullVsAddFomcAllDm$), and by \rhabRidgeFullVsAddFomcAllPct\% to \rhabEnetFullVsAddFomcAllPct\% for
the linear models ($\DM = \rhabLassoFullVsAddFomcAllDm$ to $\rhabEnetFullVsAddFomcAllDm$).  The classical extensions estimated by OLS gain little
over the HAR benchmark: \rhabOlsSharAllPct\% for SHAR, \rhabOlsHarqAllPct\% for HARQ, \rhabOlsLharAllPct\% for LHAR and
\rhabOlsSharqlAllPct\% for the three together (Panel~C); every class's full set beats the last of these
($\DM = \rhabLgbmFullVsOlsSharqlAllDm$ for LightGBM, $\rhabEnetFullVsOlsSharqlAllDm$ for the elastic net).  At the closing
half hour the picture differs: the trees' full sets lose to their own HAR plus realized moments
(\rhabLgbmFullVsAddRmClosePct\% for LightGBM, $\DM = \rhabLgbmFullVsAddRmCloseDm$; \rhabXgbFullVsAddRmClosePct\% for
XGBoost and \rhabRfFullVsAddRmClosePct\% for the forest, $\DM = \rhabXgbFullVsAddRmCloseDm$ and
$\rhabRfFullVsAddRmCloseDm$), and the linear full sets lose more (\rhabEnetFullVsAddRmClosePct\% to
\rhabLassoFullVsAddRmClosePct\%).

\paragraph{Two groups carry it.}  Removing a group from the full set (Panel~A) costs little except for two groups: the
FOMC calendar (\rhabLgbmLoFomcAllPct\% for LightGBM, \rhabXgbLoFomcAllPct\% for XGBoost, \rhabRidgeLoFomcAllPct\% to
\rhabLassoLoFomcAllPct\% for the linear models) and the futures' realized moments (\rhabLgbmLoRmAllPct\% for LightGBM,
$\DM = \rhabLgbmLoRmAllDm$; \rhabRfLoRmAllPct\% for the forest, $\DM = \rhabRfLoRmAllDm$; \rhabRidgeLoRmAllPct\% for ridge).
No other group's removal changes QLIKE by more than a few tenths of a percent.  Added to the HAR benchmark on its own
(Panel~B), the FOMC calendar gives most of the trees' gain (\rhabLgbmAddFomcAllPct\% for LightGBM against
\rhabLgbmFullAllPct\% for the full set), the realized moments about \rhabXgbAddRmAllPct\% (XGBoost), and the liquidity
and constituent-market groups gains of the same order (\rhabXgbAddVwAllPct\% for the value-weighted market) that
disappear once the rest of the set is present: their information overlaps with the futures' moments.

\paragraph{Against HAR, realized moments and the FOMC calendar together.}  At the author's request after the results
by day type, and so outside the pre-specified design, each class was also fitted on the HAR benchmark set plus both
the futures' realized moments and the FOMC calendar (132 columns, the same frozen hyperparameters; the row marked * in
Panel~D).  This model takes almost all of the full set's gain.  Over every bar the other 36 series improve on it
by \rhabLgbmFullVsAddRmFomcAllPct\% for LightGBM ($\DM = \rhabLgbmFullVsAddRmFomcAllDm$) and
\rhabXgbFullVsAddRmFomcAllPct\% for XGBoost ($\DM = \rhabXgbFullVsAddRmFomcAllDm$), by \rhabRfFullVsAddRmFomcAllPct\%
for the forest ($p = \rhabRfFullVsAddRmFomcAllP$), and not at all for the penalized linear models
(\rhabRidgeFullVsAddRmFomcAllPct\% for ridge to \rhabEnetFullVsAddRmFomcAllPct\% for the elastic net, $p \geq
\rhabEnetFullVsAddRmFomcAllP$).  The trees' remainder falls on ordinary days (\rhabLgbmFullVsAddRmFomcOrdPct\% for
LightGBM, $\DM = \rhabLgbmFullVsAddRmFomcOrdDm$; \rhabRfFullVsAddRmFomcOrdPct\% for the forest, $p =
\rhabRfFullVsAddRmFomcOrdP$); on the \rhabNEventDays{} scheduled statement days no class's full set beats it except
the lasso's (\rhabLassoFullVsAddRmFomcEvPct\%, $\DM = \rhabLassoFullVsAddRmFomcEvDm$).  Against the HAR benchmark the
same model lowers QLIKE by \rhabRidgeAddRmFomcVsHarAllPct\% (ridge) to \rhabXgbAddRmFomcVsHarAllPct\% (XGBoost); fitted
by OLS it lowers it by \rhabOlsAddRmFomcVsHarAllPct\% ($\DM = \rhabOlsAddRmFomcVsHarAllDm$), \rhabOlsAddRmFomcVsHarEvPct\%
on statement days and an insignificant \rhabOlsAddRmFomcVsHarOrdPct\% on ordinary days ($p = \rhabOlsAddRmFomcVsHarOrdP$).

\paragraph{Alternative data on its live window.}  Panel~E scores the late-starting groups only on the bars on which
their value averages are live.  There the gains are small: about one percent for the trees from the VIX
(\rhabXgbAddIvLiveIvPct\% for XGBoost, $\DM = \rhabXgbAddIvLiveIvDm$, from \rhabLiveIvStart), nothing measurable from
StockTwits (from \rhabLiveSentStart), and losses for the linear models from volatility demand
(\rhabRidgeAddVdLiveVdPct\% for ridge between \rhabLiveVdStart{} and \rhabLiveVdEnd).  The full-sample gains of these
groups in Panel~B therefore come mainly from their availability shares, not from the series' values.
