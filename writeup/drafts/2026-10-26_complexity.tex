\subsection{Window length, number of series and the exogenous gain (draft)}
\label{sec:rh_cx}
\editc{orange}{CX}{Draft for the author (2026-10-26): keep framing A, framing B or neither; the two items below
describe the same runs. Pre-patch design (the FOMC calendar features before the fix), monthly refits; to be rerun on the
patched design if kept.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Question.} Does the gain from the exogenous series grow with the number of series a model sees and with
  the length of its training window, and does the number of series worth keeping grow with the window? If the
  exogenous signal were dense and weak, more data per parameter would let more of the weak series pay for themselves,
  so the best number of series would rise with the window.
\item \textbf{What is computed.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item A grid of rolling windows $W = \rhcxWOne$, \rhcxWTwo, \rhcxWMain{} and \rhcxWLong{} trading days ($T = 13W$,
    from \rhcxTOne{} to \rhcxTLong{} bars) and of rungs $k = 0, 1, 3, 5, 10, 20, \rhcxNSeries$: each rung keeps the
    \rhcxPHar{} columns of the HAR benchmark set and all columns of the top $k$ exogenous series, so it has
    $P = 12 + 12k$ predictors (\rhcxPFull{} for the full set) and complexity $c = P/T$.
  \item The series are ranked separately at each window and for each model, by one fit before the evaluation span
    (origin 2 January 2013, explained on 2013: mean absolute SHAP value for the trees, $\sum |\beta|\,\mathrm{s.d.}$
    for the linear models). This is the first-training-window ranking of \citet{MaischZhangUlrichZimmer2026}.
  \item LightGBM and XGBoost on their frozen configurations at every window (minimum leaf sizes not rescaled with
    $T$); ridge, lasso and elastic net on each window's own frozen penalty (ridge \rhcxPenRidgeOne{} at $W =
    \rhcxWOne$, \rhcxPenRidgeMain{} at the other windows; lasso \rhcxPenLassoOne{} and \rhcxPenLassoMain; elastic net
    \rhcxPenEnetOne{} and \rhcxPenEnetMain).
  \item Each rung is refitted at the \rhcxNOrigins{} monthly origins of Section~\ref{sec:rh_fi} and held for
    the next 21 sessions. The full rung equals the every-bar walk at every origin (\rhcxNGatesPass{} of \rhcxNGates{}
    window--model gates).
  \item Gain $G(W, k)$: the QLIKE of the same window's $k = 0$ rung minus the rung's, $\times 10^3$, on the
    \rhcxNBars{} scored bars of Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos} and on the 16:00 bar; Diebold--Mariano tests against $k = 0$
    (HLN, Newey--West lag \rhcxHacLag). Figures~\ref{fig:rh_cx_complexity} and~\ref{fig:rh_cx_scaling}.
  \end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Framing A: complexity in the sense of \citet{KellyMalamudZhou2024}.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Their claim: in return prediction, a ridge regression on many random nonlinear features beats a simple model
    out of sample, and keeps improving as complexity $c = P/T$ grows, beyond $c = 1$ toward the ridgeless limit. Our
    grid covers only the low end of that axis: $c$ runs from \rhcxCMin{} to \rhcxCMax{} (\rhcxCMaxMain{} for the
    full set at $W = \rhcxWMain$).
  \item Within a window (higher $c$ through more series), the trees behave as the complexity view predicts. Their
    gain rises with $c$, and the full set is the best rung in \rhcxNFullBestTreesBar{} of 8 window--tree cells on
    every bar (LightGBM \rhcxFullBestLgbmBar{} of 4, XGBoost \rhcxFullBestXgbBar{} of 4). The linear models do not:
    their gain peaks at 5 to 10 series and falls toward the full set, which is the best rung in
    \rhcxNFullBestLinearBar{} of 12 cells (ridge at $W = \rhcxWMain$: \rhcxGainRidgeBarMainTen{} at $k = 10$,
    \rhcxGainRidgeBarMainFull{} for the full set).
  \item Across windows at a fixed $P$ (lower $c$ through a longer window), the gain rises, the opposite of what $c$
    alone would order. For example, LightGBM's full set gains \rhcxGainLgbmBarOneFull{} at $W = \rhcxWOne$ ($c =
    \rhcxCMax$) and \rhcxGainLgbmBarMainFull{} at \rhcxWMain. So $c$ is not a sufficient statistic here: at these
    values the ordinary estimation-error effect of $T$ dominates.
  \item At the 16:00 bar, the trees' full set is the best rung in \rhcxNFullBestTreesClose{} of 8 window--tree cells,
    the linear models' in \rhcxNFullBestLinearClose{} of 12.
  \end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Framing B: data scaling and feature scaling.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Feature scaling (fixed $T$). The trees' gain rises with the number of series and saturates by 10 to 20 series
    (LightGBM at $W = \rhcxWMain$: \rhcxGainLgbmBarMainFive{} at $k = 5$, \rhcxGainLgbmBarMainTen{} at $k = 10$,
    \rhcxGainLgbmBarMainFull{} for the full set). The linear models' gain is hump-shaped in the number of series
    (ridge: \rhcxGainRidgeBarMainFive, \rhcxGainRidgeBarMainTen{} and \rhcxGainRidgeBarMainFull).
  \item Data scaling (fixed $k$). The trees gain most from the first doubling of the window. LightGBM at $k = 10$:
    \rhcxGainLgbmBarOneTen{} at $W = \rhcxWOne$, \rhcxGainLgbmBarTwoTen{} at \rhcxWTwo, \rhcxGainLgbmBarLongTen{} at
    \rhcxWLong. Its full set: \rhcxGainLgbmBarOneFull, \rhcxGainLgbmBarTwoFull, \rhcxGainLgbmBarMainFull,
    \rhcxGainLgbmBarLongFull. The linear models' gain at $k = 10$ also rises with the window (ridge
    \rhcxGainRidgeBarOneTen{} to \rhcxGainRidgeBarLongTen). Their full set does not change monotonically with the
    window (ridge \rhcxGainRidgeBarOneFull, \rhcxGainRidgeBarTwoFull, \rhcxGainRidgeBarMainFull,
    \rhcxGainRidgeBarLongFull).
  \item Does the best number of series rise with the window? On every bar it \rhcxKstarReadXgbBar{} for XGBoost
    ($k^\ast = \rhcxKstarXgbBarOne$, \rhcxKstarXgbBarTwo, \rhcxKstarXgbBarMain, \rhcxKstarXgbBarLong{} at the four
    windows). It \rhcxKstarReadLgbmBar{} for LightGBM (the full set, $k^\ast = \rhcxKstarLgbmBarOne$, at every
    window). It \rhcxKstarReadLassoBar{} for the lasso ($\rhcxKstarLassoBarOne$, \rhcxKstarLassoBarTwo,
    \rhcxKstarLassoBarMain, \rhcxKstarLassoBarLong) and \rhcxKstarReadEnetBar{} for the elastic net
    ($\rhcxKstarEnetBarOne$, \rhcxKstarEnetBarTwo, \rhcxKstarEnetBarMain, \rhcxKstarEnetBarLong). It
    \rhcxKstarReadRidgeBar{} for ridge ($\rhcxKstarRidgeBarOne$, \rhcxKstarRidgeBarTwo, \rhcxKstarRidgeBarMain,
    \rhcxKstarRidgeBarLong).
    \begin{itemize}
    \item At the 16:00 bar, the best number of series rises with the window for \rhcxWhoKstarRisesClose{} of the
      models. It falls for \rhcxWhoKstarFallsClose{} and is not monotone for \rhcxWhoKstarMixedClose.
    \item The dense-and-weak prediction (a best number of series that rises with the window) therefore holds on
      every bar for \rhcxWhoKstarRisesBar{} and at the 16:00 bar for \rhcxWhoKstarRisesClose. For the linear models
      the best number of series is largest at the shortest window, where the tenfold larger penalty favours the full
      set, and smaller after it.
    \end{itemize}
  \end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Caveats.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Our $c$ is far below one (at most \rhcxCMax), while \citet{KellyMalamudZhou2024} study $c > 1$ with ridge on
    random features; the grid has no random-feature expansion and cannot reach their regime.
  \item For the trees the effective complexity is not $P/T$: the depth, the number of leaves and of trees and the
    column sampling set it. The minimum leaf sizes are not rescaled with $T$, so a shorter window also means
    relatively larger leaves.
  \item The window length is confounded with regime averaging: a longer window averages over more regimes. The
    linear penalties also change with the window (tenfold at $W = \rhcxWOne$), and the rankings are refitted at each
    window, so the series behind a given $k$ differ across windows.
  \item Monthly refits held for 21 sessions, not the every-bar walks of Table~\ref{tab:rh_oos}. Holding a fit costs
    the full linear set most.
  \item The design is pre-patch: the FOMC calendar features precede the pending fix, and they are among the
    top-ranked series of the linear models.
  \item Four windows with no intervals: descriptive.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
