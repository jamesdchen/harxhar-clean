\section{Withdrawn Claims}
\label{sec:ledger}

This paper went through three drafts whose central claim we subsequently
withdrew. We publish the record rather than the final draft alone, for two
reasons. The claims were individually plausible, several were statistically
significant at conventional levels, and one survived long enough to have the
paper rewritten around it---so the interesting content is not that they were
wrong but \emph{how} they passed. And a reader is entitled to know the
denominator: fifteen withdrawn claims is the evidence that what remains was
tested rather than merely found.

\subsection{Five Causes, Not Fifteen}

Every entry in Table~\ref{tab:ledger} traces to one of five failures. None is a
modelling error, which is why we now guard against these five specifically
rather than against wrong answers in general.

\paragraph{One factor at a time.} The stripped-down design beat the elaborate
one on the uncorrected panel and lost on the corrected one: a design $\times$
data-version interaction, discovered by accident after two rewrites. The same
error then repeated \emph{inside} the data fix, where median substitution alone
looked harmful, masked estimation alone looked inert, the IQR floor alone
looked inert, and all of the value was in the interaction. Three attempts
failed for one reason. We now run a factorial with the data version as an
explicit factor.

\paragraph{Proxy metrics never validated.} Scaled magnitude in rolling-IQR
units stood in for forecast harm, and was wrong in both directions: it called
\texttt{sumbipow\_ewstock} at $84.6$ IQRs a pathology when the composed fix
takes it to $17.7$, and it called an \texttt{effspread} regression at $932$ a
defect whose QLIKE cost was never measured. No claim denominated in IQRs is a
claim about forecasting. The same error recurred a third time in the scale-
collapse detector, which fired on a region the repaired pipeline neutralises by
design.

\paragraph{Selection contaminating the estimate.} The MIDAS shape parameter was
chosen against the reported out-of-sample loss, worth $+0.0033$; an exponent
sweep reported its argmin as achievable; penalties were inherited across a
dataset change. Selection now runs on a region strictly preceding evaluation,
and oracle arms are reported but labelled.

\paragraph{Numbers quoted across incompatible footings.} QLIKE $0.13280$ on the
uncorrected panel was tabled against $0.13177$ on the corrected one without
labels. Every number in this paper now carries its data version.

\paragraph{An arbitrary nuisance choice fixed at one value and never varied.}
The lag-compression result estimated its projection on the first $24{,}000$
rows of the panel. Nothing recommended those rows over any other $24{,}000$;
they were simply first. Every diagnostic we ran on that arm was internal to
it---refit cadences, seed variants, principal angles, a memory-halflife
sweep---and each was informative about \emph{how} the projection behaved
without ever asking whether a different window would have produced the same
verdict. It would not: repeating the identical procedure on the eight windows
nobody chose puts all eight behind the full ladder in a tight distribution
(mean $+0.00022$, sd $0.00016$), with the published window $13.5$ standard
deviations outside it. The window was a nuisance parameter, and a nuisance
parameter examined at one value is not examined. We now sweep such choices
across their plausible range and read the reported number against that
distribution, in the spirit of specification-curve and multiverse analysis;
where a sweep is infeasible, the arbitrariness is stated rather than left
implicit.

\subsection{What This Implies About the Rest of the Literature}

We make one observation and no accusation. Four of the five causes above are
invisible in a finished paper. A reader cannot tell from a results table
whether a shape parameter was selected on validation data or on the reported
test set; whether a reported ablation varied one factor while a second moved
underneath it; whether the panel a model was fitted on contained a channel that
had stopped publishing; or whether an arbitrary window, seed, or split was the
only one ever tried. Our own answers to those four questions were wrong, and we
had the code.
