\section{An Independent Replication on the State Side}
\label{sec:replication}

Everything to this point was estimated on one panel and one basis, by design
(Section~\ref{sec:ledger} is the cost accounting of that commitment). This
section reports a replication campaign run on a second construction --- a
frozen state-space basis and its dynamics --- with different machinery at every
step. Numbers here are quoted only in this section and are never merged into
the preceding tables; the panel note of the one-panel commitment stands. Every
claim below was pre-registered with a recorded expectation before its run, and
every surviving claim passed a closing Romano--Wolf step-down over its own
family (2{,}000 draws, 1{,}008-bar joint blocks); the campaign retired sixteen
further pre-registered arms at their gates. Where an expectation is reported as
wrong, the direction of the error is part of the record.

\subsection{The frozen frame and its stable objects}
\label{sec:frozen-frame}

Project the $\sim$133 base features onto the eigenvectors of their
first-window (2005--06) correlation and freeze that frame permanently. Three
geometric objects built on this frame replicate out of sample: a factor-pair
interaction map (split-half correlation $+0.62$ against a null of
$0.00 \pm 0.11$), a daily rotor (92\% curl fraction, 35-day period, phase
forecastable roughly one month ahead), and an intraday circulation (split-half
$+0.992$, kill-tested against moving-average arithmetic). A
dependence-adjusted Marchenko--Pastur bound keeps only 5 of 106 directions,
yet wider blocks carry real signal: mid-spectrum directions are
\emph{gauge-degenerate} --- unpinnable as labelled axes while their span is
stable. This resolves a puzzle from the primary basis (why representative
choice never matters) and predicts the central robustness result below.

\subsection{The lead--lag field}
\label{sec:transmission}

Let $G(t)$ be the frame scores and $D$ the antisymmetric part of their
lag-one-bar cross-correlation (trailing 504 days, quarterly re-estimation).
The columns of $\hat G_j(t) = \sum_i D_{ij} G_i(t-1)$ --- one-bar lead--lag
``arrows'' --- are genuine feature content: a raw-lag control (the same
columns without $D$-weighting) fails where the $D$-weighted block passes.

\paragraph{Width.} The frame width was an inherited convention (twenty
factors). Varying it end to end gives the campaign's cleanest structural
result: forecast improvement is \emph{flat on a plateau} of widths
$\sim$30--80 and falls off a cliff on both sides (at width 106 --- every live
direction --- the block fails its gate outright). The arrows live in a
finite-rank sub-span roughly twice the conventional frame; production runs
width 40 as a representative of the plateau, not as a tuned optimum.
Mechanically, the widening gain is carried by the \emph{new directions' own
next-bar motion}, not by new information about the old carriers; doubling the
$D$ estimation window rescues nothing (the tail is empty, not noisy); and the
new arrows replicate split-half at $+0.838$ (old block $+0.907$) --- stable
field, unpinnable labels, as Section~\ref{sec:frozen-frame} predicts.

The one apparent anomaly is itself lawful: at sixteen bars the block passes
only under a per-horizon shrinkage scaling ($\alpha \propto H$), sixteen times
the penalty of its one-bar seat --- dense-weak content pays at long horizons
only when the solver is forced to be humble, the same law the primary basis
found for its own feature classes.

\subsection{The second moment: predictability persists, complexity died}
\label{sec:second-moment}

The forecast-error second moment (the smear term in the QLIKE
reconstruction) is forecastable: daily log-amplitude carries an
out-of-sample $R^2$ near $0.5$ from its own trailing means. Two campaigns of
dictionary expansion establish what that forecastability is worth. First,
ridge over a union dictionary (ten named amplitude functionals plus the full
daily panel snapshot, generalized-cross-validated shrinkage, causal
per-refit standardization and Duan factors) \emph{beats} the named columns
($t = +3.12$, FWER $p = 0.006$) --- the channel was span-deficient, not
sparse, and the deficiency is nameable: calendar structure (day-of-week,
half-day sessions) and slow quarticity, bipower, and liquidity aggregates,
with the named ten carrying under 8\% of the fitted coefficient mass. Second,
that surplus is \emph{era-bounded}: decomposed by period, the union's edge
decays monotonically after 2015 and is dead after 2022, while the amplitude's
own-means $R^2$ is undiminished (2020 posts the sample's best year). The
second moment did not become unpredictable in the zero-days-to-expiry era; it
became \emph{simple} --- means-sufficient --- and every dictionary expansion
beyond the means (panel span, quarticity, regime labels, event decay, a
sequence model's seat) died on the modern regime. The single exception obeys
the obvious mechanism: the calendar fragment, stationary by construction,
survives on 2022--26 ($t = +2.31$ there; FWER $p = 0.007$ in the closing
step-down) and enters the production smear. The conditional smear also fails
to transfer to the end-of-day horizon (session integration averages the
vol-of-vol away) --- the trading-horizon deliverable keeps a trailing
constant, now by measurement rather than by omission.

\subsection{Horizons and the deliverable}
\label{sec:horizons}

The dense edge is fast in horizon (composite-over-backbone $t$: $+5.4$ at two
hours, $+2.8$ at four, $+1.2$ at eight), and at the end-of-day horizon it
concentrates at the open: pooled training across horizons \emph{hides} the
open-decision edge ($-0.2$), while dedicated-horizon training recovers it
($+3.2$ on the 10:00 decision slice). The per-horizon production stack that
falls out of the campaign: at one bar, the full 719-column model (679 primary
columns plus the width-40 transmission block) refit per bar; at intermediate
horizons, the width-20 block with $\alpha \propto H$ scaling; at end-of-day,
neither --- the plain 679 with a constant smear. Composed with its conditional
smear, the one-bar deliverable's QLIKE improved from $0.12579$ to $0.12303$
over the campaign ($\approx -7.2\%$ against the HAR baseline), with the final
step --- the measured width and the calendar smear --- contributed entirely by
arms that a family-wise error correction survived.

\subsection{What this replication is evidence for}
\label{sec:replication-summary}

The campaign closes with a seven-for-seven family sweep --- the study's first
with no casualties --- against sixteen pre-registered arms retired at their
gates along the way. Three of the primary basis's laws re-derive on the state
side with different machinery: density with generous uniform shrinkage beats
selection (the width plateau; the $\alpha \propto H$ revival); the edge is
fast and freshness-loving in the first moment (per-bar transmission entry,
horizon-locality of width) while the second moment obeys neither law (rolling
windows and faster refits fail there); and clever structure survives exactly
where a stable geometric object backs it (arrows replicate; the phase claim,
the flow-state features, and the lagged-quadratic terms do not). The state
side also contributes one law the primary basis could not see: regime change
manifested not as decay of predictability but as \emph{collapse of
complexity} --- a channel whose extra dictionary content dies while its
first-order structure strengthens.
