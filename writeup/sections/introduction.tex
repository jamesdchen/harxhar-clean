\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Realized-volatility forecasting sits in an unusual corner of the prediction landscape. The target is among the most forecastable objects in finance---simple heterogeneous-autoregressive (HAR) models on lagged realized variance explain the large majority of out-of-sample variation \citep{Corsi2009, AndersenBollerslevDieboldLabys2003}---yet the increments of skill beyond that autoregressive core are small, fragile, and easy to manufacture spuriously. Machine-learning comparisons in this literature routinely disagree about whether nonlinear models help at all \citep{christensen2023machine, gu2020empirical}, and we will show that part of that disagreement is mechanical: on our own panel, a hyperparameter search scored on the evaluation set produced an apparent model-class reversal roughly an order of magnitude larger than any effect we can defend under a causal protocol (Section~\ref{sec:tuning_leak}).

This paper is an attempt to make those increments of skill legible. We work on a single fixed panel---218{,}934 thirty-minute bars of S\&P~500 realized variance spanning 2005--2024, with exogenous predictors organized into eight economic buckets---and we hold one benchmark fixed throughout: an ordinary-least-squares HAR model with calendar features, refit every bar, with out-of-sample QLIKE of $0.13415$. Every model is evaluated on the identical row index, so every comparison is bar-for-bar, and every claim of improvement carries a Diebold--Mariano statistic with heteroskedasticity-and-autocorrelation-consistent inference \citep{DieboldMariano1995, HarveyLeybourneNewbold1997}.

\paragraph{The instrument comes first.} Section~\ref{sec:descriptive} argues that the target's own structure determines the forecasting instrument before any exogenous question can be posed, and determines it as a truncated power-law convolution kernel with a session clock. The argument is elementary: the fitted HAR coefficients on rolling means imply a per-lag effective kernel $\varphi(u) = \sum_{k: r_k \ge u} w_k / r_k$, a step function whose breakpoints are the lag rungs. Under this reading HAR is a quadrature rule on a scale-free grid rather than a cascade of economically distinct horizons, and the reading makes predictions the cascade reading does not. Refining the grid should pay: it does, monotonically across six geometric bases, with base-2 beating base-5 by $0.00082$ at DM $t = -10.6$, and the conventional daily/weekly/monthly structure faring worse than either. A power-law kernel should beat an exponential one: it does, by $0.011$. The reading also makes the correspondence to self-exciting point processes \citep{Hawkes1971, BacryMastromatteoMuzy2015} explicit rather than metaphorical, and Section~\ref{sec:kernel_caveats} is equally explicit about the two respects---a kernel with negative lobes, and an exponent steeper than the literature's---in which our estimate does not yet earn structural language.

This ordering has an uncomfortable consequence that we state up front rather than defer to a limitations section. Our shared benchmark uses the coarser base-5 grid, so every increment reported downstream includes $0.00082$ of backbone misspecification as though it were exogenous or nonlinear content. Section~\ref{sec:benchmark_debt} accounts for the debt table by table. It changes no ordering, but it dissolves the smallest effects and removes 28\% of the headline nonlinearity premium.

\paragraph{Where the information lives.} Section~\ref{sec:marginal} measures attribution inside a single model class so that information is never confounded with capacity. Every bucket improves on the backbone, and every improvement is individually decisive, but the gains overlap almost completely: summed within class, the seven singleton buckets give $2.08$ times the joint gain. A Frisch--Waugh--Lovell block decomposition attributes 96\% of explainable variance to the backbone, and a single mechanism-targeted engineered feature outperforms the 359-column raw exogenous block by a wide margin per column---a comparison we report with its arithmetic and its two caveats, because per-feature normalization is easy to abuse.

\paragraph{Whether extraction requires nonlinearity.} Section~\ref{sec:linvsnonlin} crosses five estimators with nine feature sets, all causally tuned, all on the shared panel. Tree ensembles hold a modest edge on every feature set, from $0.00184$ to $0.00358$; the consistent sign across nine sets is the evidence, and we are explicit that the pairwise difference has not been tested directly. The pure-nonlinearity premium on backbone features is $0.00215$ against a correctly specified linear ladder. The edge is not a tuning story from any angle we measured: causally tuned linear arms are worse than the untuned incumbent, and a test-set oracle over penalties is worth at most $0.00015$. What the trees exploit saturates at additive-plus-pairwise structure anchored to the session clock---which is what a quadratic self-exciting feedback \citep{BlancDonierBouchaud2017} predicts, converting a puzzling ceiling into an expected one.

\paragraph{What algorithm should extract it.} Section~\ref{sec:algorithms} freezes the feature set and treats estimation as the design space: exact rank-1 rolling least squares, a recursive elastic net via online homotopy \citep{Garrigues2008}, a causal rolling hyperparameter-selection protocol, and a spectral $k$-nearest-neighbors estimator that embeds trailing residual trajectories with Laplacian eigenmaps \citep{BelkinNiyogi2003}. The section's contribution is its ablations: the manifold-embedding step hurts on all eight matched configurations, and the arm that beats the incumbent is the one with the embedding switched off. What survives is an OLS-anchored, heavily averaged analog search---effective, and not novel.

\paragraph{Commitments.} Three run through the paper. First, one panel, one benchmark: no result is quoted across incompatible evaluation regimes, and superseded experiments contribute no number. Second, significance before narrative: differences without a DM statistic are labeled unresolved, and quantities we have not computed are marked in the text rather than elided. Third, negative results are reported at the same altitude as positive ones---the failures of PCA compression, deep sequence models, hyperparameter tuning, and our own manifold embedding are load-bearing facts about the dense-but-weak regime, not footnotes. Section~\ref{sec:limitations} collects the threats that could change a conclusion, of which the largest are the benchmark debt above, a target winsorization that clips 10\% of observations by construction, and a diurnal adjustment that is itself a forecast.
