\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Realized-volatility forecasting sits in an unusual corner of the prediction landscape. The target is among the most forecastable objects in finance---simple heterogeneous-autoregressive (HAR) models on lagged realized variance explain the large majority of out-of-sample variation \citep{Corsi2009, AndersenBollerslevDieboldLabys2003}---yet the increments of skill beyond that autoregressive core are small, fragile, and easy to manufacture spuriously. Machine-learning comparisons in this literature routinely disagree about whether nonlinear models help at all \citep{christensen2023machine, gu2020empirical}, and we will show that part of that disagreement is mechanical: on our own panel, a hyperparameter search scored on the evaluation set produced an apparent model-class reversal roughly an order of magnitude larger than any effect we can defend under a causal protocol (Section~\ref{sec:tuning_leak}).

This paper is an attempt to make those increments of skill legible. We work on a single fixed panel---218{,}934 thirty-minute bars of S\&P~500 realized variance spanning 2005--2024, with exogenous predictors organized into eight economic buckets---and we hold one benchmark fixed throughout: an ordinary-least-squares HAR model with calendar features, refit every bar, with out-of-sample QLIKE of $0.13415$. Every model is evaluated on the identical row index, so every comparison is bar-for-bar, and every claim of improvement carries a Diebold--Mariano statistic with heteroskedasticity-and-autocorrelation-consistent inference \citep{DieboldMariano1995, HarveyLeybourneNewbold1997}.

\paragraph{The instrument comes first.} Section~\ref{sec:descriptive} argues that the target's own structure determines the forecasting instrument before any exogenous question can be posed, and determines it as a truncated power-law convolution kernel with a session clock. The argument is elementary: the fitted HAR coefficients on rolling means imply a per-lag effective kernel $\varphi(u) = \sum_{k: r_k \ge u} w_k / r_k$, a step function whose breakpoints are the lag rungs. Under this reading HAR is a quadrature rule on a scale-free grid rather than a cascade of economically distinct horizons, and the reading makes predictions the cascade reading does not. Refining the grid should pay: it does, monotonically across six geometric bases, with base-2 beating base-5 by $0.00082$ at DM $t = -10.6$, and the conventional daily/weekly/monthly structure faring worse than either. A power-law kernel should beat an exponential one: it does, by $0.011$ in forecast loss and by $R^2$ $0.80$ against $0.44$ in kernel shape. The reading also makes the correspondence to self-exciting point processes \citep{Hawkes1971, BacryMastromatteoMuzy2015} explicit rather than metaphorical, which means it can be tested---and Section~\ref{sec:kernel_caveats} tests it. The result splits cleanly. The kernel's shape behaves: estimated across 41 channels where the log-log slope is scale-invariant, the decay exponent is $1.033 \pm 0.036$, agreeing with the Hawkes literature and rejecting both the $1.36$ earlier drafts quoted from a plotting constant and the $\approx 1.6$ a rough-volatility correspondence implies. The kernel's sign structure appeared not to: on the estimate this project had been reading, 8 of 12 lags are negative and the integrated kernel is $-0.363$, outside the $[0,1)$ a linear self-exciting model admits. Rebuilding the feature cache reproduces that estimate exactly and then explains it. The archived probe conditions the self-kernel on 41 exogenous channels, several of which are bipower variation, absolute returns and semivariance---volatility measures, and therefore near-substitutes for lagged $RV$. They absorb the target's own persistence, and what remains is a contrast between correlated proxies rather than a kernel. The \emph{marginal} self-kernel on identical rows has integrated mass $0.913$, no negative mass, and a clean power-law decay. The methodological point generalises beyond this paper: a self-kernel estimated alongside volatility proxies is not a self-kernel, and nothing in the fit signals the problem.

This ordering has an uncomfortable consequence that we state up front rather than defer to a limitations section. Our shared benchmark uses the coarser base-5 grid, so every increment reported downstream includes $0.00082$ of backbone misspecification as though it were exogenous or nonlinear content. Section~\ref{sec:benchmark_debt} accounts for the debt table by table. It changes no ordering, but it dissolves the smallest effects and removes 28\% of the headline nonlinearity premium.

\paragraph{Where the information lives.} Section~\ref{sec:marginal} measures attribution inside a single model class so that information is never confounded with capacity. Every bucket improves on the backbone, and every improvement is individually decisive, but the gains overlap almost completely: summed within class, the seven singleton buckets give $2.08$ times the joint gain. A Frisch--Waugh--Lovell block decomposition attributes 96\% of explainable variance to the backbone, and a single mechanism-targeted engineered feature outperforms the 359-column raw exogenous block by a wide margin per column---a comparison we report with its arithmetic and its two caveats, because per-feature normalization is easy to abuse.

\paragraph{Whether extraction requires nonlinearity.} Section~\ref{sec:linvsnonlin} crosses five estimators with nine feature sets, all causally tuned, all on the shared panel. Tree ensembles hold a modest edge on every feature set, from $0.00184$ to $0.00358$, and Section~\ref{sec:pairwise} tests those differences directly: because the shared incumbent cancels from any pairwise differential, a bound that assumes nothing about the dependence between arms resolves all nine comparisons in the tree's favour after Holm adjustment. The pure-nonlinearity premium on backbone features is $0.00215$ against a correctly specified linear ladder. The edge is not a tuning story from any angle we measured: causally tuned linear arms are worse than the untuned incumbent, and a test-set oracle over penalties is worth at most $0.00015$. What the trees exploit saturates at additive-plus-pairwise structure anchored to the session clock---which is what a quadratic self-exciting feedback \citep{BlancDonierBouchaud2017} predicts, converting a puzzling ceiling into an expected one.

\paragraph{What algorithm should extract it, and how little of it is needed.} Section~\ref{sec:algorithms} freezes the feature set and treats estimation as the design space: exact rank-1 rolling least squares, a recursive elastic net via online homotopy \citep{Garrigues2008}, a causal rolling hyperparameter-selection protocol, and a spectral $k$-nearest-neighbors estimator that embeds trailing residual trajectories with Laplacian eigenmaps \citep{BelkinNiyogi2003}. The section's contribution is its ablations, and they generalise past the estimator they were built to test: the manifold-embedding step hurts on all eight matched configurations, and a matched retrieval-metric experiment shows the cause is not the out-of-sample extension the design was blamed on---a principal-component projection at the same dimension has no extension step at all and loses nearly as much. On the uncorrected panel every estimated geometry we tried lost to a fixed one; on the corrected panel that statement holds only for the \emph{unsupervised} ones. A supervised six-dimensional projection onto the exogenous operator's singular triplets beats the full $516$-dimensional ambient view by $0.00540$ ($t = -8.9$), having been an exact null before the repair, while principal components and Laplacian eigenmaps at the same dimension still lose by $+0.00552$ and $+0.00450$. A distance weights every coordinate by its scale, so a channel standardised by a collapsed denominator dominates the metric outright---which is why this particular result could not have been read correctly on the panel we had.

Section~\ref{sec:stale_data} interrupts that thread, because pursuing it further on the panel we had would have been pursuing an artefact. One exogenous feed stops publishing on 2023-08-31 while the panel runs eight months past it; an unlimited forward fill carried its final value across those months, and an availability indicator computed \emph{after} the fill labelled every one of those bars observed. Features are standardised in rolling interquartile units, a frozen series has no dispersion, and the division that standardises it is then a division by nearly nothing: the affected columns reach $2{,}260$ rolling IQRs. The window cost is QLIKE $6.37170$ against $0.12391$ repaired. Three further feeds terminate the same way inside the evaluation region, and we did not find them until we built the check. That check is one line---each channel's availability indicator mean must equal its raw print rate---and on the uncorrected panel it fails for $31$ of $41$ channels. The repair is an interaction: the honest indicator changes no feature value, masked scale estimation alone leaves the worst channel untouched, and a neutral median without masked estimation is actively harmful; composed they reach $6.7$ from $2{,}260.4$.

The consequence for the thread above is direct, and it cost us a draft. On the uncorrected panel a stripped-down design---contract each channel's rung ladder against a fixed $u^{-1}$ weight, run plain ridge on the resulting 41 numbers---reaches QLIKE $0.13248$ in $53$ columns while plain ridge on the $504$-feature design reaches $0.13617$, worse than the $12$-column backbone it exists to improve. We had made that the paper's central result. On the corrected panel the ordering reverses, to $0.13272$ and $0.13146$. The stripped-down design had won by attenuating the corrupted channel twelve-fold and the raw parameterisation had lost by carrying it faithfully; neither number was about parameterisation at all. We re-ran the comparison under a design pre-registered before the results were seen, our own falsification condition for the stripped-down design was met at $-0.00126$ ($t = -5.91$), and we withdraw it. What survives the correction is narrower and better supported, and narrower again once controlled properly. Sweeping rank on the lag axis under two independent nested orthonormal bases, the two agree from $r = 4$ upward and the curve is flat there---$0.00045$ across $r \in [4, 12]$---so rank beyond four buys nothing and $r = 8$ in $340$ columns matches all $504$. The bases disagree below $r = 4$: an orthonormalised power-law triple reaches $0.13117$ in $135$ columns while log-polynomials at the same rank reach $0.13246$, and the latter's validation-selected arm loses to the full design outright by $0.00097$ ($t = +8.10$). Low-rank compression here is a claim about which shapes, not about how many; a single-basis sweep would have reported an intrinsic dimension of three, and we ran the second basis precisely because this project has already published four claims that a control would have caught. It also would have reported the $135$-column design as beating all $504$ by $0.00029$ at $t = -3.00$, and Section~\ref{sec:inference} shows why that is not reportable either: the statistic falls to $-1.30$ under a wider HAC bandwidth, a block bootstrap admits zero, five of eight eras favour it, and deleting one era leaves $-0.000027$. The two designs tie. That is the more useful finding, and it is the paper's thesis applied to itself---a nearly fourfold difference in width buys nothing, while a dead data feed cost a factor of fifty.

\paragraph{What dominates, and where it does not.} Section~\ref{sec:era_stability} reports the effects that exceed every modelling choice in this paper. A fixed training window rather than a rolling one is worth $0.11$ QLIKE. The imputation defect above is worth a factor of fifty on its window and moves the failing era from $+0.03321$ to $-0.00332$. Selecting a shape parameter against the reported loss instead of a validation tail is worth $0.0033$. Against these, the model-class effects this literature argues about are $0.002$ to $0.004$. We pre-registered the stronger version of that claim---that the data-version main effect would exceed the design main effect---and it fails: pooled over six designs the cache effect is $0.00067$ against $0.00311$. The honest statement is therefore conditional rather than universal. Data representation dominates where the defect binds, catastrophically; averaged over twenty years and over designs that mostly do not touch the affected columns, it does not. We report the failed hypothesis because the conditional version is the one that transfers, and because a pre-registration that only ever confirms is not doing any work.

\paragraph{An independent replication.} Section~\ref{sec:replication} reports
a campaign run after the main results froze, on a second construction the
primary tables never touch: the base features projected onto a
permanently frozen first-window eigenbasis, and the dynamics --- lead--lag
structure, rotational geometry, error second moments --- of the trajectory on
that basis. The section exists for the same reason the second orthonormal
basis above does: this project no longer trusts a law demonstrated once. Three
of the paper's central claims re-derive there with different machinery
(density with uniform generous shrinkage beats selection; the exploitable
content is fast and freshness-loving; estimated geometry loses to fixed
geometry without supervision), and the campaign contributes two laws the
primary basis could not see --- a finite-rank lead--lag field flat on a wide
plateau of basis truncations, and a regime change that destroys a channel's
\emph{complexity} while strengthening its first-order structure. Every arm was
pre-registered with a recorded expectation; sixteen died at their gates; the
seven survivors passed a closing Romano--Wolf sweep as a family.

\paragraph{Commitments.} Three run through the paper. First, one panel, one benchmark: no result is quoted across incompatible evaluation regimes, and superseded experiments contribute no number. Second, significance before narrative: differences without a DM statistic are labeled unresolved, quantities we have not computed are marked in the text rather than elided, and every multiple-comparison family is adjusted (Section~\ref{sec:inference})---which cost us three inherited claims and one that had never been computed. Third, negative results are reported at the same altitude as positive ones---the failures of PCA compression, deep sequence models, hyperparameter tuning, and our own manifold embedding are load-bearing facts about the dense-but-weak regime, not footnotes. Section~\ref{sec:limitations} collects the threats that could change a conclusion, of which the largest are the benchmark debt above, a target winsorization that clips 10\% of observations by construction, and a diurnal adjustment that is itself a forecast. To those we add a fourth commitment adopted partway through this study and applied retroactively: Section~\ref{sec:ledger} publishes the fifteen claims we made and withdrew, and the four causes they trace to. We think the ledger is the most useful thing in the paper for a reader deciding how much of the rest to believe.
