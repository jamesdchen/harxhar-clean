\section{Transmission: Factors and Lead--Lag Structure}
\label{sec:transmission}

% Arc: the transmission story. First the 20 factors, derived naturally; then
% the Cucuringu lead-lag features developed on top of them; ending with the
% final 4-block ridge of everything.
% Source material: alpha_manifestation_findings_2026-08-04.md,
% reference/sections/replication_state.tex (transmission width, span law).

The three blocks assembled so far are contemporaneous: the target's own
history, the exogenous panel, and the frozen pairwise products are all
functions of data observable at the current bar. This section adds a directed
lead--lag block: the transmission of activity from one part of the market to
another at a one-bar delay. The construction has three steps. First, a factor
frame is estimated from the eigenstructure of the panel's first training
window. Second, a lead--lag field is estimated on that frame, using the
toolkit of Cucuringu and coauthors~\pending{cite: Hermitian lead--lag
clustering / SPONGE / dynamic-network CPD references}. Third, the resulting
columns enter the ridge as a fourth block.

\subsection{Twenty Natural Factors}

The factor construction uses no rotation, no supervision, and no tuning. The
panel's base features comprise 133 columns, of which 106 are live,
non-degenerate directions. We form the correlation matrix of these columns
over the first training window (2005--06) and take its eigendecomposition.
The factor frame is the set of leading eigenvectors of this matrix. The frame
is then \textbf{frozen permanently}: the factor score $G_i(t)$ is the
projection of the bar-$t$ feature vector onto eigenvector $i$, and the
eigenvectors are estimated once, from feature data alone, before any
forecasting begins. Because the frame is estimated from $X$ and never from
the target, objects built on it can be validated by split-half replication
--- estimating the same object independently on the two halves of the sample
and correlating the two estimates --- without contamination from the signal.
The lead--lag field built on this frame has the highest split-half
replication of any object in the study (Section~\ref{sec:trans-leadlag}).

The count of 20 was carried as a convention and was not optimized. Three
statistics of the same first-window spectrum bracket it. The participation
ratio of the eigenvalue spectrum --- the squared sum of eigenvalues divided
by the sum of squared eigenvalues, an effective dimension count --- is
$\approx 18$; the span is effectively $\approx 20$-dimensional. The raw
Marchenko--Pastur edge --- the largest eigenvalue expected from a correlation
matrix of independent noise at the nominal sample size --- leaves 22 of the
106 live eigenvalues above the noise level. And the linear frame is stable
across halves of the 18-year sample, while every nonlinear geometric object
built from the factors (curvature, spectra of interaction forms, cluster
partitions) fails split-half replication. Twenty is the round cover of the
18--22 bracket. Section~\ref{sec:trans-leadlag} reports the experiment that
later varied the count directly.

One refinement of the Marchenko--Pastur count matters for everything that
follows. Bar data is serially dependent. The median integrated
autocorrelation time of the factor scores --- the factor by which serial
correlation reduces the effective number of independent observations --- is
175 bars ($\approx 3.6$ days). This deflates the effective sample to
$T_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 68$ per dimension and raises the
dependence-adjusted noise edge to $5.04$. Only \textbf{5} eigenvalues exceed
that edge, so only 5 eigenvectors are individually identified. This does not
mean the mid-spectrum is noise: objects built on the mid-spectrum replicate
split-half at $+0.6$ to $+0.99$, and noise replicates at zero. The correct
statement is that the data pins the subspace the mid-spectrum eigenvectors
span but not the basis inside it: the individual eigenvectors are not
identified, while their span is stable under split-half replication. This
fact predicts the section's central robustness result: any cover of the span
performs equally well, and no selection of directions inside the span beats
carrying the span whole.

\subsection{Cucuringu Lead--Lag Features}
\label{sec:trans-leadlag}

\paragraph{The lead--lag field.}
Let $C$ be the lag-one-bar cross-correlation matrix of the factor scores,
$C_{ij} = \mathrm{corr}\bigl(G_i(t-1),\, G_j(t)\bigr)$. Its antisymmetric
part is $A = (C - C^{\top})/2$: $A_{ij} > 0$ states that factor $i$ leads
factor $j$ at one bar more than $j$ leads $i$. This matrix is the raw
material of Hermitian lead--lag clustering~\pending{cite}. Two controls
remove diurnal artifacts, since the deterministic intraday timetable could
manufacture artificial lead--lag. First, each factor score is deseasonalized
per slot: it is standardized within its time-of-day slot, so that no score
carries a diurnal mean or variance profile. Second, an explicit clock control
is computed: the field implied by the deterministic time-of-day component
alone is estimated separately, and its share of the dynamic flow is $-0.003$.
The field is the most replicable object in the study: the dynamic flow
replicates split-half at $+0.992$. A third control addresses construction
arithmetic: features built from trailing moving averages induce a mechanical
lag-one cross-correlation, a closed-form expression for that induced
component explains 6\% of the field's variance, and the residual after
removing it still replicates at $+0.992$.

Three properties describe the field. First, its Hodge decomposition --- the
decomposition of an antisymmetric flow into a gradient part, which is
consistent with a single ranking of the nodes, and a cyclic part (curl),
which circulates around loops --- is \textbf{96.5\% curl}: the field is a
circulation, not a ranking. Second, the field estimated on regular trading
hours and the field estimated overnight agree at similarity $+0.967$.
Third, its hub structure is: the turnover and tail-activity factors lead
the implied-volatility factors, and the implied-volatility factors lead the
realized-volatility factors, at the one-bar (half-hour) lag.

\paragraph{From field to feature.}
The feature construction applies the field as a linear operator. Let $D$ be
the antisymmetric part of the lag-one-bar cross-correlation of the factor
scores, estimated causally on a trailing 504-day window and re-estimated
quarterly. The transmission columns are
\[
\hat G_j(t) \;=\; \sum_i D_{ij}\, G_i(t - 1\ \mathrm{bar}),
\]
one column per factor: column $j$ is the next-bar motion of factor $j$ as
predicted by the flow through it. The antisymmetric part is used, and not the
full lag-one cross-correlation, because the symmetric part is persistence
content that the moving-average ladder already spans. This choice was tested
directly: 20 columns of \emph{plain} lagged scores $G(t-1)$ --- the same
lagged information with no flow structure --- fail the entry gate outright,
while the $D$-weighted columns pass. The flow content, not generic lag-one
information, is what the design was missing. \pending{one-bar entry-gate
statistics for the plain-lag control vs the $D$-weighted columns, from the
unification campaign}

The block is evaluated against same-engine twins: models with the same ridge
engine and the other three blocks, differing only by the candidate block.
Against those twins the block enters at Diebold--Mariano $+9.90$ at one bar
--- the strongest feature statistic in the campaign. The estimation cadence
of $D$ was also varied. Re-estimating $D$ monthly ($+0.75$) or per bar
($+1.48$) fails the gate; only the quarterly cadence passes. Elsewhere in
this paper more frequent refitting helps or is neutral; for $D$ it hurts.
Lag-one $D$, re-estimated quarterly, is the complete form of the feature.

\paragraph{The width plateau.}
The width of 20 was inherited, not established by test, so it was varied end
to end. The procedure is as follows. The frames are nested: for each width
$q$, the frame is the top-$q$ eigenvectors of the same first-window
correlation matrix, and the full pipeline (scores, $D$, transmission columns,
ridge) is re-run at that width. Each width is compared head-to-head against
$q = 20$ under the same protocol. The one-bar increment is not flat in $q$.
It improves through $q = 40$ (head-to-head over $q = 20$: $+2.29$ at daily
refit, $+4.59$ per-bar), holds through $q = 80$, and fails its gate outright
at $q = 106$ --- every live direction ($+1.62$ against the twin, the width
ladder's first failure). Refinement rungs at $q \in \{30, 50, 60, 70\}$ show
the interior is flat to within noise. The forecast improvement therefore sits
on a \textbf{plateau over widths $\sim$30--80}, with worse performance on
both sides: the lead--lag structure occupies a finite-rank sub-span roughly
twice the conventional frame, is dense within that sub-span, and is absent
beyond it. Three probes locate the mechanism. The widening gain is carried by
the new directions' \emph{own} next-bar forecastability, not by new
information about the first 20 factors. Doubling the $D$ estimation window
rescues nothing at $q = 106$: the tail directions carry no lead--lag content,
rather than content estimated too noisily. And the field restricted to the
new directions replicates split-half at $+0.838$ (old block $+0.907$): the
field on the widened span is stable even though the individual eigenvectors
inside the span are not identified. Production runs width 40 as a
representative of the plateau, not as a tuned optimum. This is the outcome
the span result above predicts: covering the span is what matters, and the
span's truncation point is not identified. All width comparisons here are
one-bar results, and the width-40 choice rests on them alone.

\subsection{The Four-Block Ridge}
% Endpoint: the final composed model — all four blocks.

The transmission block completes the model. The final specification is one
blockwise ridge in a single solver --- \textbf{719 columns} --- refit every
bar on a 250-day rolling window with a one-day embargo, with a separate
penalty per block:
\[
y \;\sim\; \mathrm{ridge}_{250\mathrm{d}}
\bigl[\;
\underbrace{\text{target-HAR backbone}}_{27\ \text{cols},\ \alpha_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1}
\;\big|\;
\underbrace{\text{linear exogenous block}}_{516 + 36\ \text{cols},\ 3\times10^{3}}
\;\big|\;
\underbrace{\text{frozen products}}_{100\ \text{cols},\ 3\times10^{4}}
\;\big|\;
\underbrace{\text{transmission}}_{40\ \text{cols},\ 3\times10^{3}}
\;\bigr].
\]

while the arc notes for Sections~\ref{sec:dense_weak}
and~\ref{sec:nonlinear_product} quote the $1/100/1000$ normalization ---
harmonize one convention across the three endpoint specs}
The per-block penalties are implemented by column scaling: multiplying a
block's columns by a constant $c$ and solving a single ridge at a common
penalty $\alpha$ is identical to penalizing that block at $\alpha / c^{2}$,
so one solver call fits all four blocks. The transmission block's own
penalty was laddered, and the selected value equals the exogenous block's
penalty, not the frozen-product block's. The backbone carries the smallest
effective penalty, the linear exogenous block a much larger one, the
frozen-product block one an order of magnitude larger still, and the
transmission block the exogenous value.

The increment from the fourth block is small and statistically strong, as
every gain in this paper is. On the campaign panel, adding the width-40
transmission block to the 699-column per-bar model moves QLIKE from
$0.12546$ to $0.12529$ (Diebold--Mariano $+4.59$; $+2.32$ on 2020+), against
that panel's HAR baseline of $0.13275$. Composed with the second-moment
correction of Section~\ref{sec:smearing} in its final form, the campaign's
headline number closed at QLIKE $0.12299$ ($\approx -7.3\%$ against HAR),
having opened the campaign at $0.12579$. \decide{which number is this section's endpoint headline:

The transmission block is the only block in the model that uses information
at a delay: the other three read the current bar, and this one predicts
next-bar factor motion from the previous bar. On a cross-section of assets,
the same construction --- a lead--lag field estimated across names rather
than across one market's factors --- becomes the estimation target rather
than an added feature block. That extension lies beyond this paper.
