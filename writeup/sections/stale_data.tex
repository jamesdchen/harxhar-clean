\section{Stale Data as the Dominant Effect}
\label{sec:stale_data}

This section reports the largest effect in the paper. It is not a modelling
effect. A single exogenous feed stopped publishing partway through the sample,
the pipeline did not notice, and the resulting artefact was worth more than
every model-class, basis, penalty and shrinkage decision reported elsewhere in
this study combined---by three orders of magnitude on the affected window and
by roughly a factor of ten pooled over twenty years.

We report it as a finding rather than as a correction because the failure is
invisible to ordinary practice. No test fails. No value is missing. Nothing is
infinite or negative. The pipeline produces a dense, finite, plausibly-scaled
design matrix throughout, and the defect is detectable only by comparing the
pipeline's own bookkeeping against the raw inputs it came from---a comparison
that, so far as we can determine, is not standard.

\subsection{What Happened}

The panel is a 30-minute grid spanning 2005-01-02 to 2024-04-30, covering
Sunday 18:30 through Friday 20:00---approximately 24 hours a day, five days a
week. The 41 exogenous channels are not all published on that grid. Most are
US cash- or extended-session equity quantities, and each prints only inside its
own session. Table~\ref{tab:calendars} gives the measured print rates against
the session width each channel's own publication times imply.

Averaged across the 41 channels, $0.638$ of panel rows carry a genuine print.
The remaining $0.362$ must be filled, and the pipeline filled them by carrying
the last observed value forward without limit.

The volatility-demand family stops publishing on 2023-08-31. The panel
continues for a further $8{,}453$ bars, $3.44\%$ of the sample. Across all four
of its channels the print rate through October 2023 is exactly $0.000$. The
unlimited fill carried a value from the final August print across the following
eight months, and---because the availability indicator was computed
\emph{after} the fill rather than before it---labelled every one of those bars
as observed. Measured on the family: raw coverage $0.2197$, indicator mean
$0.6983$.

Three further feeds terminate the same way. \texttt{vix}, \texttt{vix3m} and
\texttt{vvix} each stop on 2024-02-12, leaving $2{,}745$ to $2{,}747$ dead bars
apiece inside the evaluation region. We did not identify these until we
constructed the calendar check described below, and no result in the earlier
literature on this panel accounts for them.

\subsection{Why One Ordering Error Defeated Three Guards}

Computing the indicator after the fill is a single line's worth of ordering,
and it disabled every protection the pipeline already carried.

The impute-and-indicate median substitution never fired, because the forward
fill had left no missing value to substitute for. The model could not
distinguish a fresh print from an eight-month-old one, the indicator reading
$\approx 1$ in both cases. And the scale guard---which floors each feature's
rolling interquartile range at an expanding IQR taken over its \emph{real}
values---computed that floor over the forward-filled constants, so the floor
was itself degenerate.

That last failure is the one that converts a stale feature into a destructive
one. Features are expressed in rolling-IQR units. A frozen series has no
dispersion, so its rolling IQR collapses toward zero, and the division that
standardises the feature is then a division by nearly nothing. The
volatility-demand columns reach $2{,}260$ rolling IQRs. The series had not
moved; the denominator had vanished.

\subsection{What It Cost}

On the October 2023 window the ridge fit on the full 504-feature design
attains QLIKE $6.37170$. On the same window with the defect repaired it attains
$0.12391$, against a backbone of $0.12823$ that is unaffected either way. The
forecast was one to two orders of magnitude below the realised value, and $27$
bars out of $27{,}376$ carry $47\%$ of the only catastrophic exogenous failure
in the twenty-year sample.

Pooled over the whole panel the exogenous block's era-8 deficit against the
backbone moves from $+0.03321$ to $-0.00332$. Every genuine modelling effect
reported in this paper lies between $0.0005$ and $0.004$.

The consequence for our own earlier conclusions is severe enough to state
plainly. Model comparisons run on the uncorrected panel were scored partly on
how far each design happened to \emph{dilute} the corrupted channel. A design
that contracts each channel's twelve rungs to a single amplitude attenuates it
by a factor of twelve, and duly appeared to win; the raw parameterisation
carried it faithfully, and duly appeared to lose. On the corrected panel the
ordering reverses. We had drafted a version of this paper around the former
result. Section~\ref{sec:ledger} records it and thirteen other withdrawn
claims.

\subsection{The Check That Catches It}

The defect is recoverable from one comparison, which we now run as a gate on
every built cache:

\begin{quote}
For each channel, the mean of the availability indicator the pipeline
constructed must equal the fraction of rows on which the raw feed actually
printed.
\end{quote}

On the uncorrected panel this fails for $31$ of $41$ channels; on the
volatility-demand family it reads $0.698$ against $0.223$. The check requires
no model, no held-out data and no threshold tuning---the two quantities are
estimates of the same number, and any gap is the bug or one like it. We
recommend it as standard for any mixed-frequency panel in which exogenous
series are filled.

We report four further artefact-level invariants alongside it---scale collapse
measured over live rows, staleness as the longest run of exact repetition,
imputation share, and finiteness with a magnitude summary. Table~\ref{tab:invariants}
gives the violation counts across build stages.

\subsection{The Fix Is an Interaction}

The repair has two halves, and neither works alone. This is the part we would
have missed had we not been forced to measure each separately.

Recording availability before the fill---the honest indicator---changes no
feature \emph{value}. It corrects the bookkeeping and leaves the corrupted
column exactly as it was. Estimating each feature's location and scale from
observed rows only, without also repairing the values, leaves the
volatility-demand family unchanged at $2{,}260$ rolling IQRs. Substituting a
neutral median on unobserved rows \emph{without} masked estimation is actively
harmful: it replaces varying stale values with a constant, which collapses the
rolling IQR of a densely-observed channel and takes \texttt{effspread\_vwstock}
from $17.6$ to $932.6$.

Composed, they take the worst channel from $2{,}260.4$ to $6.7$. Three separate
attempts at this repair failed before we tested the two-by-two, each because it
shipped one half.

A third component proved necessary once the first two were in place. With
availability recorded honestly, a channel's scale is estimated from observed
rows only---and in a feed's opening weeks there are few of those. The estimator
accepted a median and IQR from as few as eight observations, so a quiet opening
fortnight set the scale for everything that followed. Each newly-affected
channel's worst value sits 13 to 116 days after its feed's \emph{first} print:
a feed-initiation transient, the same defect family as the termination bug at
the opposite end of a feed's life. Requiring $512$ observations before a scale
is trusted, and flooring the IQR at $1\%$ of the largest scale the channel has
shown, takes the worst channel from $336.5$ to $96.2$ and class-B violations
from $10$ to $1$.

\subsection{What This Implies for Practice}

The general statement is that a fill policy is a modelling choice, and in this
setting it is the dominant one. At any given bar, $36\%$ of the exogenous
design is not a measurement but an echo of an earlier one; on the sparsest
channels the figure is $78\%$. A model handed those columns without an honest
indicator is fitting the fill policy, not the market, and it cannot tell the
difference because nothing in the design distinguishes them.

We therefore report the ordering of effects we actually measured, which inverts
the usual emphasis: the representation of missing and stale data dominates,
protocol choices come next, and the model class---the subject of most of the
literature this paper engages with, and of most of our own effort---comes last.
