\section{Where the Information Lives: OLS on the Feature Buckets}
\label{sec:buckets}

The preceding sections fixed the panel, the target, and the reference point
against which everything in this paper is measured: the per-bar least-squares
fit of the HAR ladder plus calendar dummies, QLIKE $0.13415$. This section asks
the first substantive question about the exogenous data programme: \emph{do the
exogenous variables contribute at all, and how much of their contribution is
shared?} We answer it at the level of the feature buckets defined in
\pending{cross-ref to the panel/descriptive section defining the buckets}, and
we answer it deliberately within a single model class, ordinary least squares.
Holding the estimator fixed at the simplest linear map separates the question
of whether a feature carries information from the question of whether a
particular estimator can exploit it; what a richer hypothesis class does with
the same features is the subject of \pending{cross-ref to the
linear-vs-nonlinear section}, and the comparison between the two is only
interpretable because this section refuses to move the estimator.

\paragraph{What ``least squares'' means here, precisely.} Honesty requires
stating the estimator actually run rather than asserting a uniform ``OLS'' that
was never run in that exact form. The attribution table below is the ridge arm
of the causal-tuning battery: rolling least squares over a window of $24{,}000$
bars ($500$ trading days), refit at every bar by exact rank-one
downdate/update of the normal equations, with a ridge penalty
\citep{HoerlKennard1970} whose strength is re-selected causally from a forward
validation split. The penalties selected are small, and the battery's own
no-exogenous baseline row bounds the distance from exact OLS directly:
penalizing the HAR ladder with no exogenous features present moves QLIKE by
only $+0.0003$ against the $\alpha = 0$ incumbent --- and in the wrong
direction, which is itself evidence that shrinkage is doing nothing here beyond
numerical stabilisation. A literal $\alpha = 0$ battery with exogenous features
has \emph{not} been run; the code path accepts $\alpha = 0$ directly, so this
is a pending robustness run rather than an open question of feasibility.

\subsection{Per-Bucket Gains}

Table~\ref{tab:buckets} reports the causally tuned ridge arm on each of the
exogenous buckets, on the shared panel ($n = 218{,}934$ thirty-minute bars,
identical rows in every cell), against the shared incumbent. Cells carry the
Diebold--Mariano $t$-statistic \citep{DieboldMariano1995} against the
incumbent, computed as the $t$-ratio of the mean per-bar loss differential to
its Newey--West HAC standard error with the Harvey--Leybourne--Newbold
small-sample correction \citep{HarveyLeybourneNewbold1997}; negative $t$
favours the bucket arm. $\Delta$ is measured against the incumbent;
$\Delta_{\text{own}}$ is measured against the same estimator's own
no-exogenous baseline ($0.13445$), which is the correct within-class
increment.

\paragraph{Every bucket improves on HAR, significantly.} Reading down the
table, there is no exogenous bucket whose addition degrades the forecast, and
no bucket whose improvement is attributable to sampling noise: the weakest arm,
\texttt{vol\_demand}, still rejects equal predictive accuracy at $t = -4.1$.
The spread between the best single bucket (\texttt{moments}, $-0.00331$
against the incumbent) and the weakest (\texttt{sentiment}, $-0.00068$) is a
factor of five, but the sign is uniform. This uniformity is itself a finding,
and a hard-won one: in an earlier revision of the pipeline, three of these
buckets --- \texttt{sentiment}, \texttt{implied\_vol} and \texttt{vol\_demand}
--- appeared to \emph{lose} to HAR, and were provisionally written up as such.
Each apparent loss turned out to be a scaling defect in the feature
construction rather than an absence of signal, and each bucket became a genuine
improvement once the defect was repaired. The general lesson, stated because it
bears on how any such table should be read, is that a feature that appears to
hurt is very often a feature the pipeline mis-scales. An earlier, independent
walk-forward battery at a fixed $\alpha = 1$ penalty --- a different
configuration on the same panel --- produces the same ranking, with
\texttt{moments} first among single buckets and the joint set dominant, so the
attribution is not an artefact of the causal penalty selection.

\subsection{Overlap: The Sum of the Parts Is Twice the Whole}

The organising fact of Table~\ref{tab:buckets} is what happens when the
individual gains are summed. Adding the seven within-class increments
($\Delta_{\text{own}}$) gives $-0.01364$, against a joint increment of
$-0.00657$: a ratio of $2.08$. \emph{The sum of the parts is roughly twice the
whole.} The buckets are therefore not seven independent channels of
information about future variance; they are seven partially redundant views of
a much smaller amount of information. The same computation on the causally
tuned LightGBM arm gives $2.24$, so the redundancy is a property of the
information rather than of the linear estimator.

We note a trap that an earlier draft of this analysis fell into. If the
overlap ratio is computed against the \emph{shared incumbent} rather than each
estimator's own baseline, every bucket's increment silently includes that
estimator's model-class premium, and the ratio inflates --- to $3.98$ for
LightGBM, against its true $2.24$. (Within the linear class the distortion is
mild: incumbent-relative increments give a ratio of $1.84$, because the ridge
baseline sits only $0.00030$ from the incumbent.) Overlap ratios must be
computed within class.

Sublinearity of this magnitude is the single most important structural fact
about the feature set: the buckets overlap so heavily that no single bucket is
a proxy for the information, yet each contributes enough unique signal that
its individual addition is detectable. It is the bucket-level image of the
dense-but-weak regime that \pending{cross-ref to the linear-vs-nonlinear /
dense-but-weak section} takes as its starting point.

\subsection{The Joint Bucket}

Redundancy is partial rather than total: the joint \texttt{all\_features} arm
at $0.12788$ beats the best single bucket by a further $-0.00297$, so stacking
still pays --- it simply pays at roughly half the rate a naive addition of
marginal gains would suggest. An independent every-bar robustness design on a
different panel --- rolling $1{,}000$-trading-day window, HAR block exactly
unpenalized via Frisch--Waugh--Lovell with only the exogenous block penalized,
scored on a fixed COVID-inclusive window from January 2018 to May 2025,
$n = 74{,}934$ bars --- reproduces the structure in full: every bucket again
rejects equal predictive accuracy against HAR-only (the weakest,
\texttt{vol\_demand}, at $t = -6.2$), and the joint set again dominates every
single bucket, at $t = -13.0$. The bucket \emph{ranking} rotates with the
window --- \texttt{implied\_vol} leads on the COVID-inclusive window,
\texttt{moments} in Table~\ref{tab:buckets} --- which is what one expects when
the evaluation window becomes dominated by a volatility crisis, and the
rotation is a further argument against promoting any single bucket.

An earlier draft went further and asserted that the $90\%$ model confidence
set \citep{HansenLundeNason2011} over the feature sets is the singleton
containing the joint bucket. We do not repeat that claim here: a model
confidence set requires the arms' per-bar loss series, which were not retained
for the battery, so the assertion was never supported by a computed set.
\pending{battery MCS over the feature sets once per-bar losses are persisted}
The conclusion the table does support is still worth stating plainly: every
individual bucket is significantly better than HAR, the joint set is better
than every individual bucket, and any narrative that promotes one bucket as
the source of skill is reading a point estimate that the overlap structure
does not support. The defensible unit of attribution is the joint feature set.

\paragraph{A caveat on the benchmark.} Every $\Delta$ in
Table~\ref{tab:buckets} is measured against the base-5 HAR incumbent, and
\pending{cross-ref to the ladder-base section} shows that the same estimator on
a base-2 ladder reaches QLIKE $0.13332$, at DM $t = -10.6$: a gap of $0.00082$
that involves no additional information and is pure quadrature error in the
benchmark. Orderings within the table are unaffected --- the debt is a constant
additive offset shared by every arm --- but the smallest bucket increments are
of the same order as the benchmark misspecification itself, and the joint gain
of $0.00627$ is the figure that survives the correction comfortably.
