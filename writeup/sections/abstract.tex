We study one-step-ahead forecasting of 30-minute realized variance of the S\&P~500 over 2005--2024 on a panel of 218{,}934 bars, and reach a conclusion about our own construction rather than about the market: almost all of the modelling apparatus we built can be deleted without loss, and what remains of the difficulty lies in protocol and data quality rather than in model class. All comparisons are made bar-for-bar on identical rows with Diebold--Mariano inference throughout. First, the backbone: the fitted HAR coefficients imply an effective per-lag kernel that is power-law-like and truncated, which recasts HAR as a quadrature rule on a scale-free grid rather than as a cascade of economically distinct horizons. Estimating that kernel's decay exponent---previously a hardcoded plotting constant---across 41 exogenous channels in six session bins, where the log-log slope is invariant to each channel's scaling, gives $\hat\beta = 1.033$ with a channel-clustered standard error of $0.036$: consistent with the exponent reported throughout the Hawkes-in-finance literature, and rejecting both the $1.36$ of earlier drafts and the $\approx 1.6$ that a rough-volatility correspondence would imply. That reading predicts, and we confirm, that refining the lag grid pays monotonically---base-2 beats base-5 by $0.00082$ (DM $-10.6$) with no change in information, model class, or tuning. The self-exciting reading the power-law shape invites is tested rather than asserted, and testing it corrects a methodological error worth reporting: a self-kernel estimated jointly with 41 exogenous channels---several of them direct volatility measures, hence near-substitutes for lagged $RV$---has its persistence absorbed by those controls, leaving an integrated kernel of $-0.363$ with 14\% negative mass that no self-exciting model admits, while the \emph{marginal} self-kernel on identical rows is well behaved: integrated mass $0.913$, no negative mass, monotone power-law decay at $\hat\beta = 1.506 \pm 0.035$ over ten lags. Second, and centrally: the trial space is a non-lever and the estimator is not. Every lag basis we tried---boxcar, band, piecewise-linear, spline, Chebyshev, Gauss collocation, MIDAS, and greedy reduced bases drawn from the MIDAS manifold---lands within $0.002$ QLIKE of every other, while the \emph{direction} of shrinkage moves results by $0.03$. Pursuing that lever to its conclusion collapses it. A $41 \times 12$ exogenous operator, its singular value decomposition, session-conditional frames, CP and Tucker factorisations, rank-constrained priors and a bespoke projection penalty are together reproduced---and beaten---by contracting each channel's rung ladder against a fixed $u^{-1}$ weight and running plain ridge on the resulting 41 numbers: 53 columns at QLIKE $0.13280$ against 504 columns with a matrix penalty at $0.13415$ ($t = -5.63$), a 12-column backbone at $0.13571$ ($t = -9.59$), and plain ridge on the 492 raw columns at $0.13617$, which is worse than the backbone it is meant to improve. The effective-degrees-of-freedom accounting explains the equivalence exactly: the elaborate penalty spends $\approx 54$ degrees of freedom on its 505 columns, which is $1 + 12 + 41$, the dimension of its own null space, and it spends $72$ of ridge's $\approx 95$ in principal directions carrying $0.02\%$ of the explained target. Relatedly, every estimated geometry we tested lost to a fixed one---Laplacian eigenmaps by $0.00831$, principal components by $0.00678$ at the same dimension and with no out-of-sample extension step to blame for it, and the operator's own leading lag direction whether re-estimated each window or frozen once from an earlier block. Third, what actually dominates. A fixed training window rather than a rolling one moves QLIKE by $0.11$; an imputation defect in our own pipeline---an availability indicator computed after a forward fill, which silently disabled three separate guards written for exactly that pathology---produced scaled features at $2{,}260$ rolling interquartile ranges, cost $0.033$ in a single year, and affects 23 of 41 channels; selecting a shape parameter against the reported loss rather than against a validation tail is worth $0.0033$. Each of these exceeds every model-class effect in the paper. Fourth, stability: the exogenous block beats the backbone in seven consecutive eras and fails once, and 27 bars out of $27{,}376$ carry $47\%$ of that failure, so a twenty-year aggregate supports opposite conclusions depending on where the window ends. We additionally report two properties of the evaluation itself: QLIKE and raw-variance MSE rank this battery almost independently ($+0.15$, $p = 0.32$, where agreement would give a negative correlation), and a validation region immediately preceding the test region mis-ordered the arms in three separate experiments. Subjecting the study's inherited claims to per-bar Diebold--Mariano tests and Hansen--Lunde--Nason confidence sets confirms four, refines two, and retires three; on a $2{,}189$-bar tile the confidence set retains 123 of 161 arms, so a leaderboard at that scale is not a ranking. The practical law we can defend is narrower than the one we set out to state: fix the protocol and the data first, use a small basis of fixed shape, and treat every estimated structure as a cost that must earn its place.
