We study one-step-ahead forecasting of 30-minute realized variance of the S\&P~500 over 2005--2024 on a panel of 218{,}934 bars, asking three questions in sequence: where the predictive information lives, whether extracting it requires nonlinear models, and what estimation algorithm should extract it. A per-bar ordinary-least-squares HAR model with calendar features (QLIKE $0.13415$) serves as a single shared benchmark, and all comparisons are made bar-for-bar on the identical panel with Diebold--Mariano and model-confidence-set inference. First, attribution: every one of eight exogenous feature buckets improves on HAR, but their contributions overlap heavily---the sum of individual bucket gains is roughly twice the joint gain---and the 90\% model confidence set over feature sets is the singleton containing the joint bucket. A Frisch--Waugh--Lovell block decomposition assigns 96\% of explainable variance to the six HAR features, and a single mechanism-targeted engineered feature carries nearly two orders of magnitude more skill per feature than the raw exogenous block. Second, model class: the signal is dense but weak---spread across hundreds of columns, none individually strong, sparse in the coordinate basis but not low-rank. In this regime, tree ensembles hold a modest, systematic edge over penalized linear models (best tree QLIKE $0.12604$ versus best linear $0.12788$); the pure nonlinearity premium on HAR features alone is $0.00297$; and the edge is not a tuning story---causally tuned hyperparameters trail fixed defaults, and an oracle over penalties improves the incumbent by at most $0.00015$. The recoverable nonlinearity saturates at additive-plus-pairwise structure anchored to the session clock. Third, algorithm design: with the feature set frozen, we develop exact every-bar estimators (rank-1 rolling least squares and an online elastic-net homotopy, the $\ell_1$ analog of the Sherman--Morrison update) and a novel spectral $k$-nearest-neighbors estimator that searches for analog episodes via Laplacian-eigenmap manifold learning. Measurement is decisive about which parts of that design pay: an OLS-anchored short-window analog search with heavy local averaging beats the incumbent (QLIKE $0.13096$, DM $-17.6$), while the manifold-embedding step itself hurts on every matched cell---its out-of-sample Nystr\"om extension error is of the same order as the embedding scale. The practical law of the dense-but-weak regime, established three independent ways, is: information $>$ model class $>$ hyperparameters.
