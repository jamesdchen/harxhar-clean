We study one-step-ahead forecasting of 30-minute realized variance of the S\&P~500 over 2005--2024 on a panel of 218{,}909 bars with 41 exogenous channels, and report a result about data representation rather than about model class. All comparisons are made bar-for-bar on identical rows with Diebold--Mariano inference throughout. The dominant effect we measure is not a modelling choice: one exogenous feed stops publishing on 2023-08-31 while the panel runs eight months past it, and an unlimited forward fill---combined with an availability indicator computed \emph{after} that fill rather than before---presented eight months of a dead feed as live data. Because features are standardised in rolling interquartile units and a frozen series has no dispersion, the resulting division produced feature values at $2{,}260$ rolling IQRs. On the affected window this costs QLIKE $6.37170$ against $0.12391$ once repaired, a factor of fifty, while every genuine modelling effect in this study lies between $0.0005$ and $0.004$. Three further feeds terminate the same way inside the evaluation region and were not previously identified. The defect is invisible to ordinary practice---no test fails, no value is missing, the design matrix is dense and finite throughout---and is recoverable from a single comparison we recommend as standard: the mean of each channel's availability indicator must equal the fraction of rows on which the raw feed actually printed. On the uncorrected panel that check fails for $31$ of $41$ channels. The repair is an interaction rather than a fix: recording availability honestly changes no feature value, estimating location and scale from observed rows alone leaves the worst channel untouched, and substituting a neutral median without masked estimation is actively harmful, taking a densely-observed channel from $17.6$ to $932.6$ rolling IQRs; composed, they reach $6.7$ from $2{,}260.4$. Three separate attempts at this repair failed before we ran the two-by-two, each because it shipped one half. We then re-run the study's model comparisons on the corrected panel under a design pre-registered before the results were seen, and report the outcomes against the hypotheses as stated. The winner depends on the data version: on the uncorrected panel a stripped-down $53$-column design attains $0.13248$ and plain ridge on the $504$-feature design attains $0.13617$---worse than the $12$-column backbone it exists to improve---while on the corrected panel the ordering reverses to $0.13272$ and $0.13146$. Our own pre-registered falsification condition for the stripped-down design is met at $-0.00126$ ($t = -5.91$), and we withdraw it: it had won by diluting the corrupted channel twelve-fold, and the raw parameterisation had lost by carrying it faithfully. Two further results survive the correction and one reverses under it. Sweeping rank on the lag axis under two independent nested orthonormal bases---a power-law family and log-polynomials in log-rung---shows the two agreeing from $r = 4$ onward and flat thereafter, spanning $0.00045$ over $r \in [4, 12]$, so rank beyond four buys nothing and $r = 8$ at $0.13145$ is indistinguishable from all $504$ columns at $0.13146$. Below $r = 4$ the bases disagree, and the disagreement is the result: the power family at $r = 3$ reaches $0.13117$ in $135$ columns and beats the full design by $0.00029$ ($t = -3.00$, with $r$ selected on validation rather than on the evaluation rows), while log-polynomials at the same rank reach only $0.13246$. What compresses this design is therefore a particular family of shapes and not a particular dimension---a distinction a single-basis sweep would have reported as an intrinsic rank of three, and which we report only because the second basis contradicted the first; a random-feature approximation to an RBF kernel on the amplitude coordinates gains only $0.00098$ against a tree-over-linear premium of $0.00184$ to $0.00358$, so whatever the trees exploit is not a smooth function of those coordinates. In a $k$-nearest-neighbour retrieval task, a supervised six-dimensional projection onto the exogenous operator's singular triplets \emph{beats} the full $516$-dimensional ambient view by $0.00540$ ($t = -8.9$) on corrected data, having been an exact null on corrupted data, while unsupervised compressions of the same dimension lose by $+0.00450$ to $+0.00552$. One pre-registered hypothesis fails, and we report it as such: pooled over all designs the data-version main effect is $0.00067$ against a design main effect of $0.00311$, so the claim that data repair outweighs any modelling choice holds on the affected window and in the failing era but not in the twenty-year aggregate. Finally, we publish a ledger of fourteen claims we made and withdrew during this study, together with the four causes they trace to: one-factor-at-a-time ablation, unvalidated proxy metrics, selection touching the reported loss, and numbers quoted across incompatible data versions. Three of those four are invisible in a finished paper. Our own answers to the corresponding questions were wrong, and we had the code.
