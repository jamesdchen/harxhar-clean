We study one-step-ahead forecasting of 30-minute realized variance of the S\&P~500 over 2005--2024 on a panel of 218{,}934 bars, asking four questions in sequence: what the target's own structure says the forecasting instrument should be, where the exogenous predictive information lives, whether extracting it requires nonlinear models, and what estimation algorithm should extract it. All comparisons are made bar-for-bar on identical rows against a single shared benchmark---per-bar ordinary least squares on a HAR ladder with calendar features, QLIKE $0.13415$---with Diebold--Mariano inference throughout. First, the backbone: the fitted HAR coefficients imply an effective per-lag kernel that is power-law-like and truncated, which recasts HAR as a quadrature rule on a scale-free grid rather than as a cascade of economically distinct horizons. Estimating that kernel's decay exponent---previously a hardcoded plotting constant---across 41 exogenous channels in six session bins, where the log-log slope is invariant to each channel's scaling, gives $\hat\beta = 1.033$ with a channel-clustered standard error of $0.036$: consistent with the exponent reported throughout the Hawkes-in-finance literature, and rejecting both the $1.36$ of earlier drafts and the $\approx 1.6$ that a rough-volatility correspondence would imply. That reading predicts, and we confirm, that refining the lag grid pays monotonically---base-2 beats base-5 by $0.00082$ (DM $-10.6$) with no change in information, model class, or tuning, and the conventional daily/weekly/monthly structure is worse than either. Because the shared benchmark uses the coarser grid, every increment we report carries that $0.00082$ of backbone misspecification, and we account for it explicitly rather than absorbing it into the effects. The self-exciting reading that the power-law shape invites is tested rather than asserted, and testing it corrects a methodological error worth reporting: a self-kernel estimated jointly with 41 exogenous channels---several of them direct volatility measures, hence near-substitutes for lagged $RV$---has its persistence absorbed by those controls, leaving an integrated kernel of $-0.363$ with 14\% negative mass that no self-exciting model admits. The \emph{marginal} self-kernel on identical rows is well behaved: integrated mass $0.913$, no negative mass, monotone power-law decay at $\hat\beta = 1.506 \pm 0.035$ over ten lags. Read as an endogeneity share that is roughly 91\%, close to where the reflexivity literature places financial activity, though we report it as an analogue rather than an identified branching ratio since a 30-minute aggregated variance is not an event stream. Second, attribution: every one of eight exogenous buckets improves on the backbone, but they overlap heavily---the sum of within-class bucket gains is $2.08$ times the joint gain---and a Frisch--Waugh--Lovell decomposition assigns 96\% of explainable variance to the backbone, with mechanism-targeted engineered features far more efficient per column than raw exogenous series. Third, model class: the signal is dense but weak---spread across hundreds of columns, none individually strong, sparse in no useful basis and not low-rank---and in this regime tree ensembles hold a modest edge over penalized linear models on all nine feature sets (best tree QLIKE $0.12604$ versus best linear $0.12788$). Because the shared incumbent cancels from any pairwise differential, that difference can be tested from the reported standard errors alone under a bound that assumes nothing about the dependence between arms; all nine matched comparisons resolve in the tree's favour after Holm adjustment, the headline pair most narrowly at $|t| \ge 2.19$. The pure nonlinearity premium on backbone features alone is $0.00215$ once measured against a correctly specified linear ladder rather than against the incumbent, roughly 28\% smaller than the uncorrected figure. Tuning is a non-lever from every angle measured: causally tuned penalties trail fixed defaults, and a test-set oracle over penalties moves the incumbent by at most $0.00015$. The recoverable nonlinearity saturates at additive-plus-pairwise structure anchored to the session clock---the ceiling a quadratic self-exciting feedback predicts. Fourth, algorithm design: with features frozen we develop exact every-bar estimators (rank-1 rolling least squares and an online elastic-net homotopy, the $\ell_1$ analog of the Sherman--Morrison update) together with a causal hyperparameter-selection protocol, and evaluate a spectral $k$-nearest-neighbors estimator that retrieves analog episodes via Laplacian-eigenmap manifold learning. Its ablations are decisive against its own novelty: the manifold-embedding step hurts on all eight matched configurations, and the arm that beats the incumbent (QLIKE $0.13096$) is the one with the embedding switched off, leaving an OLS-anchored, heavily averaged analog search that is effective and not novel. A matched retrieval-metric experiment locates the cause, and it is not the Nystr\"om extension the design was blamed on: a principal-component projection at the same dimension has no extension step at all and still loses $0.00678$ ($t = 17.5$) against $0.00831$ for the eigenmap, while a supervised coordinate of that same dimension---built from the fitted exogenous operator's singular triplets---is statistically indistinguishable from the full $516$-dimensional ambient view (Holm-adjusted $p = 1.0$). Unsupervised geometry is what fails here, at every dimension tested and whether linear or nonlinear. The same experiment shows the exogenous block is a net cost to a \emph{distance} even though a fitted map profits from it: discarding all $492$ exogenous coordinates and matching on the residual path alone beats the composed view by $0.00976$ ($t = -11.3$). We additionally report two properties of the evaluation itself: QLIKE and raw-variance MSE rank this battery almost independently ($+0.15$, $p = 0.32$, where agreement would give a negative correlation), and QLIKE systematically rewards attenuated forecasts ($+0.74$ with the Mincer--Zarnowitz slope, $p = 5.8 \times 10^{-9}$). Subjecting the study's inherited claims to per-bar Diebold--Mariano tests and Hansen--Lunde--Nason confidence sets confirms four, refines two, and retires three; on a 2{,}189-bar tile the confidence set retains 123 of 161 arms, so a leaderboard at that scale is not a ranking. The practical law of the dense-but-weak regime is: information $>$ model class $>$ hyperparameters, by roughly a factor of three at each step.
