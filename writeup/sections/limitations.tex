\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

The paper's third methodological commitment is that negative and incomplete
results are reported at the same altitude as positive ones. This section
discharges that commitment for the items that a reader should weigh against
every number above. We separate threats that could change a conclusion from
gaps that merely widen a confidence interval.

\subsection{Threats That Could Change a Conclusion}

\paragraph{Benchmark debt.} All increments are measured against a base-5 OLS
incumbent that a base-2 OLS beats by $0.00082$ at DM $t = -10.6$
(Section~\ref{sec:benchmark_debt}). Orderings within tables are unaffected;
levels and the significance of the smallest effects are not. The bottom four
rows of Table~\ref{tab:buckets} have increments of the same order as the debt
and should not be treated as established until the battery is re-run on the
base-2 backbone.

\paragraph{Target winsorization (measured: it is costing accuracy).} Stage 3
clips each series to rolling 5th and 95th percentile bounds, modifying 10\% of
observations by construction, and on a heavy-tailed variance target the clipped
mass is disproportionately the volatility spikes a forecast exists to
anticipate. We have now run the ablation
(Table~\ref{tab:kernel_experiments}). On a rebuilt panel with a single 60/40
split, relaxing the clip to 1/99 improves raw-scale QLIKE from $0.2648$ to
$0.2449$, and removing it entirely improves it further to $0.2411$. The kernel
diagnostics barely move.

The direction is unambiguous and the magnitude is large relative to everything
else in this paper: winsorizing the target costs roughly $0.024$ QLIKE, an
order of magnitude more than the model-class effect the paper spends a section
establishing. Two caveats keep this from being a headline. The split is a
single 60/40 cut with a full-sample ridge probe, not the walk-forward harness,
so the levels are not comparable to the battery's; and the ablation runs on the
rebuilt panel of Section~\ref{sec:kernel_repro}, not the frozen one.
\pending{re-run the winsorization ablation inside the walk-forward harness on
the frozen panel; on present evidence the 5/95 clip should be expected to
disappear from the pipeline}

\paragraph{The diurnal divisor is itself a forecast (measured).} Stage 1
divides by a \emph{rolling} 20-observation same-slot mean rather than a fixed
seasonal profile (Section~\ref{sec:diurnal}). That is a slowly adapting level
estimate, and it is multiplied back in at evaluation time, so every model in
the paper inherits a common predictive component.

We have now measured how large that component is. Used \emph{alone} as a
forecast---no model, no features, just the rolling same-slot baseline---it
reaches raw-scale QLIKE $0.558$ on a held-out 40\% of the panel, against
$1.320$ for an unconditional mean and $0.241$ for the best full model in the
same setup. On that scale the divisor closes roughly 71\% of the distance
between an unconditional forecast and a fitted one. Replacing it with 48 slot
dummies inside the full model costs $0.03$ to $0.06$ QLIKE
(Table~\ref{tab:kernel_experiments}), so it is not merely a normalisation
convenience: it is doing forecasting work that the reported model differences
are then measured on top of.

The implication for reading this paper is that our effect sizes are increments
over an already-strong shared component, and comparisons against a naive
benchmark that lacks it will overstate every model's skill.

This divisor was also a suspect for the archived kernel's sign structure.
Section~\ref{sec:kernel_repro} exonerates it: the cause is the exogenous
conditioning set, and the marginal kernel is well behaved with the divisor
still in place.

\paragraph{Pairwise significance (resolved, with a caveat).} The model-class
difference is now tested directly, by a bound that requires no assumption about
the dependence between arms and that resolves all nine matched comparisons
after Holm adjustment (Section~\ref{sec:pairwise}). The residual caveat is that
the headline pair is the weakest of the nine at $|t| \ge 2.19$, and that a
model confidence set over the 45 battery arms is still unavailable because
their per-bar losses were not retained.

\paragraph{Multiplicity (resolved).} All 97 testable arms are now adjusted:
Holm leaves 91 of the 93 raw-significant arms and Benjamini--Hochberg leaves 93
(Section~\ref{sec:multiplicity_results}). No claim in this paper depends on
either of the two arms that lose significance. Adjustment does not, however,
address the selection of the spectral-$k$NN headline as the minimum of 32
cells: that is a different problem and it remains open.

\paragraph{Claims withdrawn after testing.} Three findings inherited from
campaign notes did not survive per-bar inference and are no longer asserted:
that trees fail as residual readers ($t = 1.06$ and $1.11$, unresolved), that
Fourier clock modulation beats session gates ($p = 0.10$ unadjusted), and that
the rank marginal adjustment is worse than dividing ($p = 0.071$ after Holm,
against $p = 0.001$ in the original note). A fourth, the singleton model
confidence set over feature sets, was asserted without ever having been
computed and is withdrawn outright (Section~\ref{sec:mcs}). We record these
because the pattern matters: each was a directionally correct point estimate
that had been promoted to a result.

\subsection{Gaps and Inconsistencies}

\paragraph{Feature accounting does not reconcile.} The paper describes 41
exogenous channels, a 359-column exogenous block, and a 529-column design
matrix. Under the stated expansion rule of $|\mathcal{L}| = 6$ rolling means
per channel, $41 \times 6 = 246 \ne 359$, and $529 - 359 = 170$ is not
accounted for by six backbone columns plus calendar.
\pending{an explicit channels~$\to$~lag-expansion~$\to$~design-columns table,
per bucket; the three counts in circulation cannot all be right}

\paragraph{Two training-window conventions.} The preprocessing and backtest
description specifies a 500-observation rolling window; the causally tuned
battery uses 24{,}000 bars. Only the latter produced the numbers reported here.
The former should be removed or explicitly scoped to the superseded
experiments.

\paragraph{Panel heterogeneity in superseded tables.} Earlier subgroup analyses
compared rows with materially different $N$ (as low as 138{,}279 against
222{,}058) and differenced them anyway. Buckets whose coverage begins late---
social sentiment and options order flow---span a different calendar era from
the baseline they were differenced against, so those increments confounded
information with regime. All numbers in this version come from the frozen
battery, on which every arm shares $n = 218{,}934$ identical rows; we note the
issue because it explains why earlier internal numbers disagree with these.

\paragraph{The kernel was measuring the wrong thing.} The archived
coefficient vector on which every kernel diagnostic rested is reproducible---the
rebuilt cache returns it at correlation $+1.0000$---but it is a self-kernel
estimated \emph{jointly with} 41 exogenous channels, several of which are
direct volatility measures. Conditioning on near-substitutes for lagged $RV$
absorbs the target's persistence and leaves a contrast, which is what produced
the negative lobes and the inadmissible integral. The marginal self-kernel on
the same rows has integrated mass $+0.913$ and no negative mass
(Section~\ref{sec:kernel_repro}). We regard this as the most transferable
methodological point in the paper: a self-kernel estimated alongside volatility
proxies is not a self-kernel, and the failure is silent.

\paragraph{Structural claims are not identified, and one of them is refuted.}
We do not fit a Hawkes process. Section~\ref{sec:hawkes} establishes a
correspondence between our regression and a Hawkes intensity and reports
quantities that would be structural under it. Testing them splits the
correspondence in two.

The \emph{shape} survives testing. The decay exponent, previously a hardcoded
plotting constant, is estimated at $1.033$ with a channel-clustered standard
error of $0.036$ and agrees with the Hawkes-in-finance literature; the power
law beats an exponential on fit and on forecast; the exogenous operator is
rank 4--6 of 41 channels.

The \emph{sign structure} fails it, on the archived vector: eight of twelve
lags negative, 10.3\% of absolute mass pooled and up to 87.8\% within a
session, close to half of all exogenous cross-kernel cells negative, and an
integrated kernel of $-0.038$ whose sign flips across sessions.

Nested probes on the rebuilt cache isolate the cause as the exogenous
conditioning set (Table~\ref{tab:nested_probes}): the marginal kernel is
$+0.913$ with no negative mass, adding the 41 exogenous ladders takes it to
$-0.139$, and the full archived set to $-0.363$. The sign structure is a
property of conditioning on volatility proxies, not of the target. We report
$\hat n = 0.913$ as an analogue rather than an identified branching ratio,
because the estimate is ridge-penalised on transformed data and because a
30-minute aggregated variance is not an event stream.

\paragraph{A rival benchmark is missing.} A fractional-kernel forecaster sits
in the same power-law family as our backbone (Section~\ref{sec:rough}) and is
absent from the battery. Given the paper's one-benchmark discipline this is the
single most defensible addition to the ladder sweep, and it is inexpensive.

\subsection{Economic Significance}
\label{sec:economic}

Every effect in this paper lives between QLIKE $0.126$ and $0.134$. The joint
exogenous information effect is $0.00627$, or 4.7\% relative to the incumbent;
the model-class effect is $0.00184$, or 1.4\%; the hyperparameter oracle
ceiling is $0.00015$, or 0.1\%. We have not translated any of these into a
quantity that a user of the forecast would act on, and we think the ordering
\emph{information} $>$ \emph{model class} $>$ \emph{hyperparameters} is the
paper's transportable content precisely because the absolute magnitudes are
small and panel-specific.

Two considerations argue against over-reading the levels. First, the
calibration result of Section~\ref{sec:calibration} shows that QLIKE rewards
attenuation on this panel, so an arm's QLIKE advantage is not straightforwardly
an advantage in forecasting realized variance. Second, all reported levels are
out-of-sample in the walk-forward sense but in-sample with respect to the
choice of preprocessing pipeline, feature dictionary and evaluation window,
none of which were pre-registered.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

Model results in this version derive from a single frozen battery of 98 arms on
one panel of $n = 218{,}934$ bars, with per-arm metrics archived alongside the
manuscript. Superseded experiments---in particular the hyperparameter search of
Section~\ref{sec:tuning_leak} and the variable-$N$ subgroup tables---are
retained in the repository history but contribute no number to this paper.

All inference in Section~\ref{sec:inference} is reproducible from the archived
artefacts. The estimators live in \texttt{analysis/inference.py} (QLIKE,
Newey--West long-run variance, Diebold--Mariano with the
Harvey--Leybourne--Newbold correction, the Politis--Romano stationary
bootstrap, the Hansen--Lunde--Nason MCS, Holm and Benjamini--Hochberg); they
are validated in \texttt{analysis/test\_inference.py} against
\texttt{scipy} for the $t$ distribution and \texttt{statsmodels} for the HAC
standard error and both multiplicity procedures, plus an empirical size check
of the DM test at the nominal level. The two drivers are
\texttt{analysis/run\_tile\_inference.py} and
\texttt{analysis/run\_battery\_bounds.py}; \texttt{analysis/make\_tables.py}
regenerates every inference table in this paper directly from their JSON
output, so no statistic in Section~\ref{sec:inference} is transcribed by hand.
Bootstrap replications are fixed at $B = 5{,}000$ under seed $20260802$, and
the block length is chosen automatically with reported sensitivity from half to
four times its value. Environment: Python 3.11.15, NumPy 2.4.6, SciPy 1.17.1,
statsmodels 0.14.6.

The frozen battery itself is not reproducible from this repository: it was
produced on two HPC clusters and only its per-arm summary metrics were
retained, which is precisely why Section~\ref{sec:pairwise} must bound the
pairwise test rather than compute it. Persisting per-bar loss series should be
treated as a requirement of any future battery run, not an optimisation.
