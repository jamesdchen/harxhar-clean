\subsection{A Last-Bar Same-Day Option Reading}
\label{sec:close_option_results}

The forecasts of Table~\ref{tab:block_ladder} are one-bar forecasts of
realized variance. This subsection reads them against the last thirty
minutes of the expiration-day chain, under the rules of
Section~\ref{sec:close_option_methods}: the nearest out-of-the-money
call and put taken at the 15:30~ET mid, cash-settled at the close, with
the position fixed by the sign and the size of
$s_t = \widehat{RV}_t - \mathrm{IV}_{30,t}^{2}$. Joining the chain to
the seven forecast files leaves 871 expiration days common to all seven
(3~January~2020 to 30~April~2024). Table~\ref{tab:close_option_rules}
crosses every rule with every forecast on those days.

Read the control first. Selling the package every day earns a mean
0.020 per expiration day at a standard deviation of 1.127
($t=0.53$, annualized Sharpe 0.28): a positive premium, at a size that
871 days cannot separate from zero. Putting the forecast in charge of
\emph{direction} destroys even that. The long--short rule
$q=\mathrm{sign}(s)$ scores $t$ between $-0.35$ and $0.20$ across the
seven forecasts, with means within 0.013 of zero in either direction; the
forecast is above implied variance on 13 to 17 percent of days
depending on the model, and those are not days worth being long.

Putting the forecast in charge of \emph{size} is what pays. The
unit-median VRP rule of Equation~\eqref{eq:close_opt_size} lifts the
mean to between 0.047 and 0.060 per day and the annualized Sharpe to
between 0.69 and 0.90, with $t$ ranging from 1.28 for HAR{+}calendar
OLS and 1.31 for the elastic net through 1.67 for the block-diag ridge
to 1.63 for the causally tuned lasso. The ordering is the paper's
ordering, but no book in the panel is significant at conventional
levels.

Three qualifications follow, and they matter more than the point
estimates.

\paragraph{The edge is premium collection, not a variance call.} The
straddle expires worthless --- $R=-1$ exactly --- on 198 of the 871
days, 22.7 percent, whenever the close settles inside the one-strike
window. Those days carry the entire mean of the book: the unit-median
VRP return averages about $+0.63$ per day on them against about
$-0.11$ per day on every other day. What the rule harvests is the
premium on days the index pins, sized by the forecast; it is not a
directional call on realized variance, and it should not be read as one.

\paragraph{The full-sample insignificance is regime dilution.}
Splitting at 16~May~2022, when SPXW listings became daily --- a
market-structure date, not a threshold chosen on the book --- the same
rule scores $t$ between 2.18 and 2.55 on the 2022--2024 tape, against
$t=-0.17$ on the tape before it. The 2020--21 weekly-0DTE regime carries no edge
for this rule at all; pooling the two halves is what holds the
full-sample statistic below two.

\paragraph{Seven rows are one bet.} The seven daily books are correlated
at high pairwise levels, an effective count near one independent book.
Agreement down a column of Table~\ref{tab:close_option_rules} is
evidence that the rule is insensitive to which forecast drives it ---
not seven independent confirmations of the rule.

\paragraph{An event filter, with a pre-registration caveat.} Tail
forensics on the unit-median VRP book locate two calendar flags that
predict two-sided \emph{dispersion} rather than direction: FOMC
statement days and month-ends. Sitting flat on both, under the
convention of Section~\ref{sec:close_option_methods} --- 83 of the 871
days --- gives the book of Table~\ref{tab:close_option_filtered}. The
full-sample $t$ rises to between 2.19 and 2.86 (2.79 for the two-block
ridge, 2.86 for the tuned lasso) and the era $t$ to between 3.09 and
3.71, with the mean roughly 50 percent higher and the standard
deviation lower in every row. Against a matched-size random-flat
placebo the combined lift looks strong ($p<0.001$), but that null
ignores the search: fourteen flat-day variants were examined, and
under the correct null --- the maximum $t$ over the same family with
each candidate replaced by a random day-set of its size --- the
combined filter survives at $p=0.02$, the month-end leg alone is
marginal ($p=0.07$), and the FOMC leg's marginal contribution to the
mean is not distinguishable from chance ($p=0.06$ on top of
month-end). The family-max null is sobering in its own right: its
mean is $t=2.1$, so a flat-day filter that merely clears $t=2$ carries
no evidence at all.

Two things keep that from being a result. The filter was examined
\emph{after} the tail forensics that motivated it, so the $t$ in
Table~\ref{tab:close_option_filtered} is a post-hoc statistic and not a
pre-registered one; and the month-end contribution rests on roughly
seven tail events, too few to carry a distributional claim on their
own. The largest single loss in the book, 8~January~2020, carries
neither flag and survives the filter untouched. The two flags also
rest on different mechanisms, only one of which a forecast can reach.
On FOMC statement days the forecast under-predicts the last-bar
variance by roughly 46 percent at the median, and realized variance
exceeds even the quoted implied variance on 47 percent of those days
against 6 percent elsewhere --- a genuine information gap that belongs
in the forecasting model (an event-aware recalibration recovers part
of it, raising the era $t$ from 2.52 to 2.72 without any filter), and
the pre-registerable fix is event-by-time-of-day features in the
forecasts themselves. Month-ends show no forecast miss at all --- the
loss-condition frequency is at baseline --- so their tail damage is a
property of the settlement returns that no variance forecast can
absorb, and sitting flat is the only lever. The honest reading is a
hypothesis for new tape, not a finding on this one.

The book as a whole is a demonstration under the stated constants of
Section~\ref{sec:close_option_methods}, not a trading claim: there is
no delta hedge, no borrow, no market-impact model, and every fill is at
the 15:30 mid, so a book that receives the bid instead is a different
book. What survives the qualifications is narrow and worth stating
plainly. At the last bar the quoted implied variance is above the
forecast on roughly 85 percent of days, the forecast has no usable
edge on direction, and its only demonstrated contribution is the size
it puts on a short that was going to be short anyway.
