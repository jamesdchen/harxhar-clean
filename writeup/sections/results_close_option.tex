\subsection{A Last-Bar Same-Day Option Reading}
\label{sec:close_option_results}

The forecasts of Table~\ref{tab:block_ladder} are one-bar forecasts of
realized variance. This subsection reads them against the last thirty
minutes of the expiration-day chain, under the rules of
Section~\ref{sec:close_option_methods}: the nearest out-of-the-money
call and put taken at the 15:30~ET mid, cash-settled at the close, with
the position fixed by the sign of
$s_t = \widehat{RV}_t - \mathrm{IV}_{30,t}^{2}$. Joining the chain to
the seven forecast files leaves 871 expiration days common to all seven
(3~January~2020 to 30~April~2024). Table~\ref{tab:close_option_rules}
crosses every rule with every forecast on those days.

Read the control first. Selling the package every day earns a mean
0.020 per expiration day at a standard deviation of 1.127
($t=0.53$, annualized Sharpe 0.28): a positive premium, at a size that
871 days cannot separate from zero. Putting the forecast in charge of
\emph{direction} is what pays. The long--short rule
$q=\mathrm{sign}(s)$ earns between 0.071 and 0.130 per day across the
seven forecasts ($t$ between 1.85 and 3.42, significant at conventional
levels for six of the seven), with the block-diagonal ridge at a mean
of 0.115, $t=3.03$, and an annualized Sharpe of 1.63. The forecast sits
above the quoted implied variance --- a long-volatility day --- on 32
to 42 percent of days depending on the model.

The qualifications matter as much as the point estimates.

\paragraph{Where the level comes from.} Two sample splits qualify the
level rather than the sign. Excluding the first 64 trading days --- the
estimation warm-up shared by the volatility overlay below, which
happens to cover the February--March 2020 spike --- \emph{raises} the
block-diagonal ridge book to Sharpe 1.94 (Newey--West $t=3.71$ on the
remaining 807 days): the full-sample figure is dragged, not flattered,
by the COVID quarter. Splitting instead at 16~May~2022, when SPXW
listings became daily --- a market-structure date, not a threshold
chosen on the book --- the same book scores Sharpe 1.13 ($t=1.38$)
before and 2.05 ($t=2.86$) after: the direction edge is positive on
both tapes but earns its significance on the daily-0DTE era.

\paragraph{Settlement pins carry a disproportionate share.} The
straddle expires worthless --- $R=-1$ exactly --- on 198 of the 871
days, 22.7 percent, whenever the close settles inside the one-strike
window. The always-short control collects $+1$ on those days against
$-0.27$ on all others; the long--short book averages $+0.23$ on them
against $+0.08$ elsewhere, so pin days supply roughly half of its total
return from under a quarter of the days. The book monetizes a direction
call and the pin premium together, and its P\&L remains tilted toward
days the index settles inside the strikes.

\paragraph{Seven rows are one bet.} The seven daily books are correlated
at high pairwise levels, an effective count near one independent book.
Agreement down a column of Table~\ref{tab:close_option_rules} is
evidence that the rule is insensitive to which forecast drives it ---
not seven independent confirmations of the rule.

\paragraph{The signal prices tomorrow's risk, not tomorrow's return.}
Regressing day-$t$ returns on the previous day's signal finds nothing,
five ways: an OLS of $R_t$ on the causal expanding percentile rank of
$s_{t-1}$ scores $t$ between 0.70 and 1.32 across the seven forecasts,
and a split by $\mathrm{sign}(s_{t-1})$, Spearman and Kendall
association, and a top-versus-bottom tercile spread are equally null
(largest $|t|=1.31$; association $p \ge 0.24$). The same lagged rank is
strongly significant for the \emph{magnitude} $|R_t|$ ($t$ between 3.32
and 4.25, every forecast): yesterday's signal says how large today's
settlement move will be, not which way the premium resolves. The
tradable content of $s$ is its same-day sign; its magnitude is a risk
forecast.

\paragraph{Sizing by trailing book volatility standardizes risk without
touching the edge.} Scaling the daily position by the ratio of the
expanding median of the book's own trailing 63-day volatility to its
current lagged value --- self-normalizing, no free target parameter, a
cap of 3 that never binds --- leaves mean and Sharpe statistically
unchanged (block-diagonal ridge long--short 1.94 to 1.98 on the 807
post-warm-up days) while cutting the variability of the book's rolling
volatility by 15 to 21 percent for every forecast and rule and roughly
halving the spread of its per-year volatility (cross-year coefficient
of variation 0.196 to 0.104). This is a standardization of risk, not a
forecast claim, and its structural limit is stated plainly: a trailing
estimator cannot see the first day of a regime change, so single worst
days keep their size (excess kurtosis rises from 10.6 to 12.8) and the
COVID transition itself falls inside the estimator's warm-up.

\paragraph{Capping the sell-side tail costs almost nothing in dollars.}
Buying the nearest wing at least 25 (or 50) points further
out-of-the-money on every selling day turns the short straddle into a
pair of credit vertical spreads whose worst case is the larger actual
wing gap minus the credit received; long-volatility days hold the plain
straddle, whose risk is already its premium. Per dollar of body premium
the long--short book keeps most of its performance --- Sharpe 1.42 at
$w=25$ and 1.54 at $w=50$ against 1.62 uncapped on the same days ---
while the always-short control's thin edge does not survive the wing
drag ($-0.06$ and $+0.10$ against $+0.27$). In dollar terms the
insurance is close to free: at $w=25$ the wings cost 0.007 index points
per package-day (paired HAC $t=-0.04$) while cutting the worst
settlement day from $-78$ to $-22$ points and returning $+310$ points
across the ten worst naked days. Quoted per unit of capital at risk the
same cap looks expensive; that is an artifact of the denominator, which
is smallest exactly on rich-credit, high-volatility days.

\paragraph{An event filter, with a pre-registration caveat.} Tail
forensics on the unit-median VRP book locate two calendar flags that
predict two-sided \emph{dispersion} rather than direction: FOMC
statement days and month-ends. Sitting flat on both, under the
convention of Section~\ref{sec:close_option_methods} --- 83 of the 871
days --- gives the book of Table~\ref{tab:close_option_filtered}. The
full-sample $t$ rises to between 2.19 and 2.86 (2.79 for the two-block
ridge, 2.86 for the tuned lasso) and the era $t$ to between 3.09 and
3.71, with the mean roughly 50 percent higher and the standard
deviation lower in every row. Against a matched-size random-flat
placebo the combined lift looks strong ($p<0.001$), but that null
ignores the search: fourteen flat-day variants were examined, and
under the correct null --- the maximum $t$ over the same family with
each candidate replaced by a random day-set of its size --- the
combined filter survives at $p=0.02$, the month-end leg alone is
marginal ($p=0.07$), and the FOMC leg's marginal contribution to the
mean is not distinguishable from chance ($p=0.06$ on top of
month-end). The family-max null is sobering in its own right: its
mean is $t=2.1$, so a flat-day filter that merely clears $t=2$ carries
no evidence at all.

Two things keep that from being a result. The filter was examined
\emph{after} the tail forensics that motivated it, so the $t$ in
Table~\ref{tab:close_option_filtered} is a post-hoc statistic and not a
pre-registered one; and the month-end contribution rests on roughly
seven tail events, too few to carry a distributional claim on their
own. The largest single loss in the book, 8~January~2020, carries
neither flag and survives the filter untouched. The two flags also
rest on different mechanisms, only one of which a forecast can reach.
On FOMC statement days the forecast under-predicts the last-bar
variance by roughly 46 percent at the median, and realized variance
exceeds even the quoted implied variance on 47 percent of those days
against 6 percent elsewhere --- a genuine information gap that belongs
in the forecasting model (an event-aware recalibration recovers part
of it, raising the era $t$ from 2.52 to 2.72 without any filter), and
the pre-registerable fix is event-by-time-of-day features in the
forecasts themselves. Month-ends show no forecast miss at all --- the
loss-condition frequency is at baseline --- so their tail damage is a
property of the settlement returns that no variance forecast can
absorb, and sitting flat is the only lever. The honest reading is a
hypothesis for new tape, not a finding on this one.
\fi

The book as a whole is a demonstration under the stated constants of
Section~\ref{sec:close_option_methods}, not a trading claim: there is
no delta hedge, no borrow, no market-impact model, and every fill is at
the 15:30 mid, so a book that receives the bid instead is a different
book. What survives the qualifications is worth stating plainly. At the
last bar the quoted implied variance sits above the forecast on 61
percent of days; the sign of that gap carries a direction edge that is
significant for six of the seven forecasts and strongest for the ridge
this paper argues for; and the previous day's signal prices the size of
the next settlement move rather than its direction.
