\section{Descriptive Analysis: The Case for a Kernel Backbone}
\label{sec:descriptive}

Before asking what exogenous information adds, we fix the instrument that will
measure it. This section argues that the target's own autoregressive structure
determines that instrument, and determines it as a \emph{truncated power-law
convolution kernel modulated by a session clock}---of which the standard HAR
regression is a coarse quadrature. The argument matters for the rest of the
paper because every attribution in Sections~\ref{sec:marginal}
and~\ref{sec:linvsnonlin} is measured as an increment over this backbone, and
an incorrectly specified backbone donates its own approximation error to
whatever is being credited.

\subsection{The Panel and the Dense-but-Weak Fact}
\label{sec:dense_weak}

The design matrix has 529 columns. Its covariance concentrates in a handful of
directions, but the forecasting signal does not live in those directions. Three
measurements establish this. They predate the frozen battery and were made on
the earlier all-buckets cache; we report them because the regime they describe
is what the rest of the paper is about, and we mark the levels that are not
panel-comparable:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Low-rank compression destroys skill.} Principal-component
    truncation of the exogenous block retains most of the variance
    ($K=5$ recovers 72\%, $K=20$ recovers 90\%) but loses to dense shrinkage at
    every penalty setting; the best PCA-20 configuration reaches QLIKE
    $0.12890$ against $0.12530$ for the dense elastic net on the same cache.
    The signal is not in the leading eigendirections. \emph{Panel note:} these
    two levels come from the earlier all-buckets cache, not the frozen battery,
    and are comparable to each other but not to Table~\ref{tab:class}.
    \pending{PCA/PLS truncation arms on the frozen panel}
  \item \textbf{$\ell_1$ selection recovers a small unstable core plus a wide
    shallow tail.} Elastic net at its optimum retains roughly 98--120 of 529
    columns; pushing sparsity harder costs performance monotonically.
  \item \textbf{No individual column is strong.} The per-feature effects are
    uniformly small, and the strongest single engineered feature contributes
    less than the aggregate of the tail it sits in.
\end{itemize}

We use \emph{dense but weak} throughout as shorthand for this configuration:
sparse in no useful coordinate basis, not low-rank in data space, and with no
individually decisive column. Section~\ref{sec:linvsnonlin} shows what this
implies for model class; the immediate consequence is that the backbone cannot
be discovered by selection and must be specified.

\subsection{HAR Is a Quadrature Rule}
\label{sec:har_quadrature}

Write the general one-step linear forecast as a convolution of the target's own
past against a decay kernel $\varphi$:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y}_t \;=\; \mu \;+\; \sum_{u \ge 1} \varphi(u)\, y_{t-u}.
  \label{eq:convolution}
\end{equation}
HAR does not estimate $\varphi$ freely. It regresses on trailing rolling means
(boxcars) over a ladder of rungs $r_k$:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y}_t \;=\; \mu \;+\; \sum_{k} w_k \cdot \frac{1}{r_k}\sum_{u=1}^{r_k} y_{t-u}.
  \label{eq:har_boxcar}
\end{equation}
Collecting the total coefficient that Equation~\eqref{eq:har_boxcar} places on
an individual lag $u$ gives the \emph{effective kernel} implied by a fitted HAR:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\varphi(u) \;=\; \sum_{k \,:\, r_k \ge u} \frac{w_k}{r_k}\;}
  \label{eq:eff_kernel}
\end{equation}
which is a decreasing step function with breakpoints at the rungs. Under this
reading the $|\mathcal{L}|$ fitted HAR coefficients are not $|\mathcal{L}|$
distinct facts about volatility at $|\mathcal{L}|$ distinct horizons: they are
quadrature weights on a piecewise-constant approximation to a single underlying
curve, with the rungs as knots. Figure~\ref{fig:target_kernel} plots the fitted
boxcar coefficients and the implied $\varphi$.

\subsection{The Kernel Is Power-Law and Truncated}
\label{sec:powerlaw}

The implied kernel is approximately linear on log-log axes, and a
single-parameter power law $\varphi(u) \propto u^{-\hat\beta}$ approximately
reproduces the entire fitted ladder---the strongest available evidence that the
ladder approximates one curve rather than measuring several components.

Earlier drafts quoted $\hat\beta \approx 1.36$. That figure was a plotting
constant, never an estimate. Estimating it changes the number and, with it, one
of the paper's conclusions. The self-kernel is a poor place to estimate from:
only four of its twelve lags are positive (Section~\ref{sec:kernel_caveats}),
and fitting the log-log slope on those four gives
$\hat\beta = 1.51$ with a standard error of $0.54$---a 95\% interval of
$[0.45, 2.56]$ that contains every value any competing theory proposes.

The exogenous cross-kernels are a far better instrument, for a specific
reason: a log-log slope is invariant to the arbitrary scaling of each channel,
so the 41 channels estimated in each of six session bins give 244 comparable
estimates of the same shape parameter, with a median of six positive lags each.
Clustering by channel---the six session fits for one channel come from one
series and are not independent---gives
\begin{equation}
  \hat\beta = 1.033, \qquad \mathrm{SE} = 0.036, \qquad
  \text{95\% CI } [0.963,\, 1.104].
  \label{eq:beta_estimate}
\end{equation}
This rejects the $1.36$ of earlier drafts ($t = -9.1$,
$p = 2.6 \times 10^{-11}$), is consistent with the $\approx 1$ reported
throughout the Hawkes-in-finance literature ($t = +0.9$, $p = 0.36$), and
rejects the $\approx 1.6$ that the rough-volatility correspondence would imply
($t = -15.8$, $p = 8.4 \times 10^{-19}$).

We report one disagreement rather than suppress it. Rebuilding the
\emph{self}-kernel from raw data (Section~\ref{sec:kernel_repro}) gives
$\hat\beta$ between $1.43$ and $1.58$ across seven specifications, with
standard errors of $0.06$ to $0.23$---materially steeper than the cross-kernel
estimate above. The two are different objects (own-lag versus cross-channel
response) measured on different panels (an archived operator versus a
rebuild), so the gap is uninformative rather than contradictory. It does mean
the paper cannot assert a single exponent, and Section~\ref{sec:rough} is
written accordingly.

If the kernel is genuinely power-law---that is, scale-free---then the
quadrature reading of Section~\ref{sec:har_quadrature} makes three predictions
that are not made by the conventional reading of HAR as a
daily/weekly/monthly cascade. All three are testable on the shared panel, and
all three are confirmed.

\paragraph{Prediction 1: a scale-free kernel wants a scale-free grid, and
refining the grid pays.} Uniform knots in $\log$-lag are the natural quadrature
for a power law, and halving the geometric base doubles the knot density per
decade. Error should therefore fall monotonically as the base falls.
Table~\ref{tab:ladder} reports the measured ladder.

The ordering is monotone in the base across all six bases---the rank
correlation between base and QLIKE is exactly $+1.000$, with no
inversions---and DM $t = -10.6$ for $b{=}2$ against the incumbent $b{=}5$. The
signature is
precisely that of a quadrature error: \emph{how finely the kernel is sampled
matters, where the sum is truncated barely does}. This is difficult to explain
if the rungs were recovering genuinely distinct daily, weekly and monthly
components, and easy to explain if they are knots on one smooth curve. We note
that the conventional explicit HAR structure is not merely suboptimal but
\emph{worse than the incumbent}, at DM $t = +3.1$.

\paragraph{Prediction 2: power law, not exponential.} A single exponential
kernel fitted in place of the power law is $+0.011$ QLIKE worse---roughly an
order of magnitude larger than any effect elsewhere in this paper. The kernel
shape agrees: fitting $\log\varphi$ against $\log u$ (power law) and against
$u$ (exponential) on the same points gives $R^2 = 0.80$ and $0.44$
respectively, with the power law preferred by AIC. This is the one prediction
of the three that the fitted shape supports without qualification.

\paragraph{Prediction 3: truncation is real, and it is an interior optimum.}
Direct power-law convolution features degrade sharply when the convolution
window is extended too far, while the boxcar ladder's cap is a near-non-lever.
Per-bar Diebold--Mariano tests on the campaign tile
(Section~\ref{sec:tile_inference}) resolve the direct-feature side of this in
both directions: a truncation at $U = 256$ beats $U = 32$ by $0.0074$
($t = -4.16$), beats $U = 128$ by $0.0026$ ($t = -3.50$), and beats no
truncation at all by $0.0077$ ($t = -3.83$), every comparison surviving Holm
adjustment across the contrast family. Truncating the kernel is therefore not a
computational convenience---removing it costs real accuracy---and the optimum
is interior rather than at either extreme.

We attribute the contrast with the boxcar ladder's indifference to its cap to
normalisation: the direct features normalise weights to sum to one, so
extending $U$ redistributes mass away from recent lags, whereas adding a boxcar
rung leaves the other rungs undisturbed. That reconciliation remains an
argument rather than a measurement.

\subsection{The Hawkes Reading}
\label{sec:hawkes}

Equation~\eqref{eq:convolution} with a power-law $\varphi$ is, in discrete
time, the conditional-intensity equation of a self-exciting point process
\citep{Hawkes1971}. This subsection makes the correspondence explicit, states
what it buys, and---at equal length---states where it does not yet hold.

\subsubsection{From point process to variance regression}

Let $N$ be a linear Hawkes process with conditional intensity
\begin{equation}
  \lambda(t) \;=\; \mu \;+\; \int_{-\infty}^{t}\varphi(t-s)\,dN(s),
  \qquad \varphi \ge 0,
  \label{eq:hawkes_intensity}
\end{equation}
and let the price be a pure-jump process driven by $N$. Realized variance over
a bar is then, up to the squared jump size, the event count over that bar, so
that $RV_t \propto N(t-\Delta, t)$ and
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[RV_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]
  \;\propto\; \int_{\text{bar}} \mathbb{E}[\lambda(s)]\,ds
  \;=\; \tilde{\mu} + \sum_{u \ge 1} \bar{\varphi}(u)\, RV_{t-u},
  \label{eq:hawkes_to_rv}
\end{equation}
where $\bar\varphi$ is $\varphi$ aggregated to the bar grid. Equation
\eqref{eq:hawkes_to_rv} is Equation~\eqref{eq:convolution}. The correspondence
is therefore not an analogy: for a linear Hawkes process the conditional
intensity is exactly linear in the observed past, so a regression of counts on
past counts identifies $\varphi$ itself rather than a proxy for it. What we
estimate is a bar-aggregated Hawkes kernel, subject to the caveats of
Section~\ref{sec:kernel_caveats}.

\subsubsection{The branching ratio is already estimated}

The central quantity of Hawkes theory is the branching ratio
$n = \int_0^\infty \varphi(u)\,du$: the expected number of direct offspring per
event, equivalently the fraction of activity that is endogenously generated.
Stationarity requires $n < 1$, and $n \to 1$ is the ``critical reflexivity''
regime that has been the subject of an extended empirical debate
\citep{HardimanBercotBouchaud2013, FilimonovSornette2012}.

For the boxcar parameterisation of Equation~\eqref{eq:har_boxcar} there is an
exact identity. Substituting Equation~\eqref{eq:eff_kernel},
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{u=1}^{r_{\max}} \varphi(u)
  \;=\; \sum_{u}\sum_{k\,:\,r_k \ge u} \frac{w_k}{r_k}
  \;=\; \sum_k \frac{w_k}{r_k}\,\bigl|\{u : 1 \le u \le r_k\}\bigr|
  \;=\; \sum_k w_k .
  \label{eq:branching_identity}
\end{equation}
\emph{The sum of the fitted HAR coefficients is the discretised kernel
integral.} We verify the identity numerically to machine precision
($-0.038000$ against $-0.038000$, error $2.5 \times 10^{-15}$), so every fitted
ladder yields a branching-ratio analogue at no additional computational cost.

Computing it is not kind to the structural reading.
Table~\ref{tab:hawkes_kernel} reports $\hat n$ pooled and by session. The
pooled value is $\hat n = -0.038$: \textbf{negative}, and therefore outside the
admissible range $[0, 1)$ entirely rather than merely sub-critical. Across
sessions it ranges from $+0.020$ overnight to $-0.260$ in the after-hours
session, so its \emph{sign} is not even stable. Whatever this quantity is
measuring, it is not a branching ratio, and we do not report a reflexivity
estimate. Section~\ref{sec:kernel_caveats} argues that the estimation design,
not the market, is the reason.

We flag one consistency point in our favour. The reflexivity debate turns on
whether high estimates of $n$ are artifacts of an unmodelled time-varying
background intensity $\mu(t)$: \citet{FilimonovSornette2012} argue that
ignoring intraday seasonality biases $\hat n$ upward toward one. Our Stage-1
diurnal adjustment (Section~\ref{sec:diurnal}) is exactly the $\mu(t)$
correction that this critique demands, adopted originally for an unrelated
reason. Section~\ref{sec:kernel_caveats} argues that it may be too aggressive
in the other direction.

\subsubsection{Two existing negative results become predictions}

The Hawkes reading retro-predicts two of the paper's otherwise awkward
findings.

\paragraph{Bounded nonlinearity.} The quadratic Hawkes model of
\citet{BlancDonierBouchaud2017} makes the feedback a quadratic form in the
past rather than a linear functional, generating volatility clustering and fat
tails endogenously and capturing the Zumbach effect that linear ARCH-type
feedback misses. What it predicts about extractable structure is specific:
real nonlinearity, \emph{additive plus pairwise, and no more}. That is
precisely the saturation reported in Section~\ref{sec:saturation}---the
recoverable nonlinearity is additive-plus-pairwise anchored to the session
clock, and deeper capacity does not pay. A result that reads as a puzzling
ceiling under a generic machine-learning framing reads as a confirmation under
this one.

\paragraph{A low-rank operator.} A multivariate Hawkes process has a matrix
kernel $\Phi_{ij}(u)$, and a low-rank factorisation
$\Phi_{ij}(u) \approx \sum_k a_k\, u^{(k)}_i v^{(k)}_j g_k(u)$ is the natural
structural restriction on it. The operator result---41 exogenous channels
sharing a small number of lag profiles, with quasi-static geometry and fast
amplitude motion---is such a factorisation, and it is measurable directly. Across the six session bins, the
$41 \times 12$ exogenous operator needs only \textbf{4 to 6} singular
components to carry 90\% of its energy, with the leading component taking
28--58\%. The rank-5 structure the paper reports is therefore a property of the
operator and not of any particular estimation window. Nonparametric estimation
of multivariate Hawkes kernels and their low-rank structure is an established
problem \citep{BacryGaiffasMuzy2015, AchabBacryGaiffas2017}, which gives this
part of the analysis a comparison class it currently lacks.

We attach the same caveat as above, and it is a serious one for this reading:
close to half of the cross-kernel cells are negative, so the operator is a
contrast---each channel compared against a reference past---rather than a
matrix of excitation kernels. The low-rank finding is robust; its
interpretation as a multivariate Hawkes kernel matrix is not.

\subsection{Where the Reading Does Not Yet Hold}
\label{sec:kernel_caveats}

Three discrepancies stand between the correspondence above and a defensible
structural claim. We state them here rather than in a limitations section
because two of them plausibly share a single cause, and that cause is our own
preprocessing.

\input{stats/tab_hawkes_kernel}

\paragraph{(i) The archived kernel takes negative values.} A linear Hawkes
kernel must be non-negative; Equation~\eqref{eq:hawkes_intensity} does not
otherwise define a point process. On the archived coefficient vector,
$\varphi$ violates this badly: 8 of 12 lags are negative, carrying 10.3\% of
total absolute kernel mass, rising to 87.8\% at the open and 86.3\% after
hours. The archived exogenous cross-kernels are no better---between 44.7\% and
50.4\% of all (channel, lag) cells are negative, indistinguishable from a coin
flip. The archived integrated kernel is $\hat n = -0.038$, outside the
$[0,1)$ range a linear Hawkes process admits rather than merely sub-critical,
and its sign is not stable across sessions.

\paragraph{(ii) None of it survives a rebuild from source.}
Section~\ref{sec:kernel_repro} reports the attempt. Rebuilding the same nominal
specification from the committed raw data with the committed transform code
reproduces the panel size exactly but not the kernel, and the rebuilt kernel is
well behaved on every diagnostic that the archived one fails. We therefore do
not attribute the sign structure to the market, to the diurnal filter, or to
the partial-versus-marginal distinction. We attribute it to the archived
artefact, whose construction is not recoverable from this repository, and we
withdraw the structural reading that rested on it.

\subsection{Reproducibility of the Kernel Estimate}
\label{sec:kernel_repro}

The diagnostics above are computed on a coefficient vector recovered from an
archived driver log. The panel that produced it, an intermediate memory-mapped
feature cache, was not retained. We therefore rebuilt the estimate from the
committed raw data using the committed transform code, on the same nominal
specification---base-2 ladder to 2048, unit-ridge probe, 41 exogenous channels,
3{,}125-bar burn-in---and ran the two experiments that were supposed to
separate the suspected causes of the sign structure.

\paragraph{The rebuild does not reproduce the archived kernel.} It matches the
panel size exactly at $242{,}934$ rows, and disagrees on everything else. The
coefficient vectors correlate at only $+0.65$; the archived integrated kernel
is $-0.038$ while the rebuilt one is $+1.000$. Neither the raw-ladder scale nor
the per-standard-deviation scale reconciles them. The likely cause is visible
in the archived driver: it partitions its feature cache into a ladder block and
an ``other'' block, and reads the self-kernel out of the latter, which holds the
HAR ladder \emph{alongside} calendar terms and engineered features. A
self-kernel conditioned on those is a different estimand, and we cannot
reconstruct that conditioning set.

\input{stats/tab_kernel_experiments}

\paragraph{On the rebuilt panel, the kernel is well behaved.}
Table~\ref{tab:kernel_experiments} reports the $2 \times 2$ that was meant to
separate the diurnal filter from the partial-versus-marginal distinction, plus
a winsorization arm. Across all seven cells the integrated kernel lies between
$+0.906$ and $+1.079$---sub-critical to marginally critical, which is the
regime the reflexivity literature reports---and negative lags carry between
0.0\% and 0.4\% of absolute mass. Neither suspected cause moves these
diagnostics much: dropping the exogenous block changes $\hat n$ from $+1.000$
to $+0.927$, and replacing the rolling divisor with 48 slot dummies changes it
from $+1.000$ to $+1.079$.

The experiment therefore returns a negative answer to the question it was
designed to ask, and a more useful answer to a question we had not asked. The
sign pathology is not a consequence of the diurnal filter, and not a
consequence of conditioning on the exogenous block. It is a property of an
artefact we cannot rebuild.

\paragraph{Consequences.} Three, and we would rather state them than work
around them. First, no structural claim about the kernel---branching ratio,
criticality, reflexivity, inhibition---is supported by this paper, and we make
none. Second, the exponent is not settled either: the archived exogenous
cross-kernels give $1.033 \pm 0.036$ while the rebuilt self-kernel gives
$1.43$--$1.58$ with standard errors of $0.06$--$0.23$, and these are different
objects on different panels, so the disagreement is uninformative rather than
contradictory. Third, the quadrature results of
Section~\ref{sec:powerlaw}---which are the load-bearing part of this
section---are untouched, because they come from the frozen forecast battery and
not from any kernel estimate.

\subsection{Relation to Rough Volatility}
\label{sec:rough}

Nearly unstable Hawkes processes with heavy-tailed kernels converge, under
rescaling, to rough fractional processes \citep{JaissonRosenbaum2016}, which is
the microstructural route to the rough-volatility models of
\citet{GatheralJaissonRosenbaum2018} and to rough Heston
\citep{ElEuchRosenbaum2019}. In that limit a kernel tail exponent $1+\alpha$
corresponds to a Hurst parameter $H = \alpha - \tfrac{1}{2}$, so the
$H \approx 0.1$ typically reported for volatility corresponds to a tail near
$1.6$.

Earlier drafts, working from the unestimated $1.36$, reported our exponent as
sitting closer to that rough-volatility value than to the Hawkes-kernel
literature's $\approx 1$. Estimation does not support that reading, but neither
does it cleanly support the opposite one. The cross-kernel estimate,
$\hat\beta = 1.033$ with a 95\% interval of $[0.963, 1.104]$, rejects $1.6$ and
is consistent with $1.0$; the rebuilt self-kernel estimate, $1.43$--$1.58$,
points the other way. Under the $H = \alpha - \tfrac{1}{2}$ mapping the two
imply $H \approx 0.03$ and $H \approx 0.5$ respectively---the first rougher
than the $H \approx 0.1$ usually reported, the second not rough at all. We
conclude that our data do not currently locate the volatility process on this
axis, and we decline to place it.

The practical consequence for the benchmark set is unchanged and if anything
strengthened: a fractional-kernel forecaster is a rival in the same power-law
family as our backbone, our measured exponent puts us in the same corner of
that family as the Hawkes literature, and the battery still does not contain
one. \pending{fractional/RFSV arm on the shared panel}

a genuine gap: a fractional-kernel forecaster is a \emph{rival in the same

cheap to add, since the direct power-law convolution machinery of

\subsection{The Clock Organ}
\label{sec:clock}

A Hawkes process of the form \eqref{eq:hawkes_intensity} is time-homogeneous.
Intraday realized variance emphatically is not, and the calendar block is the
single largest organ we measure ($-0.0023$ QLIKE, on the organ-ablation cache
rather than the frozen battery). The backbone is therefore
kernel $\times$ clock rather than kernel alone.

The clock does more than scale the kernel. Session-conditional estimation shows
the target kernel \emph{rotating} through the day---cosine similarity to the
pooled kernel of $+0.83$ overnight against $-0.55$ at the open
(Table~\ref{tab:hawkes_kernel})---so the geometry deforms at session
transitions rather than merely breathing in amplitude.

We can now say how much. Stacking the six session kernels as a $6 \times 12$
matrix, the leading singular value carries only \textbf{43.8\%} of the
variance, and three components are needed for 84\%. Pure amplitude motion on a
fixed shape would put essentially all of it in the first component. The session
variation of the \emph{self}-kernel is therefore a genuine rotation, not a
breathing amplitude---a distinction that matters because the exogenous
operator, by contrast, does behave as the amplitude story predicts
(Section~\ref{sec:hawkes}). The two objects move differently through the day,
and only the exogenous one has quasi-static geometry.

This is the hook that Section~\ref{sec:saturation} pays off: the nonlinear
structure that survives is anchored to exactly these transitions.

\subsection{Summary: The Instrument}
\label{sec:backbone_summary}

The backbone is a truncated power-law self-kernel with a clock organ, of which
the base-5 HAR ladder is a coarse quadrature. The quadrature evidence is
solid: it comes from the frozen forecast battery, it is monotone across six
bases, and it does not depend on any kernel estimate. The kernel estimates
themselves are not: the archived vector fails every linear-Hawkes diagnostic, a
rebuild from raw data fails none of them, and the two cannot be reconciled
(Section~\ref{sec:kernel_repro}). We therefore carry the power-law backbone
forward as an instrument, carry the Hawkes correspondence forward only as a
source of hypotheses, and make no structural claim. Three consequences follow:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The incumbent is quadrature-limited.} The base-5 ladder used
    as this paper's shared benchmark is beaten by base-2 OLS by $0.00082$
    QLIKE at DM $t = -10.6$, with no change in information set, model class, or
    tuning. Section~\ref{sec:benchmark_debt} accounts for what this does to
    every increment reported downstream.
  \item \textbf{The natural basis is a mixture of decay exponents.} Direct
    power-law convolution features $K_\beta(t) = \sum_u u^{-\beta} y_{t-u}$,
    normalised, span the same structure as the boxcar ladder but are
    parameterised by decay exponent rather than window length---the natural
    coordinate if the kernel reading is correct, and freshness-robust in a way
    boxcars are not (a 3{,}125-wide rolling mean weights a 65-day-old bar
    exactly as heavily as yesterday's).
  \item \textbf{Two later negative results are predictions, not surprises.}
    Bounded additive-plus-pairwise nonlinearity and a low-rank exogenous
    response operator are what the quadratic and multivariate extensions of
    this backbone respectively predict, and the rank is measured at 4--6 of 41
    channels. Both remain consistency arguments: we fit no self-exciting model,
    and until the kernel estimate is reproducible there is no stable object to
    fit one to.
\end{enumerate}
