\title{How many dimensions does a twelve-rung HAR ladder carry?\\
       \large Method note: one column matches twelve, and the rank-3 win was
       one window}
\author{}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A base-2 HAR ladder holds twelve moving averages of one series at horizons
$\{1,2,\dots,2048\}$. We ask how many of those twelve are doing work. The
answer is one. A single fixed column---no estimation, no fitted direction---
matches all twelve rungs, by three independent routes that agree: a
nine-window placebo averages $+0.00001$, the pooled estimated direction gives
$-0.00054$ ($t=-1.33$), and the best \emph{a priori} shape gives $+0.00034$
($t=+1.65$). The mechanism is that the ladder's effective rank is $2.81$ of
$12$ with $72.8\%$ of its variance in the top direction, so eleven of the
twelve columns are near-duplicates. Which single column works is governed by
one parameter. Because rung $j$ is a block mean of the last $2^j$ squared
returns, a weight $w_j \propto L_j^{-\alpha}$ over rungs induces a kernel
$\kappa(i) \propto i^{-(\alpha+1)}$ over \emph{raw} lags, so $\alpha$ is the
long-memory parameter $d$; kernels decaying slower than $i^{-1.35}$ fail in
$0$--$2$ eras of $8$, and everything from $d \approx 0.4$ upward ties. That
optimum agrees with the standard estimate for realised variance, which is
independent corroboration rather than a fitted result. An earlier version of
this note reported that \emph{three} supervised coefficients beat twelve rungs
by $0.00080$. That claim is withdrawn on evidence far stronger than the
original doubt: repeating the identical procedure on the eight estimation
windows nobody chose puts all eight behind the full ladder in a tight
distribution (mean $+0.00022$, sd $0.00016$), with the published window
$13.5$ standard deviations outside it. It is not a chance draw, not curvature,
and not a discoverable feature---every imposed shape fails, including one
hand-copied from the winning window's own loadings. \S\ref{sec:corrections}
lists five statements this note previously made that were wrong.
\end{abstract}

\section{What a weight over rungs means on the raw lags}
\label{sec:kernel}

This is the conceptual core and it is elementary, but it was not stated in the
earlier note and it reframes every result that follows.

Rung $j$ is the block mean $M_j = L_j^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{L_j} y^2_{t-i}$ with
$L_j = 2^j$. A weighted sum of rungs is therefore a quadrature rule on the lag
axis with piecewise-constant weights, and in the variance domain the map is
exact:
\[
  \sum_j w_j M_j \;=\; \sum_i \kappa(i)\, y^2_{t-i},
  \qquad
  \kappa(i) \;=\; \sum_{j\,:\,2^j \ge i} \frac{w_j}{2^j}.
\]
Each raw lag $i$ is collected by every rung long enough to contain it,
discounted by that rung's own $1/L_j$. Setting $w_j \propto L_j^{-\alpha}$
makes the inner sum geometric with ratio $2^{-(\alpha+1)}$ from
$j_0 = \lceil \log_2 i\rceil$, giving
\[
  \kappa(i) \;=\; \frac{2^{-j_0(\alpha+1)}}{1-2^{-(\alpha+1)}}
             \;\propto\; i^{-(\alpha+1)} .
\]
\textbf{A power law of exponent $\alpha$ over rungs is a power law of exponent
$\alpha+1$ over raw lags}; the extra power is the $1/L_j$ normalisation of the
block means. Since an ARFIMA$(0,d,0)$ has $\mathrm{AR}(\infty)$ weights
decaying as $i^{-(d+1)}$, the rung exponent \emph{is} the long-memory
parameter.

The identity is asymptotic. With twelve rungs truncated at 2048 the measured
exponent runs above $\alpha+1$, because truncation removes long-lag weight and
steepens the effective decay---most where small $\alpha$ puts the most mass:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{rrrr}
\toprule
$\alpha$ & predicted $\alpha+1$ & measured & half-weight rung \\
\midrule
$0.00$ & $1.00$ & $1.189$ & 32 \\
$0.25$ & $1.25$ & $1.389$ & 8 \\
$0.50$ & $1.50$ & $1.597$ & 2 \\
$1.00$ & $2.00$ & $2.032$ & 1 \\
$2.00$ & $3.00$ & $2.946$ & 1 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent
So the \emph{measured} exponent is the quantity to read, not the nominal
$\alpha$. The difference matters: it moves the implied $d$ at the optimum from
$0.25$ to $\approx 0.4$.

\subsection{Why: the ladder is not twelve-dimensional}

The rungs are nested moving averages of a single series, so they are close to
duplicates. Averaged over the nine estimation windows, the ladder covariance
spectrum is
\[
  0.728,\ 0.128,\ 0.057,\ 0.030,\ 0.018,\ 0.011,\ 0.008,\ 0.006,\ 0.005,\
  0.004,\ 0.003,\ 0.002
\]
in variance shares---an entropy effective rank of $\mathbf{2.81}$ of $12$, with
$72.8\%$ in the top direction. Discarding eleven columns discards little.

Some of the tie is also a genuine variance saving:
$\operatorname{tr}((A'A)^{-1})$ is $9.33\times 10^{-2}$ at rank 12 against
$6.54\times 10^{-4}$ at rank 1, a factor of $143$ from collinearity. Mean
in-sample prediction variance still scales only as $p/n$ ($22$ against $11$ over
$24{,}000$ rows), so the bias--variance trade here is real but bounded, not the
main effect.

\section{Retraction: the rank-3 win was one window}
\label{sec:retraction}

The earlier note reported three supervised coefficients beating twelve rungs by
$0.00080$ ($t=-3.30$), with the projection estimated once on rows $[0,W)$ and
applied through 2024. Four tests, run in this order, close it.

\subsection{Refit cadence does not rescue it, and there is no drift to track}

Rebuilding the basis causally from the trailing window loses at every cadence
($+0.00023$ to $+0.00027$). Sweeping an exponential halflife on the moments the
basis is built from traces the whole bias--variance curve between a fully local
refit and an expanding window that never forgets:

The $h=\infty$ arm is the control the original design lacked. Its information
set strictly \emph{contains} the freeze's at every boundary past the first, and
with no drift more data cannot give a worse estimate. It forecasts $0.00098$
worse.

\subsection{Nothing imposed recovers it}
\label{sec:whyk0}

Why is window $k{=}0$ different? It is the flattest long-horizon stretch in the
sample: smallest minimum eigenvalue of any window ($7.78\times10^{-4}$ against
$1.15$--$4.23\times10^{-3}$) and flattest long rungs
($\mathrm{sd}(2048)/\mathrm{sd}(1) = 0.124$ against $0.17$--$0.36$). So
$C^{-1}$ amplifies the low-variance tail hardest there, and only that window
manufactures a long-memory contrast. Note $\operatorname{cond}(C)$ is
\emph{not} the discriminator ($412$ against $331$--$408$).

If that contrast were a real feature, imposing one would find it. It does not:

One arm survives and it is the subject of \S\ref{sec:rank1}: $k{=}0$'s first

beating the ladder) and $k{=}0$ is only $1.1$ sd from its mean---best of nine
but an ordinary draw. \textbf{The rank-1 edge of that window is chance; the
rank-3 triple is not.} The two ranks give opposite verdicts, and that is the
resolution.

\section{Corrections to the earlier note}
\label{sec:corrections}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
  \item \textbf{``The compression claim is withdrawn''} was too broad. The
        \emph{rank-3 superiority} claim is withdrawn. The compression claim is
        not: one column matches twelve rungs, by three routes
        (\S\ref{sec:rank1}).
  \item \textbf{``Direction 1 is stable to $1$--$2^{\circ}$''} was wrong as
        stated. That figure is the smallest principal angle between
        three-dimensional \emph{subspaces}. The direction-1 \emph{vectors}
        differ across windows by $30.1^{\circ}$ on average ($13.9$ to
        $42.8$). The subspaces share a direction; the first Krylov vectors do
        not.
  \item \textbf{Coefficient variance is not negligible at rank 12.}
        $\operatorname{tr}((A'A)^{-1})$ is $143\times$ larger there than at
        rank 1. The trade is bounded, not absent.
  \item \textbf{The $r^{-1}$ imposed decay endorsed elsewhere in this project
        is the second-worst rank-1 arm} ($+0.00989$). The shape that ties the
        ladder decays far more slowly.
  \item \textbf{``It is a property of the estimator, not a
        three-dimensional structure in the data''} was right in conclusion and
        wrong in evidence. The seed ladder alone cannot distinguish an
        estimator artifact from an unreproducible feature; the placebo can, and
        it shows the published window is an outlier rather than a draw.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
Two conceded caveats from the earlier note still stand: the frozen arm moves
by $0.00021$ across implementations differing only in NaN handling, and this is
not a reproduction of the battery (different training clip and panel).

\section{The pre-registered test}
\label{sec:prereg}

The $-0.00032$ of \S\ref{sec:rank1} is an argmin over eleven shapes and cannot
be reported. The fix is a split, not a correction factor: twenty-one candidate
shapes are scored on rows $[24{,}000, 130{,}000)$, the best is taken, and that
\emph{one} shape is then tested on rows $[130{,}000, n)$. The regions are
disjoint, so no row that chose the shape judges it, and one arm tested once has
no multiplicity to correct. Widening the grid from eleven to twenty-one costs
nothing here, because the reported statistic never sees the grid.

\subsection{What this does and does not establish}

\textbf{It passes.} One fixed, estimation-free column beats twelve rungs on
rows never used to choose it, by $-0.00111$, robust to era deletion and to
bandwidth out to $100$. At the widest bandwidths the statistic is marginal
($p \approx 0.05$) and two of three bootstrap intervals touch zero, so this is
a solid result rather than an overwhelming one.

\textbf{But the sign is era-conditional, and that is the more useful finding.}
The selection half favours the \emph{ladder} by $+0.00054$; the test half
favours the \emph{column} by $-0.00111$. Those reconcile exactly with the
pooled number of \S\ref{sec:rank1}:
\[
  \frac{106{,}000 \times (+0.00054) + 112{,}909 \times (-0.00111)}{218{,}909}
  \;=\; -0.00031
  \qquad\text{against } -0.00032 \text{ measured.}
\]
So the full-sample $-0.00032$ is not a small uniform edge. It is a
$+0.00054$ in the first half netted against a $-0.00111$ in the second, and
reporting only the pooled figure would have hidden a sign change.

\textbf{Had the split run the other way, the pre-registered test would have
failed.} We state this rather than leave it implicit: a single split is itself
an arbitrary choice, which is cause five of \S\ref{sec:corrections} in a new
costume. What rescues the exercise is that selection did not \emph{need} the
sign---no shape won on the selection region, so the winner cannot have been
chosen for winning. Only the \emph{ordering} of shapes transferred across the
split, and it transferred cleanly.

The claim we are willing to defend is therefore conditional and precise: in the
second half of this sample, one fixed column with induced lag kernel decaying
near $i^{-1.9}$ beats a twelve-rung ladder; across the full sample the two are
a tie; and in the first half the ladder wins. The compression is real
throughout---one column is never far from twelve---but which side of the tie it
falls on depends on the era.
