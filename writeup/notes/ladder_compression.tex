\title{Is the twelve-rung HAR ladder three-dimensional?\\
       \large Method note: supervised rank-3 compression against the full ladder}
\author{}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We ask whether the twelve rungs of a base-2 HAR ladder carry twelve degrees of
freedom or fewer. Every arm keeps the same twelve lags $\{1,2,4,\dots,2048\}$
and differs only in how many shape coefficients the ladder is allowed. Under
fixed \emph{a priori} bases the ladder does not compress. Under a supervised
basis it does: three coefficients in twelve columns beat all twelve rungs in
twenty-one columns by $0.00059$ QLIKE ($t=-3.30$), with an interior optimum at
$r=3$. Principal components at the same rank are the worst arm tested. The
result is not yet robustness-tested and the projection is estimated from a
single frozen window; \S\ref{sec:caveats} states what must be conceded.
\end{abstract}

\section{Panel and rows}

Cache \texttt{results/b2\_mmap\_warm}, the corrected-fill build: availability
recorded \emph{before} the forward fill, a 26-bar staleness bound, scale
estimated from observed rows only, and a warm-up guard on feed initiation.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item 242{,}934 bars on a 30-minute grid, Sunday 18:30 through Friday 20:00,
        2005-01-02 to 2024-04-30.
  \item Training window $W = 24{,}000$ bars; the walk begins at bar 24{,}000.
  \item 218{,}934 predictions per arm; 218{,}909 scored after dropping bars
        whose truth is non-positive or whose features are non-finite.
  \item The scored row set is \emph{identical} across arms, so every
        comparison is bar-for-bar.
\end{itemize}

\section{Target}

\texttt{robust\_transform(df, "RV")} chains

\[
  \text{diurnal adjustment} \;\longrightarrow\;
  \text{\texttt{sqrt} semantic transform} \;\longrightarrow\;
  \text{rolling winsorize}
\]

The diurnal step is multiplicative and returns the baseline $b$. Winsorization
uses a 240-bar rolling window at the \textbf{1/99} quantiles---the interior
optimum measured under a fixed truth, where the project default of 5/95 costs
$0.00304$ and removing the clip entirely does not help. The model trains on the
transformed target $y$; raw variance is reconstructed as $y^{2}b$.

Scoring is on the \textbf{clipped 5/95} footing, $y_{5/95}^{2}\,b$, which is the
footing the paper's battery uses and where its OLS HAR base-5 plus calendar
sits at $0.13415$.

\section{Features}

Let $s = y^{2}$ be the bar-level adjusted variance. For each rung
$L \in \{1,2,4,\dots,2048\}$,

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
H[:, k] = sqrt( s.rolling(L, min_periods=L).mean().shift(1) )
\end{lstlisting}

The \texttt{.shift(1)} is the causality guard: bar $t$'s feature uses data only
through $t-1$. Nine calendar columns are appended---\texttt{DOW\_0..4},
\texttt{hour}, \texttt{is\_overnight}, \texttt{is\_open},
\texttt{is\_close}. \textbf{No exogenous channels are used.}

\section{The two arms}

Both designs are $[\,H G,\ \text{cal}\,]$ with an intercept; only $G$ differs.

\paragraph{Baseline, \texttt{base-2 (r=12)}.} $G = I_{12}$, giving
$12 + 9 = 21$ columns plus intercept---the untouched ladder.

\paragraph{Compressed, \texttt{sup r=3}.} $G \in \mathbb{R}^{12\times 3}$,
giving $3 + 9 = 12$ columns plus intercept. $G$ is built on rows $[0,W)$
\emph{only}:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
C  = cov(H[:W])                       # 12 x 12 ladder covariance
xy = (H[:W]-mean).T @ (y[:W]-mean)/W  # ladder-target cross-covariance
d0 = solve(C, xy)                     # the OLS coefficient direction
fam = [d0, C@d0, C@C@d0, ...]         # Krylov space K_r(C, d0)
G   = gram_schmidt(fam)[:r]
\end{lstlisting}

This is a \textbf{Krylov / PLS-type subspace}, not a generic ``supervised
projection'', and the distinction matters for how the result should be
described. At $r=1$ it is the frozen OLS coefficient direction. Because
$C d_0 = xy$, for $r \ge 2$ the span contains both the OLS direction and the
raw cross-covariance direction.

\section{Estimator: per-bar OLS, exact}

Both arms refit at \emph{every bar}, with no penalty, matching the battery's
convention rather than this project's usual 1{,}000-bar cadence. A window step
changes $X^{\top}X$ by rank two---one row leaves, one enters---so the inverse is
updated in $O(p^{2})$ rather than re-solved in $O(p^{3})$:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
cf  = Ainv @ c
u = A[t-W]; v = A[t]                    # row leaving, row entering
Ainv += outer(Au,Au) / (1 - u.T@Ainv@u) # remove
Ainv -= outer(Av,Av) / (1 + v.T@Ainv@v) # add
c = c - u*y[t-W] + v*y[t]
\end{lstlisting}

At $p \approx 20$ this is roughly 500 flops per bar, so all 218{,}934 refits
cost less than a single 1{,}000-bar walk of a wide design. The fastest method
here is also the correct protocol, which is why it is worth the care.

Rank-2 updating accumulates floating-point error, so the inverse is recomputed
exactly every 5{,}000 bars and the worst observed relative drift is reported:
$1.3\times10^{-12}$ for \texttt{sup}, $1.0\times10^{-13}$ for the baseline. The
fast path is exact, not approximately exact.

\section{Prediction and scoring}

\begin{align*}
  \widehat{RV}_t &= \Bigl( (A_t \hat\beta)^{2}
                   + \tfrac{1}{W}\textstyle\sum_i r_i^{2} \Bigr) b_t
                   &&\text{(Duan smearing)}\\[2pt]
  \mathrm{QLIKE} &= \rho - \log \rho - 1,
                   \qquad \rho = RV_t / \widehat{RV}_t
\end{align*}

Inference is Diebold--Mariano with a Newey--West HAC variance at the
rule-of-thumb bandwidth and the Harvey--Leybourne--Newbold small-sample
correction. Eras are eight equal blocks of the scored index.

\section{Result}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
arm & lag dims & cols & QLIKE & vs base-2 \\
\midrule
\texttt{base-2 (r=12)} & 12 & 21 & $0.13438$ & --- \\
\textbf{\texttt{sup r=3}} & \textbf{3} & \textbf{12} & $\mathbf{0.13379}$ &
  $\mathbf{-0.00059}$ ($t=-3.30$) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent
Three coefficients beat twelve rungs. The \texttt{sup} curve has an
\emph{interior} optimum rather than a monotone preference for fewer columns:
$-0.00017$, $-0.00024$, $\mathbf{-0.00059}$ at $r=3$, then $+0.00031$ and
$+0.00032$ at $r=4$ and $r=6$. Getting worse past $r=3$ is what makes this a
statement about dimension.

For context, the fixed bases at $r=3$ all lose: power $+0.00128$,
log-polynomial $+0.00108$, and principal components $+0.00135$---the worst of
the four. Unsupervised compression of the ladder is actively harmful, matching
the exogenous block, where a supervised projection beat the 516-dimensional
ambient view while principal components and Laplacian eigenmaps lost. Variance
directions and prediction directions are different objects here, and only the
second compresses.

\section{What must be conceded}
\label{sec:caveats}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
  \item \textbf{$G$ is frozen from 2005--2007 and applied through 2024}, never
        refit---seventeen years stale at the end of the sample. This is the
        single largest assumption in the design.
  \item \textbf{$G$ is estimated}, so \texttt{sup} carries three fitted
        directions of complexity the baseline does not. It is estimated on rows
        disjoint from every scored bar, so it is not leakage; it is not free
        either.
  \item \textbf{$t=-3.30$ rests on the rule-of-thumb HAC bandwidth}, which has
        produced two false positives elsewhere in this project. The margin has
        not been tested by block bootstrap, leave-one-era-out, or a bandwidth
        sweep.
  \item \textbf{This is not a reproduction of the battery.} Base-2 should land
        near $0.13333$, being $0.00082$ better than base-5's $0.13415$, and
        $0.13438$ is $0.00105$ the wrong way. The training clip is 1/99 rather
        than 5/95 and the panel differs. The agreement confirms the footing and
        nothing more.
\end{enumerate}
