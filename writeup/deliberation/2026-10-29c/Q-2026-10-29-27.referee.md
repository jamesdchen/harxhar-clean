## Answer

**Replace the sentence: yes. Replace it with the D18 contrast as worded: no.**

- **Why the current sentence must go.** "The series a model uses most are not the ones that make it more accurate" is false, by group and by series (Evidence 1).
- **Why not the D18 sentence.** "The six groups beyond HAR + RM + FOMC hold 20-23% of the trees' attribution but give 4-11% of the gain" has correct numbers but does not support the claim it would carry. In LightGBM and XGBoost the realized moments show the same pattern: the same share of attribution, and the same removal cost as the six groups together. C5's own table prints the two rows one above the other. The contrast is the mark of two substitutes, each of which removal makes look small. It is not evidence that attribution misleads about the six groups.
- **What the sentence should say.** The one claim that survives every measure is the FOMC calendar's, consistent with Q-26.

- **Add-alone.** In the boosted trees, each of liquidity, EW and VW alone lowers the loss about as much as RM does: -2.8% to -3.5% against -3.0% to -3.4%. In the forest RM is ahead: -3.2% against -2.3% to -2.5%.

**3. What a JFEc referee would say about the D18 sentence.**

> "Your RM row has the same attribution share and the same removal cost as your 'six together' row (Table rh_fi_reconcile). By your own yardstick RM is also 'used but not accurate'. What you show is that the two blocks are substitutes."

This is textbook:
- **Covert, Lundberg and Lee (2020):** "two perfectly correlated features with significant predictive power would both be deemed unimportant by a feature ablation study."
- **Hooker, Mentch and Zhou (2021):** leave-out measures "reduce the importance of x1 and x2 when these become more correlated ... x1 being able to account for some of the signal coming from x2."
- **Verdinelli and Wasserman (2024):** Shapley values "do not eliminate feature correlation."

The one-sided sentence cannot answer that objection, and the table printed in the same subsection supplies it. Two further flaws:
- **Mixed denominators.** "20-23%" is a share of the total, HAR included, while "4-11% of the gain" is the gain over HAR. The like-for-like attribution is 45-48% of the exogenous total (`\rhfrcBeyondShareExogMin/Max`).
- **A weak low end.** The "4" is the forest, whose six-group removal is not significant (p 0.096) and whose RM twin is pre-patch (Q-29).

**4. The calendar sentence survives every check.**
- **Share against gain.** 1.4-2.3% of the attribution (`\rhfipFomcBucketMin/Max`) against 65-68% of the gain added alone (`\rhabTreeFomcGainShareMin/Max`) and 60-63% unique.
- **The mechanism is documented.** The calendar moves the forecast on few bars: 42-45% of the boosted trees' exogenous attribution on statement days, against about 2.5% on other days.
- **Literature.** Rare-effect regressors are understated by averaged importance (Lundberg et al. 2020, the paper's TreeSHAP citation; Christensen et al. 2023, JFEc: the earnings-announcement dummy).

**5. The D18 result is already stated.** The bullet's preceding sentence gives it: the other 36 series give the linear models nothing and the boosted trees a small gain, from a few series. An attribution share adds to it only if stated symmetrically.
