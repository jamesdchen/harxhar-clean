## Answer

**Yes, narrow it, but to the FOMC calendar, not to "that pair".** Also drop the realized-moments "converse" clause.

- **Why not "the pair".** On the add-alone gains, the calendar ranks out of order against every group with a larger share: the realized moments, the liquidity measures and both constituent-market groups (and implied volatility in the forest). It does so in every tree, not only against the realized moments. A sentence saying the order is reversed "only" for FOMC and the realized moments would be the next claim a referee checks and finds incomplete. "Attribution understates the FOMC calendar" is exact.
- **Why drop "converse".** The realized moments are first in exogenous attribution and their removal is second-largest (DM 7.1 and 7.2), so they are not a converse case. The word "only" in front of the second-largest removal misreads the table. Dropping the clause also removes the forest's pre-patch removal (`\rhabRfLoRmAllPct`, against `full_pre`) from this bullet, which disposes of Q-29 here.

**3. Literature, in papers the paper already cites.**
- **Lundberg et al. (2020):** "SHAP summary plots avoid conflating the magnitude and prevalence of an effect into a single number, and so reveal rare high magnitude effects" (treeshap.txt l.350-352). They say this of the mean-|SHAP| bar chart that the shares are.
- **Christensen, Siggaard and Veliyev (2023, JFEc), the closest RV-forecasting precedent:** their earnings-announcement dummy gets a low importance score, "a weakness with the ALE analysis, since it is mainly a tool for finding the drivers behind the average prediction. Earnings information is released infrequently, but it is arguably very important" (WP 2021-03, p. 19; check the page in the published version).
- **Covert, Lundberg and Lee (2020)** class mean-|SHAP| as a heuristic that does not measure predictive power (Table 1; l.434-435). The paper's own l.152-153 and A_fi:83 say the same: "A share measures how much a series moves the forecast, not how much it improves it". So "wrong way round" also presumes a right order that the two estimands were never meant to share.
