## Evidence

**1. Only the every-bar ladder measures shares of Table 1's gain.**
- **Every bar.** $L_0$ and $L_{46}$ are Table 1's walks: `ladall_scores.csv` reproduces `table1_w3000/metrics.csv` to 1e-12 (scorer checks, `ladall_checks.json` pass).
- **Monthly.** $L_0$ and $L_{46}$ are monthly refits: the full sets are 1.0-3.3% worse in QLIKE (`\rhldLgbmPctMonthlyVsWalk` 1.0 ... `\rhldLassoPctMonthlyVsWalk` 3.3).
- **The linear gains are cut by about a third or more.** QLIKE gain ×10³, monthly vs every bar: ridge 6.86 vs 11.43, lasso 11.36 vs 16.97, elastic net 9.95 vs 12.67 (`readings.csv`; `\rhldEB<M>BarGainFull`).
- **What a referee asks of the monthly ladder.** Its "188%" is a share of a gain that is not Table 1's. He would ask which gain the paper means.

**2. The linear "concentrated" verdict is a property of the monthly refits.** Holding a fit for 21 sessions costs the 10-series linear rung 0.2% QLIKE but the full linear set 1.6-3.3%:

| Rung | Ridge | Lasso | Elastic net |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top 10 (`\rhldEB<M>BarPctMonthlyTen`) | 0.2 | 0.2 | 0.2 |
| Full set (`\rhldEB<M>BarPctMonthlyFull`) | 2.6 | 3.3 | 1.6 |

For the trees the cost is even: 1.1-1.2% for the top 10 against 1.0-1.4% for the full set. So the monthly ladder penalises exactly the rung that defines the linear denominator, and that is why:

| Linear models | Monthly | Every bar |
|---|---|---|
| Top-10 share $R_{10}$ (%) | 130-188 | 104-115 |
| Top-5 share $R_5$ (%) | 99-145 | 78-87 |
| k90 (series for 90% of the gain) | 5 | 10 |
| Rule's reading | CONCENTRATED | INTERMEDIATE |

The paper already concedes the mechanism (`A_fi.tex:399-402`, "Part of the full set's shortfall is the cost of holding a fit on 564 predictors for a month"). The coordinator flagged it before the run: transcript bd4224e0 2026-10-29T00:02:27Z, "'the linear models do best with 10 series, beating the full set' may partly reflect the monthly refits rather than the series". A referee who knows how the refit schedule interacts with high-dimensional penalized fits would raise this first. Under a monthly primary the paper's own appendix answers him against itself.

**3. The deviation weakens the paper's claim, which defuses the forking-paths objection.**
- **Design fixed before its own results.** Addendum K was committed at 1152482 (28 Oct 17:27 PDT), "before any job it affects". It reuses the pre-registered rungs, the primary ranking (`rank_primary.csv`, git blob 53d057da), the scoring conventions and the reading of ladder RULE §6 (addendum K (a), (h)).
- **One change only.** The refit schedule moved to Table 1's, which the pre-registered RULE itself listed as a caveat to state (ladder RULE §8: "monthly refits held for 21 sessions (not the every-bar walks of Table 1)").
- **Direction.** The every-bar result is less favourable to the headline (linear CONCENTRATED → INTERMEDIATE; the "top 10 beat the full set" significance gone in 2 of 3). Choosing it cannot be read as specification search for a better result.
- **Precedent.** Pre-analysis-plan practice (e.g. Olken 2015, *JEP* 29(3)) asks for the pre-specified analysis to be reported and any departure labelled and justified. The answer does both.

**4. Panel B is inconsistent within its own table if left monthly.** Panel A of `tab:rh_channels_sparse` is Table 1's every-bar walks. On ordinary days the monthly Panel B reads as follows:
