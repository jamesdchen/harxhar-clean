**Literature.** GiannoneLenzaPrimiceri2021 (the bib key; the abstract on econometricsociety.org, "the illusion of sparsity"). A JFEc referee steeped in GLP accepts "ten ranked series capture the forecast gain" as a predictive-sufficiency statement. He objects to "the signal is sparse" as a statement about the data-generating process. Hence "the forecast gain sits in" in (d), in place of "the exogenous signal sits in". Correlated proxies, such as the futures' and the constituents' absolute returns, make the ranked subset a sufficient set, not the unique carrier. Section 7's "spread across correlated proxies" reading already covers this.
