\begin{center}{\Large\bf Feature importance of the 15:30 forecast (16:00 bar)}\\[3pt]
{\small MDI, permutation importance on the causal held-out tail, SHAP --- trees and the per-bar linear models; 1,469 forecasts 2018-06-25 .. 2024-04-30, 147 refits}\end{center}

\textbf{Tree settings (read from the refit files).} LightGBM: lgbm\_opt\_00 (392 rounds, 20 leaves, learning rate 0.0130, min child samples 8; the top entry of the 1998-2002 dev-span search); XGBoost: xgb\_opt\_00 (367 rounds, depth 5, learning rate 0.0123, min child weight 4.18; the top entry of the 1998-2002 dev-span search (since 2026-10-07; was the 7th-ranked xgb\_opt\_06)); random forest: trial 255 (485 trees, depth 20, min leaf 1, 90\% of the features at a split, bootstrap of 41\% of the rows; the top entry of the forest's 400-trial 1998-2002 dev-span search (since 2026-10-08; was the library defaults)). Leaf minimums are scaled from the pooled 24,000-row window to the 2,000-row window as in the per-bar spec.

\textbf{Setup.} Models: per-bar ridge and lasso (coefficients re-solved every session, penalty re-chosen causally every 250 sessions) and the untuned per-bar LightGBM, XGBoost and random forest (refit every 10 sessions; settings below). Every fit uses the 2000 sessions before it. A refit's \emph{held-out tail} is the $\le 10$ sessions its model forecasts, up to the next refit: never in its fit, so a loss change measured there is out of sample and causal. Inputs: all features. Library: scikit-learn 1.9.0 (random forest), LightGBM 4.6.0, XGBoost 3.2.0, shap 0.51.0; the permutation and drop-column loops are written out, because a library permutation routine scores one fitted model on one test set, not 147 walk-forward models on their own tails.

\textbf{Measures.} (1) \emph{MDI / gain}: LightGBM gain, XGBoost total gain, random-forest impurity decrease; share per refit, averaged. (2) \emph{Permutation importance} on the tail: a unit's values are replaced with rows drawn from the refit's own training window (past data only; P2) and the change in loss is measured --- QLIKE of the plain back-transform $\hat y^2\times$ diurnal baseline against the bar's realized variance (no recalibration) for the trees, squared error in the fit space for every model (the linear models' QLIKE permutation is not shown: moving an extreme input onto another row can push an unbounded linear forecast through zero); 10 draws per unit and refit, the same draws for all five models. A unit is a column or a \emph{series} (all columns of one input: lags, flags, gates); a series' columns move together. Intervals: 95\% circular-block bootstrap over refits (block 6). (3) \emph{SHAP}: TreeSHAP (path-dependent) on the same tail rows; for the linear models $\beta_j(x_j-\bar x_j)$ with the window mean as background. Linear extras: $|\beta\cdot sd|$ and drop-column importance (re-solve without the unit, penalty held).

\textbf{Key.} Every exogenous input is divided by its time-of-day baseline, and the HAR columns \texttt{har\_ma\_*} are trailing means of the adjusted target, so no name carries a prefix. \texttt{har\_ma\_*} realized variance of the bar (the target's own HAR ladder, means over the last 1, 5, 25, 125, 625, 3125 bars; the per-bar design no longer carries the session-edge interactions \texttt{har\_ma\_*\_x\_open/close}); \texttt{sumabsret\_ma\_*} absolute return; \texttt{sumret\_ma\_*} signed return; \texttt{sumret3/4\_ma\_*} 3rd/4th-power returns; \texttt{sumpret2\_ma\_*} upside squared returns; \texttt{sumbipow\_ma\_*} bipower variation; \texttt{sumautocov\_ma\_*} return autocovariance; \texttt{sumvolume\_ma\_*} ES volume; \texttt{numobs\_ma\_*} ES prints per bar; \texttt{vix/vvix/vix3m\_ma\_*} the Cboe indices; \texttt{fomc\_*} FOMC flags and distances; \texttt{*\_avail/active\_ma\_*} is-present / is-nonzero flags; calendar = \texttt{DOW\_*}, \texttt{is\_*}, \texttt{hour}, \texttt{days\_to\_opex}. all features adds the constituent cross-section (\texttt{*\_ewstock}, \texttt{*\_vwstock}), Cboe volume (\texttt{voldemand\_*}) and StockTwits (\texttt{stocktwits\_*}).

\textbf{Reading.} For the trees, everything below the HAR ladder whose interval excludes zero is together worth 4--11\% of the ladder's permutation importance on QLIKE (1.5--4.4\% on squared error), and the largest single input at most 3.2\%. For the linear models the credit is spread more widely (ridge: 20 series, together 77\% of the ladder's; lasso 24\%): the penalty shrinks the ladder and correlated inputs share its weight. So for the trees the ranking below the leader orders inputs that are small in absolute terms, and the cardinality bias below matters only for that ordering.

\textbf{Cardinality bias.} MDI and TreeSHAP favour many-valued columns; the tables below show how much, and why their rankings below the leader are not a feature list.

\textbf{Reading.} A column of pure noise with a new value on every row is ranked by MDI above 77\%, 74\%, 85\% (LightGBM, XGBoost, forest) of the real all-features columns the model uses; the binary noise column sits near the bottom. The same noise gets a permutation importance of at most 0.09$\times10^{-3}$ in absolute value (3 of 3 intervals include zero), against at least 0.23 for \texttt{har\_ma\_*}. TreeSHAP, which splits the fitted trees, inherits part of the bias. Over the real columns, MDI and SHAP correlate 0.82--0.80 with the number of distinct values, permutation -0.04--0.13. So below the leader, the MDI ranking of this design is partly a ranking by cardinality; the permutation ranking is the one to use for selection. At the series level: \texttt{har\_ma\_*} is the leader of 12 of 12 tree bucket x model x measure rows. The cardinality bias matters for everything below the leader. Series that MDI puts in the top 5 but permutation P2 ranks outside the top 10 (permutation rank in brackets): all features LightGBM: \texttt{sellturnover\_vwstock\_ma\_*} (36); all features XGBoost: \texttt{sellturnover\_vwstock\_ma\_*} (33); all features random forest: none.
