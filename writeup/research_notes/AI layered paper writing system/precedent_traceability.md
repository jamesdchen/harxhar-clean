# Precedents: requirements traceability, assurance cases, MBSE, ADR enforcement ("a top-layer decision propagates down and flags what it affects")

Research date: 2026-10-28. Scope: how industrial traceability and assurance tools record top-level decisions, link them downward, detect impact when one changes, and limit human review to what changed. Each section ends with lessons for the design under consideration: `decisions.yaml` holds the parameters; generators stamp every number with the decisions it was computed under; the build fails on a violation; and after a decision changes, the author reviews only claims whose direction or significance flipped.

Source-quality legend: **[vendor doc]** official product help; **[vendor mkt]** marketing; **[forum]** community Q&A (unofficial); **[paper]** peer-reviewed or arXiv; **[snippet]** only the search-result summary was seen, not the full page; **[UNVERIFIED]** from background knowledge and not confirmed in this session.

## Q1. Requirements-traceability tools (DOORS Next, Jama Connect, Polarion, Codebeamer, Sphinx-needs, Doorstop; OpenReq/ReqIF): how does change propagation work?

### Takeaway
Every commercial tool surveyed uses **flag-for-review, never auto-update**. A change to an upstream item marks the *link* to each downstream item as "suspect", and a human clears it, one link at a time or in bulk. The vendors have spent the last decade cutting noise. IBM moved from timestamp-based suspicion to **content comparison**, and Jama and Codebeamer let admins choose **which fields** trigger suspicion. The docs-as-code tool (Sphinx-needs) instead offers **build-time constraints that warn or fail the build**, the "decision as a check" half of the design. None of the tools flags based on whether the *downstream outcome* changed. They all flag when the *upstream input* changed.

### Cited Findings

**IBM DOORS Next (ELM)**
- IBM introduced "Link Validity" in CLM 6.0.1 (blog dated 2015-11-12) as the successor to "Suspect Links". The old mechanism "relies on modification time, which does not work in a configuration-managed world". Link Validity "compares the actual contents of the artifacts in a relationship", which "prevents insignificant changes from causing a relationship to become suspect" — [Jazz Team Blog, "Link Validity — Coming in CLM version 6.0.1"](https://jazz.net/blog/index.php/2015/11/12/link-validity-coming-in-clm-version-6-0-1/comment-page-1/) [vendor doc/blog]
- Link Validity has three states: **Suspect** (the initial state for new or unknown links), **Valid** and **Invalid**. Old Suspect Links had two states (Suspect / Not Suspect), and new links started as Not Suspect. Team members set Valid or Invalid, and the system records **who set the state, when, and an optional comment**. A "Validity Summary" shows the worst case, and downstream link states do not affect the upstream artifact's summary — [Jazz blog](https://jazz.net/blog/index.php/2015/11/12/link-validity-coming-in-clm-version-6-0-1/comment-page-1/)
- **Validity is reused across configurations.** A Valid/Invalid assertion made in one stream carries over to other streams where the linked artifacts' content is identical. If the content differs, the status is not inherited. Restorations can reuse a prior validity status — [Jazz blog](https://jazz.net/blog/index.php/2015/11/12/link-validity-coming-in-clm-version-6-0-1/comment-page-1/)
- In DOORS Next 7.0, "Suspect Links is replaced with Link Validity" — [New & Noteworthy, DOORS Next 7.0](https://jazz.net/pub/new-noteworthy/erm/7.0/7.0/index.html) [vendor doc; seen via search summary]
- Link Validity works only in projects with configuration management enabled. Other projects keep the older suspect-link behaviour — [Jazz Forum: Suspect Links in DOORS Next Generation](https://jazz.net/forum/questions/281494/suspect-links-in-doors-next-generation); [Jazz Forum: What are suspect links in DNG](https://jazz.net/forum/questions/284539/what-are-suspect-links-in-dng-and-how-can-it-be-fixed) [forum, snippet]
- A community answer says there is no public REST API for link validity, only a private API used by ELM applications — [Jazz Forum](https://jazz.net/forum/questions/281494/suspect-links-in-doors-next-generation) [forum, snippet; unverified against IBM docs]
- Known issue (7.0.2): if you undo a change, previous validity assertions are kept for RM-to-RM links but not for links that involve other applications — [Known issues DOORS Next 7.0.2](https://jazz.net/library/article/96396) [vendor doc, snippet]
- A user asked in the blog comments whether a workflow-state change alone could avoid altering link validity. No answer was shown. This is evidence that users want metadata-only changes to stay silent — [Jazz blog comments](https://jazz.net/blog/index.php/2015/11/12/link-validity-coming-in-clm-version-6-0-1/comment-page-1/)

**Jama Connect**
- Suspect links are flagged on the **downstream** item when an **upstream** item changes: "The change to the requirement causes a suspect relationship to the verification item" — [Jama Connect Help: Clear suspect links](https://help.jamasoftware.com/en/manage-content/coverage-and-traceability/relationships/clear-suspect-links.html) [vendor doc]
- "Only updates to certain fields trigger suspect links." The organization admin chooses those fields when configuring item types — [Jama Help: Clear suspect links](https://help.jamasoftware.com/en/manage-content/coverage-and-traceability/relationships/clear-suspect-links.html)
- Clearing is manual: "you can manually clear the suspect link" when there is no impact or after making the needed changes. **Clear** handles one link and **Clear All** handles all visible items. Suspect links turn the relationship status indicator red — [Jama Help: Clear suspect links](https://help.jamasoftware.com/en/manage-content/coverage-and-traceability/relationships/clear-suspect-links.html)
- The status indicator distinguishes "Suspect" (one or more upstream items changed) from "Causing Suspect" (this item's change flagged its downstream items) — [Jama Help: Relationship Status Indicator](https://help.jamasoftware.com/ah/en/manage-content/coverage-and-traceability/relationships/relationship-status-indicator.html) [vendor doc, snippet]
- The legacy SOAP API (Spring 2015) exposes `clearSuspectLinksForItem` — [Jama SOAP ContourSoapServiceImpl](https://dev.jamasoftware.com/SOAP/spring2015-1/com/jamasoftware/contour/soap/v3/ContourSoapServiceImpl.html) [vendor doc, snippet]
- Jama's traceability is governed by a **Traceability Information Model**, a schema of which item types may or must relate — [Jama: What Is the Traceability Information Model?](https://www.jamasoftware.com/requirements-management-guide/requirements-traceability/traceability-information-model/) [vendor mkt, snippet]
- The help page does **not** say whether suspicion is transitive (multi-level) or whether a version diff is shown when clearing — [Jama Help: Clear suspect links](https://help.jamasoftware.com/en/manage-content/coverage-and-traceability/relationships/clear-suspect-links.html)

**PTC Codebeamer**
- Rationale: "if you have two requirements 'A' and 'B', furthermore 'A depends on B', then every time when B gets a update, it is advisable to review A, as well." Propagation cascades through edits: updating the affected item "may trigger further suspected links, effectively propagating the impact of the original change", and "By the time you clear all suspected links, you will have a consistent set of requirements, again" — [codebeamer.com wiki 84812](https://codebeamer.com/cb/wiki/84812?version=35) [vendor doc, snippet; a direct fetch reached only the login page]
- Field-level trigger control: if a field has the "Omit Suspected when changing" property set, changes to that field do not trigger suspicion — [PTC community: Automate creation of suspected link when updating custom fields](https://community.ptc.com/t5/Codebeamer/Automate-Creation-of-Suspected-Link-when-updating-Custom-Fields/m-p/993012) [forum, snippet]
- "Propagate Suspects" is set **per reference**. A SUSPECTED badge with an up arrow means an upstream reference was modified, and a down arrow means a downstream one was. Clicking it opens a **Merge dialog that lists the fields whose values differ**, with a Changes tab for the referred item and a Derived Item Changes tab for the original. The user can apply (merge) the changes or tick **Clear Suspected** without merging. Fields marked "Omit Merge" are listed under "Fields not merged automatically" — [PTC Codebeamer Help r3.0: Propagate Suspects – Reference](https://support.ptc.com/help/codebeamer/r3.0/en/codebeamer/user_guide/ug_propogate_suspects_reference.html) [vendor doc]
- Computed fields can test suspect state: `hasUpstreamSuspectedLink(TrackerItemDto)` and a downstream counterpart exist in versions 9.4.0+ — [Codebeamer X User Guide: Suspected Reference Functions](https://support.ptc.com/help/codebeamer/r3.2/en/codebeamer/cbx_user_guide/cbx_trackers/cbx_ug_suspectedref_funcs.html) [vendor doc, snippet]
- Users ask how to bulk-edit (mass-clear) suspected flags — [PTC community: Bulk edit suspected flags in codebeamer](https://community.ptc.com/t5/Codebeamer/Bulk-edit-suspected-flags-in-codebeamer/m-p/1005215) [forum, title/snippet only]

**Siemens Polarion**

**Sphinx-needs (docs-as-code)**

### Inferences
- **The universal pattern is "upstream change, then link flagged, then a human clears it with a recorded who/when/why."** No surveyed tool auto-rewrites the downstream item. Codebeamer comes closest with a field Merge dialog, and a human still applies it. The planned design (regenerate numbers automatically, flag only claims) is *more* automated than any of these tools. That is reasonable because the downstream artifacts (numbers) are computed, not authored.
- **Copy IBM's content-based validity, not timestamps.** Each stamp should hash the decision *parameters actually consumed* by a generator (field-level, as in Jama/Codebeamer field whitelists), not the file mtime of `decisions.yaml`. A comment edit or reordering in `decisions.yaml` should flag nothing.
- **Copy the three-state model (Suspect / Valid / Invalid) and the audit record (who, when, comment).** For the paper this means each claim's review status, the decision-hash it was validated under, the reviewer (author vs. agent), and a note.
- **Copy IBM's "reuse validity when content is identical".** If a decision changes but a regenerated number is byte-identical, or its sign and significance class are unchanged, carry the prior clearance forward automatically. That is the planned "show only flips" filter, and IBM's stream-reuse rule is a precedent for it.
- **The proposed design goes past every tool on one axis.** Commercial tools flag on *input* change. The design flags on *output-semantics* change (direction or significance flip). That is closer to an "actual impact" vs. "potential impact" distinction (see Q2) than to suspect links. No surveyed traceability tool does this.
- **Avoid Sphinx-needs' soft-warning trap.** Make decision violations hard errors with a non-zero exit code. A warning channel that CI does not see (issue #344) defeats the purpose.
- **Watch the bulk-clear "Clear All" button.** Bulk clearing exists because users drown in flags. In an AI-maintained paper, an agent that can "Clear All" is a rubber stamp. Restrict which flag classes an agent may clear (e.g., only "number changed, classification unchanged") and reserve flips for the author.

### Gaps
- **ReqIF** (OMG Requirements Interchange Format) and **OpenReq** (EU H2020 project) were not researched this session. Background knowledge [UNVERIFIED]: ReqIF is an XML exchange format for moving requirements between tools and does not itself define suspect-link semantics. OpenReq (c. 2017–2019) built recommender and dependency-detection services for requirements engineering. Neither looks central to the propagation question.
- **Doorstop** [UNVERIFIED, background knowledge]: items carry a `reviewed:` fingerprint. Each child's link stores the parent's fingerprint at link time, and `doorstop` validation warns "suspect link" when the parent's fingerprint changes. `doorstop review` / `doorstop clear` re-stamp them, and `.doorstop.yml` can add extra attributes to the fingerprint. If confirmed, this git-native **hash-stamp-on-link** scheme is the closest structural precedent to "generators stamp each number with the decisions it was computed under". Confirm against https://doorstop.readthedocs.io before citing.
- Whether Jama and Polarion suspicion is **transitive** (A→B→C flags C when A changes) was not documented on the pages read. Codebeamer's wiki suggests propagation happens when the intermediate item is *edited*, not automatically through the whole chain.
- Polarion official help on suspect links was not fetched. The findings rely on training-material snippets, the API reference and the extension portal.
- The current Sphinx-needs docs (2025–2026 versions) were not checked for any newer change-detection or "needs diff" feature. useblocks' commercial tooling was not investigated.

## Q2. Assurance / safety cases (GSN, CAE, ASCE, AdvoCATE, dynamic assurance cases): how do they handle changed evidence invalidating claims?

### Takeaway
Assurance cases have the closest **conceptual** precedent to "claims supported by evidence, flagged when evidence changes". GSN v3 adds **Confidence Arguments** and **Dialectic (defeaters)**. Research tools separate *potential* impact (auto-computed) from *actual* impact (a human judgement). Recent work labels goals **valid / invalid / uncertain** after a change and automates re-generation only for cheap evidence. Industrial tools (ASCE) track evidence changes. The literature says ripple effects are the hard part.

### Cited Findings
- The GSN Community Standard v3 (SCSC-141C) defines three extensions: **Argument Pattern, Confidence Argument, and Dialectic**. Adelard's ASCE 5.1 supports all three using the GSN 3.0 schema — [Adelard: ASCE 5.1 delivers support for GSN v3](https://adelard.com/news/asce-51-delivers-enhanced-functionality-support-for-gsn-v3-and-next-generation-assurance-case-design) [vendor news, snippet]; [SCSC-141C](https://scsc.uk/scsc-141c) [standard landing page, snippet]
- The Confidence Argument extension "allows a separate argument to be made about the confidence in the element or relationship". In GSN 3.0, "a new link type and decorator have been implemented to indicate that a relationship or GSN element have been challenged and/or defeated"; Adelard says "defeater management is used to increase confidence in the assurance case" — [Adelard ASCE 5.1 news](https://adelard.com/news/asce-51-delivers-enhanced-functionality-support-for-gsn-v3-and-next-generation-assurance-case-design) [vendor, snippet]
- GSN v2→v3 changes include deprecating the Model element (replaced by Context) and adding new and changed link types and node properties — [SCSC: GSNv2-to-v3 changes](https://www.scsc.uk/file/gc/GSNv2-to-v3_changes-1092.pdf) [standard body, snippet]
- ASCE "contains a number of tools to support: linking to, management and tracking of changes in the underlying evidence" — [Adelard ASCE product page](https://www.adelard.com/asce/) [vendor, snippet]
- The Assurance 2.0 framing (Bloomfield & Rushby) centres on defeaters and confidence — [Bloomfield, Adelard: Assurance 2.0 slides, SSS Feb 2021](https://www.csl.sri.com/~rushby/slides/bloomfield_adelard_pt908v01c_Assurance2.0_SSS_feb2021.pdf) [slides, title/snippet only]
- **ExplicitCase (fortiss AutoFOCUS3)** runs change impact analysis on GSN cases. It separates **potential impact** (a starting point) from **actual impact**, which "a safety engineer must decide on after reviewing the challenges". **Indirect, ripple-effect impact** is described as the hardest part to capture — [fortiss AF3 help: Assurance case maintenance](https://git.fortiss.org/af3/af3-rcp/-/raw/29e6b519f2e3e7629d58446fa6b4d1a3a7cab28a/org.fortiss.af3.rcp.help/html/assuranceCases/maintenance.html) [tool doc, snippet only; direct fetch failed with connection refused]
- **GAGE (Mälardalen University)** maps the GSN argument onto an AADL architecture model, so a tool can show which parts of the argument a design change affects. The mapping is also a prerequisite for composing modular safety cases — [MDH publication 2687](https://www.es.mdh.se/pdf_publications/2687.pdf) [paper, snippet]
- **Continuous assurance with formal verification** (arXiv 2511.14805, Nov 2025) proposes a change-management phase. Nodes marked as deferred evidence or confidence monitors are entry points when a change occurs. An impact summary labels goals **valid, invalid, or uncertain** using evidence-cost tags and trace links back to RoboChart/PRISM artefacts. Regeneration is partly automated for **low-cost goals and manual for high-impact ones** — [arXiv 2511.14805](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14805) [paper, snippet]
- **ACCESS** (arXiv 2403.15236, Mar 2024) turns a development-time assurance case into a **dynamic runtime** one evaluated against runtime data. It automates change impact analysis from engineering artefacts to the assurance case, with a prototype and an AUV case study — [ACCESS, arXiv 2403.15236](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2403.15236) [paper, snippet]
- On dynamic assurance cases: "the evidence might change over time, potentially invalidating claims that were previously thought to be true" — [arXiv 2411.03657](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03657) [paper, snippet; attribution of this quote to this specific URL is likely but not confirmed]
- **OntoGSN** (arXiv 2506.11023, Jun 2025) proposes ontology-based semantic management of assurance cases, i.e. storing GSN as queryable data — [OntoGSN, arXiv 2506.11023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.11023) [paper, title only]
- Automated assurance-case generation from models — [A Methodology for Automating Assurance Case Generation, arXiv 2003.05388](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.05388) [paper, title only]

### Inferences
- **A paper is an assurance case.** Headline claims are top-level goals, the identification strategy is the argument strategy, tables and figures are evidence, and robustness checks are a confidence argument. GSN v3's **Dialectic/defeater** construct maps onto "referee objections / alternative explanations". Recording these as data would let the build show which claims still have an open defeater.
- **The design's "only show flips" filter matches the potential/actual split.** Potential impact is every claim whose stamps touch the changed decision (computed). Actual impact is claims whose sign or significance flipped (also computed in this design, a step beyond ExplicitCase, where a human decides). Copy the **valid / invalid / uncertain** three-way label from arXiv 2511.14805. "Uncertain" is the right bucket for "no flip, but p moved from 0.01 to 0.09", "magnitude halved" or "CI now straddles a threshold". Otherwise "show only flips" will hide material weakening.
- **Copy cost-tiered regeneration.** Cheap evidence (re-reading a CSV) regenerates automatically. Expensive evidence (HPC re-runs) is marked "stale-pending" rather than silently treated as valid. This matches the arXiv 2511.14805 split.
- **Ripple effects are flagged as the hard part.** The claim graph (number → sentence → paragraph → abstract/intro/conclusion) has to be explicit, or a flipped table entry will not reach the abstract sentence that summarises it.

### Gaps
- **AdvoCATE** (NASA Ames; Denney & Pai): only older descriptions surfaced (argument analysis, evidence selection, claim composition). No current source on GSN v3 support or evidence-change handling was found. Background [UNVERIFIED]: Denney, Pai & Habli's "dynamic safety cases" work (c. 2015) proposed updating safety cases from operational data and monitors.
- **CAE (Claims-Arguments-Evidence)**, Adelard/Bloomfield: not separately researched. Its change-handling is assumed similar to GSN's through ASCE, but not verified.
- No quantitative study was found on how much review effort assurance-case impact analysis saves.
- The ASCE 5.1 release date and the GSN v3 publication date were not confirmed. Background [UNVERIFIED]: GSN Community Standard v3 was published by SCSC around 2021.

## Q3. MBSE and the digital thread (SysML parameters, Capella): parameters defined once and used everywhere

### Takeaway
MBSE practice treats parameters as living in **one authoritative store** (e.g., Teamcenter) and synchronising into models (e.g., Capella PropertyValueGroups). "Single source of truth" is mostly a vendor claim. The sources found are integration and sync stories, not evidence of downstream consumers being re-validated automatically. It is a precedent for `decisions.yaml` as the single parameter store, and a weak one for the propagation-and-flag half.

### Cited Findings
- In a Capella–Teamcenter (System Modeling Workbench) setup, the reply to a user was that "parameters are managed by Teamcenter and Teamcenter is the source of truth". Parameters are "synchronized within the Capella models as plain PropertyValueGroups, no need of PVMT", and there was "no integration yet between PVMT and PropertyValueGroups used for Teamcenter Parameters" — [Capella forum: How do Capella and Teamcenter interact?](https://forum.mbse-capella.org/t/system-modeling-workbench-how-do-capella-and-teamcenter-interact/6340) [forum, snippet]
- An Eclipse/Capella webinar (2025-04-03) describes a **Synchronized Source of Truth (SSoT)** hub that stores assets centrally, with bidirectional requirements synchronisation and SysML↔ARCADIA conversion — [Eclipse newsroom: Unifying Digital Threads](https://newsroom.eclipse.org/node/41860); [Capella forum announcement](https://forum.mbse-capella.org/t/webinar-april-3rd-2025-unifying-digital-threads-capella-and-interoperability-to-support-a-synchronized-source-of-truth/10509) [vendor/community, snippet]
- Obeo's SysML Bridge maps Capella custom property values to SysML tagged values, and its default rules are bidirectional — [Obeo: SysML Bridge for Capella](https://www.obeosoft.com/en/products/sysml-bridge-for-capella) [vendor mkt, snippet]
- Siemens says SysML v2 APIs and a common standardised data set "ensure a single source of truth" for the digital thread — [Siemens Teamcenter blog: SysML v2 guide](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/teamcenter/sysml-v2-guide/) [vendor mkt, snippet; marketing claim, not evaluated]

### Inferences
- The MBSE lesson for `decisions.yaml` is structural: **one owner per parameter**, with every consumer reading it by reference and never copying it. In the paper, no sample-span string, benchmark name or date range should ever be typed into prose. All of them come in through macros generated from `decisions.yaml`. That is the paper equivalent of "parameters synchronised as PropertyValueGroups from Teamcenter".
- MBSE tools' synchronisation story ("bidirectional sync") is a warning sign for the paper. Bidirectional sync between prose and data invites drift. Keep the flow **one-way**: decisions → generated numbers → macros → prose.

### Gaps
- SysML v2 specification-level mechanisms were not verified this session. Background [UNVERIFIED]: SysML v2 has attribute/value bindings, `constraint def`, and `requirement def` elements whose constraints can be evaluated against model attribute values, and OMG formally adopted SysML v2 around 2025. A requirement in SysML v2 can in principle be checked mechanically against parameter values, the "build fails on violation" analogue. This needs confirmation from the OMG spec.
- No source was found on MBSE tools flagging downstream analyses as stale when a parameter changes, beyond vendor "digital thread" claims. Not found ≠ not existing. Teamcenter, Cameo/Magic and Valispace-type tools may have this, but it was not researched.

## Q4. Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) and enforcement (fitness functions, ArchUnit): turning a recorded decision into a check

### Takeaway
Software architecture practice has a well-trodden pattern: **record the decision in an ADR (prose rationale) and enforce it with an executable "fitness function" (e.g., an ArchUnit test) that fails CI on violation**. The two are linked by convention, not by tooling. Practitioners warn against over-constraining. This is the closest precedent for "the build fails when a number or comparison violates the governing decision".

### Cited Findings
- ArchUnit is a library for "automated tests that enforce architecture decisions". Each rule is the test form of a decision, and a decision is something that affects source code and that a team chooses to enforce — [Publicis Sapient France (Medium): Enforcing architecture decisions with ArchUnit](https://medium.com/publicis-sapient-france/enforcing-architecture-decisions-with-archunit-4d8b9f61cf4a) [practitioner blog, snippet]
- ArchUnit rules "can be executed by any unit testing framework, with a convenient integration into JUnit". They are commonly used for allowed and disallowed dependencies between layers or modules — [InfoQ: Fitness functions architecture](https://www.infoq.com/articles/fitness-functions-architecture/) [trade press, snippet]
- "Documentation alone doesn't prevent drift" is the argument for operationalising ADRs as automated fitness functions — [DEV: Stop Architecture Drift — Operationalizing ADRs with Automated Fitness Functions](https://dev.to/alexandreamadocastro/stop-architecture-drift-operationalizing-adrs-with-automated-fitness-functions-22oi) [practitioner blog, snippet]
- Practitioners recommend documenting each fitness function in an ADR that records the architectural goal it protects, and warn that **over-constraining with too many fitness functions can make a system harder to change** — [Architectural Decision Records (ADRs), techdebt.pages.dev](https://techdebt.pages.dev/architectural-decisions/) and related search results [practitioner, snippet; the exact source of the over-constraining warning among the results was not pinned down]
- Equivalent tools exist across ecosystems: ArchUnitNET and NetArchTest (.NET), ArchUnitTS (TypeScript), dependency-cruiser (JS) — [Milan Jovanović: Architecture fitness functions in .NET with ArchUnitNET](https://milanjovanovic.tech/blog/architecture-fitness-functions-archunitnet) [practitioner, snippet]

### Inferences
- `decisions.yaml` plus build-time checks is **ADR + fitness function with the link made explicit in data**. In common ADR practice the ADR is prose and the test is code, linked only by a comment. The design improves on that by having the check *read its parameters from the decision record*, so the two cannot drift.
- Copy the **ADR fields that the YAML currently lacks**: status (proposed/accepted/superseded), date, the decision it supersedes, and a short rationale. "Superseded-by" chains give the author a history of why the sample span changed, which a referee response often needs.
- **Over-constraining warning:** encode only decisions whose violation would be an *error in the paper* (wrong sample, wrong benchmark, comparison against the wrong baseline). Do not encode style preferences as hard failures, or the build becomes a tax on every edit.

### Gaps
- Primary sources were not fetched this session. Background [UNVERIFIED]: Michael Nygard's 2011 blog post "Documenting Architecture Decisions" introduced the ADR format. "Fitness functions" come from Ford, Parsons & Kua, *Building Evolutionary Architectures* (O'Reilly, 2017; 2nd ed. 2022). ThoughtWorks Technology Radar has listed lightweight ADRs as "Adopt". Confirm before citing.
- No empirical study was found measuring whether ADR + fitness-function enforcement reduces architectural drift or review effort.

## Q5. Known problems (suspect-link fatigue, over-linking, review burden) and evidence on effort saved or cost

### Takeaway
There is solid evidence that **traceability helps** maintainers: controlled experiments show faster, more correct changes. There is equally solid evidence that **hand-maintained links erode**, because practitioners do not update them. I found **no study quantifying suspect-link false-positive rates or "suspect-link fatigue"** directly. The evidence for fatigue is indirect: vendors keep adding noise filters (content comparison, field whitelists, bulk clear) and users keep asking for them.

### Cited Findings
**Benefit studies**
- Mäder & Egyed, "Do developers benefit from requirements traceability when evolving and maintaining a software system?", *Empirical Software Engineering* 20(2):413–441, 2015. Controlled experiment, 71 subjects, real maintenance tasks on two third-party systems: traceability made tasks **~24% faster** on average and gave **~50% more correct solutions** (correct rate ~50% without vs. ~74% with) — [itemis blog summary](https://blogs.itemis.com/en/benefits-from-requirements-traceability-when-maintaining-software-systems); [itemis article](https://www.itemis.com/en/blog/compliance-intelligence/requirements-traceability/requirements-traceability-maintenance/); [dblp record](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/ese/MaderE15.html); [TU Ilmenau report citing the figures](https://www.db-thueringen.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/dbt_derivate_00047951/2192-5283_5_2015_4_85-86.pdf) [paper via secondary summaries; full text not read]
- The earlier ICSM 2012 version reported **21% faster and 60% more correct** — [manuscript.isc.ac record](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/49/588591.htm) [bibliographic record, snippet]. The 2012 and 2015 figures differ, so cite the 2015 journal version.
- Jaber, Sharif & Liu (2013, IEEE): an experiment with 28 subjects found task accuracy of **86.06%** when traceability links were provided. They note that the literature "often states that traceability is useful but expensive to build and maintain" — [manuscript.isc.ac record](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/49/61382.htm) [bibliographic record, snippet]

**Cost / erosion studies**
- Cleland-Huang, Chang & Christensen, "Event-based traceability for managing evolutionary change", *IEEE TSE* 29(9):796–810, 2003. "The traceability infrastructure underlying a software system tends to erode over its lifetime", because busy practitioners do not consistently update links and impacted artifacts, even with automated tools. The proposal links artifacts loosely through an **event service** that notifies dependents of change events — [UCI ISR page](https://isr.uci.edu/content/event-based-traceability-managing-evolutionary-change.html) [abstract, snippet]
- Mäder, Riebisch & Philippow describe event-based approaches as automatically generating "a message with information about the type of change" when a change event occurs — [Uni Hamburg PDF (2006, German)](https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/en/inst/ab/swk/research/publications/pdf/2006-paper-wsr06paper_maeder.pdf) [paper, snippet]
- Maro, Steghöfer et al., "Traceability Maintenance: Factors and Guidelines" (ASE 2016): interviews with 24 stakeholders across 15 industrial cases. "Establishing traceability links is a challenging and cost-intensive task, which is uneconomical without suitable strategies for maintaining high link quality" — [Chalmers research record](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/239302) [paper record, snippet]
- A multiple-case study in *Requirements Engineering* (2018/2020) of 24 practitioners in 15 industrial projects examined how development-effort characteristics shape traceability management — [Springer RE journal PDF, s00766-018-0306-1](https://rd.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00766-018-0306-1.pdf) [paper, snippet]
- Fransson & Jonasson (2019, Gothenburg): six of eight open-source subjects contained explicit links, but these were "for the most part not updated apart from a few exceptions", and teams did not share responsibility for updating them — [GUPEA: Analyzing the usage of traceability links within OSS projects](https://gupea.ub.gu.se/handle/2077/62533) [thesis, snippet]
- A licentiate thesis on embedded systems reports that the manual work of establishing traceability is high and that ROI is hard to measure — [GUPEA 2077/52196](https://gupea.ub.gu.se/handle/2077/52196?show=full) [thesis, snippet]

**Indirect evidence of suspect-link noise / fatigue**
- IBM replaced timestamp-based suspicion with content comparison explicitly to stop "insignificant changes from causing a relationship to become suspect" — [Jazz blog 2015](https://jazz.net/blog/index.php/2015/11/12/link-validity-coming-in-clm-version-6-0-1/comment-page-1/)
- Jama limits triggers to admin-selected fields — [Jama Help](https://help.jamasoftware.com/en/manage-content/coverage-and-traceability/relationships/clear-suspect-links.html). Users ask to suppress suspicion from irrelevant Polarion fields — [PTC community](https://community.ptc.com/t5/Systems-Software-Engineering/Edit-attributes-for-suspecting-regarding-the-PTC-Modeler/td-p/884940) — and to bulk-edit suspected flags — [PTC community](https://community.ptc.com/t5/Codebeamer/Bulk-edit-suspected-flags-in-codebeamer/m-p/1005215)

Impact analysis (what *could* be affected) and suspect flags (what *was* touched) are separate features in some tools — [Jazz Forum: suspect link in impact analysis diagram](https://jazz.net/forum/questions/145075/suspect-link-in-impact-analysis-diagram-design-manager) [forum, snippet]

### Inferences
- **The two failure modes are erosion (links not maintained) and fatigue (too many flags, rubber-stamped).** The design attacks erosion directly: stamps are *generated* by the code that computes the number, so they cannot fall out of date the way hand-maintained links do (Cleland-Huang; Fransson & Jonasson). It attacks fatigue by filtering to semantic flips, a stronger filter than any vendor's field whitelist.
- **The residual fatigue risk moves to the agent layer.** If AI agents rewrite prose after every regeneration, the author faces a prose diff even when no claim flipped. The "show only flips" view should be paired with a rule that **unflipped claims keep their prose unchanged** (numbers update via macros, words do not), or the review surface grows back.
- **Silent-weakening risk.** Mäder & Egyed's gain comes from people *seeing* the relevant links. A flip-only filter hides non-flip changes that may still matter (effect size halves, a robustness check goes from 9/10 to 6/10 significant). Add an "uncertain" tier (Q2), cheap to skim, between "unchanged" and "flipped".
- **No one has measured suspect-link clearing effort**, so the design cannot borrow an effort estimate. The author could log flags raised, flags cleared and time per decision change, which would itself be novel evidence.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that measures **suspect-link false-positive rates**, **time spent clearing suspect links**, or **rubber-stamp rates** in DOORS, Jama, Polarion or Codebeamer deployments. Vendor case studies with ROI claims probably exist but were not retrieved, and would be marketing in any case.
- "Over-linking" (too many trace links, which raises flag volume) as a named, studied phenomenon was not found in this session.
- Within this scope, no system was found that **(a)** stamps computed numbers with the governing decision parameters, **(b)** fails the build on decision violations, **and (c)** filters review to downstream *outcome* flips. Pieces exist separately: (a) Doorstop-style hash stamps [UNVERIFIED] and IBM content-based validity; (b) Sphinx-needs constraints and ArchUnit fitness functions; (c) the valid/invalid/uncertain labelling in recent assurance-case research. Precedents from reproducible-research tooling (e.g., literate-programming or "results traceability" systems) are covered by the adjacent note `results_traceability.md` and were not re-searched here.
