QUESTION
Section 8 (writeup/rehaul/08_feature_importance.tex) asks whether the exogenous signal is "dense and weak" (many series
each adding a little) or concentrated (a few series carry it).  Following Maisch, Zhang, Ulrich and Zimmer (SSRN
7269778, Table 5: retrain on the top 69 / 20 / 10 / 5 / 3 / 1 features; their SHAP ranking comes from the FIRST
TRAINING WINDOW only, to avoid look-ahead, their footnote 4), retrain every model on the top-k exogenous series only and
see how the out-of-sample loss depends on k.  If a few series carry the signal, a small k keeps most of the full set's
gain over the HAR benchmark; if the signal is dense and weak, the loss falls steadily as k grows.  The group ablations
(leave-one-group-out / add-one-group, results/ablations_2014start_w3000 on -ablations) are a different question and are
not redone here.

2. ORIGINS AND ESTIMATION (as the tree SHAP refits)
- The 126 monthly origins of fi_by_window_2014start (results/tree_shap_w3000_2014start_2026-10-23/inputs/fiw_origins.csv,
  sha256 checked): the 10:00 bar of every 21st session from 2014-01-02 through 2024-04-05; design row r_k = 39,000 +
  oos_row_k.  Block N_k = design rows [r_k, r_{k+1}) (r_127 = end of data); the blocks partition the 33,970 forecast rows
  2014-01-02 10:00 .. 2024-04-30 16:00.
- Trees: at origin k, for each rung, one fit on design rows [r_k - 39,000, r_k) restricted to the rung's columns (the
  every-bar walk's refit at r_k); its forecasts of every row of N_k (held for the block).
- Linear: the walk's own state at r_k on the rung's columns: the walk replayed from the last 250-solve tune boundary
  g = (i // 250) x 250 <= i = r_k - 39,000 through i (fiw_replay.py "replay"; a cold seed at g, the warm updates and
  any KKT / mask reseeds through i), then that state's predict_one on every row of N_k (held for the block).  Why the
  replay and not a cold fit at r_k: the cold fit differs from the walk's state by up to 3.8 / 9.4 / 6.9 % (ridge /
  lasso / elastic net, fi_by_window run_info.json), the replay reproduces the walk exactly.
- Forecasts are pred_adj (the adjusted scale); plain = pred_adj^2 x B with B of the bar (the linear template
  w3000/forecasts.parquet, equal to the trees' B).

3. RANKINGS (the order in which series enter; each model its own)
- PRIMARY (no look-ahead; the analogue of Maisch et al.'s first-training-window SHAP ranking): ONE pre-sample fit of
  the full set at the origin 2013-01-02 10:00 (design row r_0; window [r_0 - 39,000, r_0)), explained on the 2013
  rows only (design rows [r_0, 50,777): every bar dated 2013-01-02 .. 2013-12-31, the year of the penalties' validation
  fold), fixed for all 126 origins.
    trees: I_s = mean over the 2013 rows of |sum over the series' 12 columns of the TreeSHAP value| (path-dependent
      TreeSHAP of that fit: LightGBM pred_contrib, XGBoost pred_contribs, the forest shap.TreeExplainer on the
      sklearnex estimators_ with input_dtype float64, as code/shap_run.py of the SHAP study); additivity checked
      (1e-9; XGBoost 1e-5 x max(1, |f|)).
    linear: I_s = sum over the series' 12 columns of |beta_j| x sd_j, beta_j the coefficient of the walk state's forecast
      function on the design column j (beta_j = f(e_j) - f(0) of its predict_one, f affine), sd_j the s.d. (ddof 0) of
      column j over the 2013 rows.  The linear fit at r_0 is the walk replayed from g_0 = (i_0 // 250) x 250 through
      i_0 = r_0 - 39,000 (as every rung).
- SECONDARY (LABELLED LOOK-AHEAD, descriptive only): the full-span pooled ranking -- trees: mean |SHAP| of
  results/tree_shap_w3000_2014start_2026-10-23/m1_variables.csv (all 33,970 forecast rows, each model its own);
  linear: the paper's W = 3,000 s.d.-weighted exogenous shares (same directory, m2_linear_shares.csv share_exog, each
  model its own).  These rankings use the evaluation span, so the secondary ladder is NOT an out-of-sample test of the
  selection; it bounds what a ranking that knew the span could do.
- Ties (e.g. series constant in the ranking window, I_s = 0) are broken by design order (the series' first column).
  Rung k = the top k series of the ranking.  A rung whose series set equals another ranking's rung of the same model
  is fitted once and reported under both.

5. SCORING (local; the conventions of Table 1, writeup/make_rehaul_oos_rerun.py / experiments/ablate_score.py)
- Bars: Table 1's 33,527 scored bars (the 2,579 evaluation days 2014-01-02 .. 2024-04-30: NYSE days with a positive
  16:00 print, early closes dropped; bars with RV > 0; asserted equal to Table 1's count, and every rung must have a
  finite positive forecast on all of them).  RV = data/core_stats.parquet sumret2.
- Loss: QLIKE = r - ln r - 1, r = RV / plain (primary); squared error recorded.
- Spans: "all" = every scored bar; "c16" = the 16:00 bar of the 2,579 days; "c16_2020" = the 16:00 bar on 2020-01-03 ..
  2024-04-30 (Table 1's 16:00 column; secondary).
- Tests: Diebold-Mariano (src/evaluation/diebold_mariano.dm_test: HLN correction, Bartlett, Newey-West lag max(h - 1,
  auto) = 8 on 2,579 days) on the daily average loss ("all") or the per-day 16:00 loss ("c16"); every rung vs the same
  model's k = 46 and vs its k = 0 (same ranking; k = 46 and k = 0 do not depend on the ranking).  Negative = the rung
  has the lower loss.
- Retained gain: R_k = (L_0 - L_k) / (L_0 - L_46) x 100 % (QLIKE), reported when L_0 - L_46 > 0; > 100 % = the rung
  beats the full set.
- Reference rows (context only): the every-bar walks of Table 1 (results/oos_final_2014start/table1_w3000/metrics.csv:
  each model's full set and HAR benchmark), to show what the monthly refit costs against the every-bar refit.
